§. VII. *THE HALF OF THE TRIBE OF Manasseh* *MANASSE**.*

## p02143 · sig. 229b

†. I. *Of the bounds of this half Tribe: and of Scythopolis,* *Salem, Tirzah* *Thersa**, and others.*

## p02144 · sig. 229b

The next Tribe which joins it self to Issachar *Isachar* towards the South, is the half of Manasseh, on the West side of *Jordan. Manasseh *was the first begotten of Joseph *Ioseph*, the eleventh son of Jacob *Iacob*. His mother was an Egyptian *Aegyptian*, the daughter of Putiphar, Priest and Prince of Heliopolis: which Manasseh with his brother Ephraim, the grand-children of Jacob, were by adoption numbered amongst the sons of Jacob, and made up the number of the twelve Patriarchs *Patriarkes*.

## p02145 · sig. 229b

Of Manasseh there were increased in Egypt *Aegypt*, as they were numbered at Mount 〈◊ 〉, 32200. able men: all which being consumed in the Deserts *Desarts*, there entered of their issues, 52700. bearing arms. The Territory which fell on this one half of Manasseh, was bounded by Jordan on the East, and Dor *Doraea* upon the Mediterranean *Mediterran* Sea on the West, Jezreel *Iesrael* on the North, and Michmethath *Machmata* is the South border.

## p02146 · sig. 229b

The first and principal City which stood in this Territory was Beth-shean *Bethsan*, sometime Nysa, says Pliny *Plinie*, built by Liber Pater, in honour of his Nurse there buried, of the same name; which Solinus confirms. Afterward when the 〈◊ 〉 invaded Asia the less, and pierced into the South, to the uttermost of Coelesyria, they built this City a-new, and very magnificent: and it had thereupon the name of Scythopolis, or the City of Scythians given it by the Greeks *Greekes*.

## p02147 · sig. 229b

These barbarous Northren people constrained the Jews *Iewes* to fight against their own Nation and kindred, by whose hands when they had obtained victory, they themselves set on the Jews which served them, and slew them all. Stephanus makes it the utmost towards the South of Coelesyria: and Strabo joins it to Galilee. It is seated between Jordan and the Hills of *Gilboe, in aulone ad montes acrabitenae* ("Stella, Laicstan, Adrichome")*, *says Ziegler. But I find it in the East part of the Valley of Jezreel near Jordan: after that, Jordan straitens it self again into a River: leaving the Sea or Lake Genezareth. Notwithstanding, Montanus describes it far to the West, and 〈◊ 〉 the Mediterranean Sea, near Endor, contrary to *Stella, Laicstan, Adrichome* ("and he passed over into Salem, a city of the Shechemites"), and all other the best Authors. This City was the greatest of all those of Decapolis: but the children of Manasseh could not expel the Inhabitants thereof: and therefore called it Sane an enemy, or Beth-san, the house of an enemy.

## p02148 · sig. 229b

Over the walls of this Beth-shean the Philistines *Philistims* hung the body of Saul, and his sons, slain at Gilboe. It had, while the Christian Religion flourished in those parts, an Archbishop, who had nine other Bishops of his Diocesse, numbered by Tyrius, in his 14. Book and 12. Chapter: but the same was afterward translated to Nazareth. The later travellers in those parts affirm, that there is daily taken out among the rubble and the ruins of that City, goodly pillers and other pieces of excellent marble, which witness the stately buildings, and magnificence which it had in elder times, but it is now a poor and desolate Village.

## p02149 · sig. 229b

From Beth-shean keeping the way by Jordan, they find an ancient City called 〈◊ 〉, which City the ancient Rabbins *Rabbines*, says Jerome *Hierome*, do not find to be the same with Jerusalem *Hierusalem*: there being in the time of Jerome and since, a town of that name, near Scythopolis before remembered, which if the place of Scripture Gen. 13. 18. do not confirm, where the Vulgar reads *transivitque in Salem urbem Sichemorum* ("he came in safety to the city of Shechem")( for which others read, *venit incolumis ad Civitatem* *Ciuitatem** Sechemum* ("To correct this inconvenience of the places, [he drew] a circle [round them];"), making the word 〈◊ 〉 not to be a proper name, but an adjective) yet the place John *Iohn* 3. 13. where it is said, that John was baptizing in Aenon near Salem, may somewhat strengthen this opinion, and yet it is not unlikely that this Salem of which S. John speaks, is but contracted of Shahalim, of which in the Tribe of 〈◊ 〉, 1. Sam.. 9. 4. This word 〈◊ 〉 makes to be the plural of Shual *Shuhal*: of which we read, 1. Sam.. 13. 17. for as for that which is added out of Cant. 6. 12. of Shulammite *Shulammitis*, as if it had been as much as a Woman of this Salem *Saleim*, near Aenon, it has no probability.

## p02150 · sig. 230a

Not far from thence where they place Salem, they find Bezech the City of *Adoni-bezek* *Adonibezet**, Josephus* *Iosephus** *calls it Bezek *Bala*, here it was that Saul assembled the strength of Israel, and Judah *Iuda*, to the number of 330000. when he meant to relieve Jabesh *Iabesh* Gilead, against Nahash *Naash* the Ammonite: who would give them no other conditions of peace, than to suffer their right eyes to be thrust out. Near Bezech is the City of Beth-barah *Bethbera* or rather Beth-bara, of which Judg *Iudg*. 7. 24. in the story of Gideon: and then Ephra or Hophra, wherein Gideon *Gedeon* inhabited: in the border whereof stood an Altar consecrated to Baal: which he pulled down and defaced: and near it that stone, on which 〈◊ 〉 the Bastard slew his 70. brothers: a Heathenish cruelty, practised by the Turk *Turke* to this day; and not 〈◊ 〉 hence, between the Village of Asophon and *Jordan, Ptolomaeus* Lathyrus *Lathurus* overthrew Alexander King of the Jews: and slaughtered as 〈◊ 〉 numbers them 3000. but according to Timagenes 50000. after which victory, as Ptolemy *Ptolomie* past by the Villages of the Jews; he slew all their women: and caused the young children to be sod in great cauldrons, that the rest of the Jews might thereby think that the Egyptians *Aegyptians* were grown to be man-eaters, and strike them with the greater terror.

## p02151 · sig. 230a

Towards the West and on the border of Issachar, they place the Cities of * 〈◊ 〉 of the Levites *Leuites*, and Abel-Mehola, which *Junius* *Iunius**, Judg*. 7. 22. places in Ephraim, it was the habitation of 〈◊ 〉 the Prophet, numbered among those places, 1. Reg. 4. 12. which were 〈◊ 〉 in charge to Baana by Solomon *Salomon*, to whose charge also 〈◊ 〉 belonged, a place of great strength, which at the first resisted Joshua *Iosua*, though their King was afterward hanged, and their City given to the Levites.

## p02152 · sig. 230a

In the body of this Territory of Manasseh, but somewhat nearer Jordan, than to the Mediterranean Sea, were three great Cities, to wit, Tirzah, whose King was one of those that Joshua slew: which the Kings of Israel used for their Regal seat: till such time as Samaria was built. From hence the wise of Jeroboam *Ieroboam* went to Ahijah *Achia* to enquire of her sons health: who knowing her, though she were disguised, told her of her sons death.

## p02153 · sig. 230a

The second was Thebes near Samaria, of which name there are both in Egypt, and Greece, of great fame: in the assault of the Tower of this Town, into which the Citizens retired, the Bastard Abimilec was wounded by a weighty stone, thrown by a Woman over the Wall, who despairing of his recovery, commanded his Page to slay him out-right, because it should not be said that he perished by the stroke of a Woman. But others set this City in Ephraim near Shechem *Sichem* or Neapolis.

## p02154 · sig. 230a

The third is Acrabata, of which the Territory adjoining is called Acrabattene *Acrabatena*, ( one of the ten Toparchies or Governments *Gouernments* in Judea *Iudaea*) for which Jerome, 1. Mac. 5. reads Acrabattene: but in the Greek *Greeke* it is *Acrabattene: Isidore *calls it Acrabattene. This City had one of the largest Territories of all Palaestine belonging to the Governor thereof. Josephus remembers it often, as in his second Book of the Jews Wars *Warres*, c. 11. 25. 28. and elsewhere.

## p02155 · sig. 230a

The difference between a tetrarchy and a Toparchy, was, that the first was taken for a Province, and the other for a City with some lesser Territory adjoining, and a Tetrarch is the same with Praeses in Latine, and President in English, being commonly the fourth part of a Kingdom: and thereof So called: Pliny names seventeen Tetrarchies in Syria: the Holy Land had four, and So has the Kingdom of Ireland to this day, *Leinster* *Lemster**, Ulster, Connacht* *Connath*, and Munster *Mounster*.

## p02156 · sig. 230b

To the South-west of Acrabata they place the Cities of Balaam or Bileam *Bilham*, and Gath-rimmon *Gethremmon* of the Levites: but Junius out of Josh.. 21. 25. and 1. Chron. 6. 70. gathers that these two are but one: and that Ibleam *Iibleham* Josh.. 16. 11. is another name of the same City.

## p02157 · sig. 230b

Then is Jezreel a Regal City, set at the foot of the Mountains *Mountaines* of Gilboe, towards the South-west: herein 〈◊ 〉 by a false accusation caused Naboth to be stoned, to the end she might possess his Vineyard adjoining to the City, which Naboth refused to sell, because it was his inheritance from his Father.

## p02158 · sig. 230b

Joram *Ioram* also was cast unburied into the same field: for which his Mother Jezebel murdered Naboth.

## p02159 · sig. 230b

Toward the Sea from Jezreel is the City which they call Gaber: in whose ascent as Ahaziah King of Judah fled from Jehu *Iehu*, when he had slain Joram, he was wounded with the shot of an arrow, of which wound he died at Megiddo *Mageddo* adjoining. The Scripture calls this City of *Gaber, Gur*.

## p02160 · sig. 230b

Then Hadad-rimmon *Adadremmon*, near unto which the good King Josiah *Iosias* was slain by Necho, King of 〈◊ 〉, in a War unadvisedly undertaken. For Necho marched towards Assyria against the King thereof; by the commandment of God: whom Josiah thought to resist in his passage. It was afterward called Maximianopolis.

## p02161 · sig. 230b

A neighbour City to 〈◊ 〉 was Maggeddo, often remembered in the Scriptures; whose King was slain among the rest by Joshua; yet they defended their City for a long time against Manasseh. The River which passes by the Town, may perhaps be the same which Ptolemy calls Chorseus: and not that of which we have spoken in Zebulun *Zabulon*. For because this name is not found in the Scriptures, many of those that have described the Holy Land delineate no such River. Moore only sets it down in his Geography of the twelve Tribes: but the River which passes by Maggeddo he understands to be but a branch, falling into it. Laicstan and Schrot make a great confluence of waters in this place: agreeable to this Scripture in the fifth of *Judges* *Iudges**: Then fought the Kings of Canaan in Taanach* *Tanaac** by the waters of Maggeddo.* But these Authors, and with them Stella, give it no other name then the Torrent So called.

## p02162 · sig. 230b

But seeing that ancient Cosmographers stretch out the bounds of Phoenicia, even to Sebaste or Samaria; and Strabo far beyond it on the Sea-coast: And Josephus calls Caesarea *Caesaria* Palestine *Palaestinae* a City of Phoenicia, yea Laurentius Corvinus *Coruinus* extends Phoenicia as far as Gaza: seeing also Ptolemy sets down Chorseus for the partition of Phoenicia and Judea: this River running East and West parallel with Samaria: it is very probable that this Torrent called Maggeddo, after the name of the City, which it waters, is the same which Ptolemy in his fourth Table of Asia calls Chorseus. The later travellers of the holy Land call Maggeddo Subimbre at this day.

## p02163 · sig. 230b

†. II. *Of Caesarea Palestine, and some other Towns* *Townes**.*

## p02164 · sig. 230b

From Maggeddo toward the West, and near the Mediterranean Sea, was that glorious City of Caesarea Palestine: first, the Tower of Straton: the same which Pliny calls Apollonia: though Ptolemy sets Apollonia elsewhere, and toward Egypt, between this City and Joppa *Ioppe*, to which Vespasian gave the name of Flavia Colonia. It was by Herod rebuilt, who therein laboured to exceed all the works in that part of the World. For besides the edifices, which he reared within the Walls, of cut and polished marbles, the Theater and Amphitheater, from whence he might look over the Seas far away, with the high and stately Towers and Gates: he forced a Harbour *Harborow* of great capacity, being in former times but an open Bay: and the wind blowing from the Sea the Merchants haunting that Port, had no other hope, but in the strength of their cables and Anchors. This work he performed with such charge and labour, as the like of that kind has not been found in any Kingdom, nor in any age: which, because the Materials *Materialls* were fetched from far, and the weight of the stones was such as it exceeds belief, I have added Josephus own words of this work: which are these: *Hanc locorum incommoditatem correcturus, circulum* ("in the districts of Dor") *portus circumduxit, quantum 〈◊ 〉 magnae classi recipiendae sufficere: & in viginti ulnarum profundum, praegrandia saxa demisit: quorum pleraque pedum quinquaginta longitudinis, latitudinis vero octodecim, altitudine novem-pedali: fuerunt quaedam 〈◊ 〉 maiora, minora alia; To mend this inconvenience of place*( says JOSEPHUS) *he compassed in a Bay* *wherein a great fleet might well ride: and let down great stones 〈◊ 〉 fathom deep: whereof some were fifty foot long, eighteen foot broad, and nine foot thick: some bigger, and some lesser. *To this he added an arm or causeway of two hundred foot long, to break the waves: the rest he strengthened with a stone wall, with divers, stately Towers thereon built: of which the most magnificent he called Drusus after the name of Drusus the son in law of Caesar: in whose honour he entitled the City it self, Caesarea of Palaestine: all which he performed in twelve years time. It was the first of the Eastern Cities that received a Bishop: afterward erected into an Archbishopric, commanding twenty others under it, says Tyrius.

## p02165 · sig. 231a

St. Jerome names *Theophilus, Eusebius, Acacius, Euzoius* *Euzorus**, *and Gelasius to have been Bishops thereof. In this City was Cornelius the Centurion baptized by S. Peter: and herein dwelt Philip the Apostle: S. Paul was herein two years prisoner, under the President Felix *Foelix*, unto the time and government of Porcius Festus: by whom making his appeal, he was sent to Caesar. Here, when Herod Agrippa was passing on to celebrate the Quinquennalia, taking delight to be called a God by his flatterers, he was stricken with an Angel unto death, says Josephus.

## p02166 · sig. 231a

To the North of Caesarea stands Dora, or Naphoth Dor, as some read Josh.. 1. 2; So called( says Adrichem *Adrichomius*) because it joins to the Sea, whose King was slain by Joshua. But Junius for *in 〈◊ 〉 Dor*, reads *in tractibus Dor* ("Gabe. Jerome in the Hebrew Places"): and So the *Vulgar, in regionibus Dor* ("the Vulgate: 'in the regions of Dor'"): although 1. Reg. 4 11. for the like speech in the Hebrew it reads *omnis Nephath Dor* ("all Naphath-Dor"): The Septuagint in the place of Joshua call it Naphoth-Dor *Nepheth-Dor*, and in the other of the Kings, Naphoth-Dor: but the true name by other places( as Josh.. 12. 23. Judg. 1. 27.) may seem to be Dor. It was a strong and powerful 〈◊ 〉: and the fourth in account of those twelve Principalities or Sitarchies, which Solomon erected. Junius upon Maccabees *Macchab*. 15. 11. places it between the Hill Carmel and the mouth of the River Cherseus: for So some name the River Chorseus, of which we have spoken already.

## p02167 · sig. 231a

Into this City, for the strength thereof, Tryphon fled from Antiochus the son of Demetrius: where he was by the same Antiochus besieged with 120000. foot-men and 8000. Horse: the same perfidious villain that received 200. talents for the ransom of Jonathan *Ionathan* Maccabeus *Macchabaeus*( whom he had taken by treachery) and then slew him: and after him slew his own Master, usurping for a while the Kingdom of Syria. It had also a Bishops seat of the Diocesse of Caesarea.

## p02168 · sig. 231a

From Caesarea towards the South, they place the Cities of *Capharnaum, Gabe*, and Galgal: for besides that Capharnaum famous in the Evangelists *Euangelists*, they find in these parts near the West Sea, another of the same name. Of *Gabe Jerome in locis Hebratcis* ("Gaza. Phoenicia was called [so] among the ancients."). The famous Galgal or Gilgal, was in Benjamin *Beniamin*: but this Gilgal, they say, it was whose King was slain by Joshua.

## p02169 · sig. 231a

Then Antipatris So called of Herode, in honour of his Father: but in the time of the 〈◊ 〉 it was called Capharsalama: in the fields whereof Judas *Iudas* Maccabeus overthrew a part of the Army of Nicanor, Lieutenant to Demetrius: an army drawn into Judea by a traitorous Jew, called Alcimus: who contended for the Priest-hood, first under Bacchides, and then under Nicanor. To this was S. Paul carried prisoner from Jerusalem, conducted by 470. Soldiers *Souldiers*, to defend him from the fury of the Jews. In after-times the Army of Godfrey of Bouillon *Bulion* attempted it in vain: yet was it taken by Baldwin *Baldwine*. It was honoured in those days with a Bishops seat, but it is now a poor Village called Asshur *Assur*, says Brochard. Near unto this City the Prophet Jones was three days preserved in the body of a Whale.

## p02170 · sig. 231b

Into the Land, from Antipatris and Caesarea, stands Narbata, whereof the Territory takes name: which Cestius the Roman *Romane* wasted with fire and sword, because the Jews which dwelt at Caesarea fled thence, and carried with them the Books *Bookes* of Moses. Near unto it is the Mountain of Abdia, the Steward of King Ahab *Achab*: wherein he hid an hundredth Prophets, and fed them, after which he himself is said to have obtained from God the Spirit of prophecy also.
