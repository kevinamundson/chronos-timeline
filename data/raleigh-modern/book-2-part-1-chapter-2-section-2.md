§. II. *That by the account of the Egyptian* *Aegyptian** Dynasties, and otherwise, it appears that *CHAMS *reign in Egypt* *Aegypt** began in the year after the Flood* *Floud**, *191.

## p01622 · sig. 168b

TO reconcile these, or gather out of them the times of the ancient Kings, about whom is most controversy, the best mean is by help of the Dynasties: of whose continuance there is little or no disagreement. The account of the Dynasties( besides the authority of approved Authors) has this good ground, that it agrees for the most part, if not altogether with the Histories of the *Assyrians, Trojans* *Troians**, Italians*, and others, &c. The beginning of the 16. Dynasty. is joined by general consent, with the 43. year of Ninus: in which Abraham was borne. The twelve first Dynasties lasted each of them seven years, under the twelve, which were called the greater Gods: So that all the years of their continuance were 84. The thirteenth Dynast endured fourteen years: the fourteenth 26: the fifteenth 37. These three last, are said to have been under the three younger *yonger* Gods. So the 〈◊ 〉 first Dynasties lasted one hundred sixty one years. As I do not therefore believe that the continuance of these Dynasty. was such as has been mentioned, because Annius in such-wise limits out their time: So I cannot reject the account upon this only reason, that Annius has it So: considering that both hitherto it has passed as currant, and is greatly strengthened by many good reasons. For, whereas Eusebius places the beginning of the sixteenth Dynasty, in the year of Abrahams birth, as aforesaid: the reckoning is easily cast; by which the sum of 161. years, which according to our account were spent in the fifteen former, being subtracted out of the sum of 352. years, which were between the Flood and Abrahams birth, show that the beginning of the first Dynasty, which was the beginning of Chams reign in Egypt, was in the year 191. As also by other probabilities the same may appear. For it is generally agreed, that the multitude of mankind which came into Shinar, arrived at *Babel, Anno a diluvio* ("Babel, in the year after the Flood.")* *〈◊ 〉. In building the Tower were consumed forty years, as Glicas records: whose report I have elsewhere confirmed with divers probabilities. That Ham *Cham* was long in passing with his company, their Wives *Wiues* Children, Cattle, and substance, through all 〈◊ 〉 then desolate, and full of Bogs *Bogges*, Forrests, and Briars *Bryers*( which the Deluge and want of culture in one hundred seventy one years had brought upon it) no reasonable man will doubt. To this his passage therefore, and the seating of himself in Egypt, we allow twenty year: and these sums being added together, to wit, one hundred thirty one years after the Flood, before they arrived at Babel, 40. years for their stay there, and 20. for 〈◊ 〉 passage into Egypt, and settling there, make up the sum of 191. years: at which time we said that Ham began his reign in Egypt, in the beginning of the first Dynasty. And to this sum of 191. years if we ad the 161. years of the 15. first Dynasties, as they are numbered in common account, we shall fall right with the year of Abrahams birth, which was An. Dil. 352. And hereto omitting many other reasons, which might be brought to prove that these first Dynasties must needs have been very short, and not containing in the whole sum of their several times above 161. years: Let it suffice that had they lasted longer, then either must Egypt have been peopled as soon as Babel after the Flood: or the Dynasties( as 〈◊ 〉 thinks) must have been before the Flood. That the arrival at Babel was many years before the plantation of Egypt, after the Flood, enough has been said to prove: and that the Dynasties were not before the Flood the number of the long-lived generations between Adam and the Flood, which was less than the number of the Dynasties *Dinasties*, may sufficiently witness. Or if we will think, that one life might( perhaps) be divided into many Dynasties, then may this have been as well after the Flood, as before: considering that the sons of 〈◊ 〉 did not in every Country erect such form of Policy, as had been used in the same ere the Deluge: but such, as the disposition of the people, the authority and power of the Conductor, together with many other circumstances, did induce or enforce them to.
