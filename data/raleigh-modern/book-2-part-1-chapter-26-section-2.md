§. II. Of ACHERRES; whether he were UCHOREUS *VCHOREVS* that was the eighth from Ozymandias *OSYMANDYAS*. Of Ozymandias and his Tomb.

## p03178 · sig. 356a

IN this business I hold it vain to be too curious. For who can hope to attain to the perfect knowledge of the truth, when as Diodorus varies from *Herodotus, Eusebius*, from both of them; and late Writers, that have sought to gather the truth out of these and others, find no one with whom they can agree? In this case Annius would do good service, if a man could trust him. But it is enough to be beholding to him, when others do either say nothing, or that which may justly be suspected. I will therefore hold my self contented, with the pleasure that he has done me, in saying somewhat of *Osiris, Isis, Horus* *Orus*, and those antiquities removed So far out of sight: as for the Kings following the departure of Israel out of Egypt *Aegypt*, it shall suffice, that *Herodotus, Diodorus*, and Eusebius, have not been silent, and that Reineccius has taken pains, to range into some good order the names that are extant in these, or found scattering in others.

## p03179 · sig. 356a

From the 〈◊ 〉 of Israel out of Egypt, unto the reign of Thuoris( who is generally taken to be the same that the Greeks call Proteus) there is little or no disagreement about the Egyptian *Aegyptian* Kings. Wherefore I set down the same which are found in Eusebius, and give to every one the same length of reign.

## p03180 · sig. 356a

Acherres was the first of these, who succeeded unto Chenchres, that perished in the Red Sea. This King seems to Reineccius to be the same whom Diodorus calls 〈◊ 〉, the founder of Memphis. But whereas mention is found in Diodorus of a great King, named Ozymandias, from whom Uchoreus is said to be the eighth; it will either hardly follow, that Timaus( as Reineccius conjectures) was the great Ozymandias; or else that this Acherres was Uchoreus: for the distance between them was more than eight generations. Mercator judges Ozymandias to have been the husband of *Acencheres, Horus *the second his Daughter; thinking that Manethon ( cited by Josephus *Iosephus*) does omit his name, and insert his wives, into the Catalogue of Kings, because he was King in his wives right. As for Uchoreus, it troubles not Mercator to find him the eighth from this man: for he takes Ogdous, not to signify in this place of Diodorus *Diodore*( as that Greek *Greeke* word else does) the eighth, but to be an Egyptian name, belonging also to Uchoreus, who might have had two names, as many of the rest had. I will not vex my brains in the unprofitable search of this, and the like inextricable doubts. All that Diodorus has found of this Ozymandias, was wrought upon his monument; the most thereof in figures, which I think the Egyptians *Aegyptians* did fabulously expound. For whereas there was portrayed a great Army with the siege of a Town, the Captivity *captiuitie* of the People, and the triumph of the Conqueror; all this, the Egyptians said to denote the conquest of Bactria made by that King: which how likely it was, let others judge. I hold this goodly piece of work, which Diodorus So particularly describes, to have been erected for a common place of burial, to the ancient Kings and Queens *Queenes* of Egypt, and to their Viceroys *Viceroyes*; whilst yet they were not So ambitious, as every one to have his own particular monument, striving therein to exceed all others. This appears by the many statues, therein placed, by the Wars *Warres*, the judgement Seat, the receiving of Tribute, the offering Sacrifice to God, the account of Revenues *Reuennues*, and plenty of all Cattail and Food; all which were there curiously wrought, showing the several Offices of a Governor. On the Tomb of Ozymandias was this inscription. I am Ozymandias *King of Kings; If any desire to know what I am, or where I lie, let him exceed some of my works. *Let them, that hope to exceed his works, labour to know what he was. But since by those words, *Or where I lie*, it should seem that he lay not there interred, we may lawfully suspect that it was Joseph *Ioseph*, whose body was preserved among the Hebrews *Hebrewes*, to be buried in the Land of Canaan, and this empty Monument might King Horus, who out-lived him, erect in honor of his high deserts, among the royal Sepulchers. To which purpose, the plenty of Cattle, and all manner of viands, had good reference. The name Ozymandias does not hinder this conjecture; seeing Joseph had one new name given to him by Pharaoh, for expounding the dream, and might, upon further occasions, have another, to his increase of honor. As for that stile, *King of Kings*, it was perhaps no more than Beglerbeg, as the Turkish Bassas *Bassaes* are called, that is, Great above the Great.

## p03181 · sig. 356b

Now although it be So, that the reckoning falls out right, between the times of Joseph and Acherres( for Acherres was the eighth in order, that reigned after the great Horus, whose Viceroy Joseph was) yet will I hereby seek, neither to fortify mine own conjecture, as touching Joseph, nor to infer any likelihood of Acherres his being Uchoreus. For it might well be, that Memphis was built by some such King as was Jawhar *Gehoar*, Lieutenant unto the Caliph Elcain, who having to his Masters use, conquered Egypt, and many other Countries, did build, not far from old Memphis, the great City of Cairo( corruptly So pronounced) naming it El Qahira *Cahira*, that is, an enforcing, or an imperious Mistress, though he himself were a Dalmatian slave.
