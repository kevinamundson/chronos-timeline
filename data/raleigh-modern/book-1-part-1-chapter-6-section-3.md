# Book I, Part I, Chapter VI, §. 3

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p01065 · sig. 87b

§. III. *That in process of time these lesser errors drew on greater: as appears in the gross Superstitions of the Egyptians* *Aegyptians**.*

## p01066 · sig. 87b

BUT as men once fallen away from undoubted truth, do then after wander for evermore in vices unknown, and daily travail towards their eternal perdition: So did these gross and blind Idolaters every Age after other descend lower and lower, and shrink and slide downwards from the knowledge of one true and very GOD; and did not thereby er in worshipping mortal men only, but they gave divine reverence, and had the same respect to Beasts, Birds, Fishes, Fowls *Fowles*, Winds, Earth, Water, Air, Fire, to the Morning, to the Evening, to Plants, Trees and Roots *Rootes*, to Passions and and Affections of the Mind, to Paleness, Sickness, Sorrows *Sorrowes*, yet to the most unworthy and basest of all these. Which barbarous blasphemy, Rhodius Anaxandrides derides in this manner.
