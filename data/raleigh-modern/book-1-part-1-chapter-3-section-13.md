## p00973 · sig. 71b

§. XIII. *Of the River Pishon* *Pison**, and the land of Havilah* *Hauilah**.*

## p00974 · sig. 71b

After the discovery of Eden, and the testimonies of the fertility thereof, it rests to prove that Pishon and Gihon *Gehon* are branches of Tigris, and Euphrates. For that the knowledge and certainty of these two Rivers *Riuers* should trouble So many wise men, it is strange to me, seeing necessity it self( Tigris and Euphrates being known) finds them out: for Euphrates or Tigris, or both be that river or rivers of Eden, which water Paradise, which river or rivers Moses witnesses afterward, divided into four heads, whereof the one is called Pishon, the other Gihon, &c. Could there be a stranger fancy in the World, then when we find both these( namely) Tigris and Euphrates in Assyria and Mesopotamia, to seek the other two in India and Egypt *Aegypt*, making the one Ganges, and the other Nile *Nilus*? Two Rivers as far distant, as any of fame known or discovered in the World: the Scriptures making it So plain, that these Rivers were divided into four branches, and with the Scriptures, Nature, Reason and Experience bearing witness. There is no error, which has not some slippery and bad foundation, or some appearance of probability, resembling truth, which when men( who study to be singular) find out, ( straining reason according to their fancies) they then publish to the World matter of contention and jangling: not doubting but in the variable deformity of men's minds, to find some partakers or sectators, the better by their help to nurse and cherish such weak babes, as their own inventions have begotten.

## p00975 · sig. 71b

But this mistaking( and first for the river of Pishon) seems to have grown out of the not-distinguishing of that Region in India, called Havilah, from Havilah, which adjoins to Babylonia, afterward known by the name of Susiana. For Havilah upon Tigris took name from Havilah the son of Cush; and 〈◊ 〉 in India, from Havilah the son of 〈◊ 〉, the one remembered by Moses in the description of Paradise, the other where Moses sets down the generations of Noah, and his sons after the Flood *floud*. For the sons of Cush were *Seba, Havilah, Sabtah*, and Raamah, and the sons of Joktan *Ioctan* were Ophir, and Havilah, &c. of which latter( to wit) of Ophir and Havilah the sons of Joktan, that *Island of Ophir*, ( whence Solomon *Salomon* had gold) and Havilah adjoining had their names. Now because Ganges is a great and a famous River of the East India, and Havilah a Country of the same, and is situated upon Ganges, hence it came that Ganges was taken for Pishon, which River is said by Moses to 〈◊ 〉 the land of Havilah. Or perhaps it was supposed that those four Rivers, named by Moses, must of necessity be four of the greatest in the World; whence( supposing that Ganges was the next great and famous river after Tigris &amp; Euphrates) they chose out this River to make one of the four. And yet certainly there is another River, whom in these respects they should rather have chosen then Ganges; for the River Indus on this side India, for beauty, for nearness, & for ability, gives no way place to Ganges, but exceeds it in all. And how can any reasonable man conceive, that Ganges can be one of the four heads, seeing Indus comes between it and Tigris? and between Tigris and Indus is all that large Empire of Persia, consisting of many Kingdoms *Kingdomes*. And 〈◊ 〉, farther toward the East, and beyond Indus, are all those ample Dominions of India within the Ganges *Indiaintra* Gangem, which lie between those two proud Rivers of Indus and Ganges, now called the Kingdom of Mogor. So as if Indus be not accounted for any of the four, because it is removed from Tigris by all the breadth of Persia, then how much less Ganges, which falls into the Ocean, little less then forty degrees to the Eastward of Indus? Surely whosoever reads the Story of Alexander, shall find, that there is no river in Asia, that can exceed Indus. For Hydaspis was of that breadth and depth, as Alexander thereon in great Galleys *Gallies* transported himself, and the greatest part of his army, and in sailing down that branch of Indus, found it So large and deep, and by reason thereof So great a billow, as it endangered his whole Fleet, which was ready to be swallowed up therein: Hydaspis( as aforesaid) being but one of many branches of Indus, comparable to it, and as great as it, having besides this, the Rivers of Coas, of *〈◊ 〉, Acesines, Adris, *( otherwise *Hirotis, Hispalis*, and Zaradrus, all which make but one Indus, and by it are swallowed up with all their children and companions, which being all incorporate and made one stream, it crosses athwart Asia, and then at Cambay *Cambaia* visits the Ocean Sea.

## p00976 · sig. 72a

But because Pishon, which compasses Havilah, as also Gihon, which waters Cush, must some-where be joined with the rest in one body, or at least be found to proceed out of the same Country of Eden, out of which the other two heads do proceed, out of doubt they cannot either the one or the other, be Ganges, or Nile: for Nile rises in the uttermost of the South, and runs Northward into the Mediterranean *Mediterran* Sea; and the River Ganges rises out of the mountain Imaus, or( as others will have it) Caucasus, which divides the Northern Scythia from India, and runs from North to South into the Indian Ocean. And as for Euphrates *Perath* and Hiddekel( that is, Euphrates and Tigris) the one of them is begotten in Armenia, near Georgiana or Iberia, the other not far off in the same Armenia, by the 〈◊ 〉 mountains, So as Ganges, who only travels in her own India, and Nile through Ethiopia *Aethiopia* and Egypt, never saw the land of Eden, or joined themselves in one Channel, either with themselves, or with either of the other; and therefore could not at any time from thence be separated, or divided into four heads or branches, according to Moses.

## p00977 · sig. 72a

Therefore the River Pishon, which enriches Havilah, is the same which by joining it self with Tigris, was therefore called Pisi-Tigris, or Piso-Tigris, of Pishon and Tigris, which River waters that Havilah, which Havilah the son of Cush gave name unto, and not Havilah of India, So called of Havilah the son of Joktan, who inhabited with his brother Ophir in the East. And this Havilah of the Cushites has also Gold, Bdellium, and the Onyx stone. This Bdellium is a tree, of the bigness, of an Olive, whereof Arabia has great plenty, which yields a certain gum, sweet to smell to, but bitter in taste, called also Bdellium. The Hebrews *Hebrewes* take the Lodestone for Bdellium. Beroaldo *Beroaldus* affirms, that Bdela in Hebrew signifies Pearl: So does Eugubinus; and Jerome *Hierome* calls it Oleaster: be it what it will, a tree bearing gum, or Pearl: Havilah, or Susiana has plenty of both. Now this Country of 〈◊ 〉 or Havilah stretches it self toward the North, as far as the Altars of Hercules, and from thence embraces all that Tract of land Southward, as far as the Persian Gulf, on the East side thereof: from which East side had the Shebans( which traded with the City of Tyre, according to Ezekiel) their great plenty of gold, which Strabo also witnesses, as was showed before.

## p00978 · sig. 72a

The Greeks *Greekes* had a conceit, that Pishon was Danubius: the Rabbins *Rabbines* take it for *〈◊ 〉. Aben-Ezra*( says Hopkins) out of Rabbi Saadia translates Pishon into Nile: But Nile finds the same impossibility that Ganges does: and Danubius has the Sea of Hellespont and all Asia the less, between it and Tigris. Now Pishon, which runs through Havilah or Susiana, does to this day retain some sign of this name; for where it and Tigris embrace each other under the City of Apamia, there do they agree of a joint and compounded name, and are called Piso-Tigris. And it is strange unto me, that from So great antiquity there should be found remaining any resembling sound of the first name: for Babylon itself, which dwells So near these rivers, is by some writers known by the name of Bandas, as, by Postellus, by Castaldus, of Baldach: by Barius, of Baghdad *Bagdad*; and of Baghdad, by Andrew Thevet *Theuet*; and yet all those that have lately seen it, call it Baghdad. To this river of *Pishon, Ptolemy* *Ptolomie* indeed with many others give the name of Basilius, or Regius, and Gihon they term Mahar-sares and 〈◊ 〉, and Baarsares. So is Euphrates, near the Spring and fountain, by Strabo and Pliny *Plinie* called Pixirates: by *Junius* *Iunius**, 〈◊ 〉*, out of the Hebrew, ( that is) The profusion, or coming forth of Euphrates: where it breaks through the mountain Taurus, it takes the name of *Omyra. Plutarch *calls it Medus and Zaranda: the Hebrews Parath, ( says Ar. Montanus: ) *Pagninus, Euphrates: Josephus* *Iosephus**, Phorah; Eusebius* *Eusibius**, Zosimus* *Zozimus**: Ammianus, Chalymicus: Gistilanus, *and Colinutius term it Chebar *Cobar*: which Ezekiel *Ezechiel* calls Chebar; but this is but a branch of Euphrates. The Assyrians know it by the name of Armalchar, or Nahor Malcha: but now commonly it is called Frat.

## p00979 · sig. 72b

The same confusion of names bath Tigris, as Diglito, and *Diglath, Seilax*, and Sollax: of the Hebrews it was called Hiddekel *Hiddikel*: now of the inhabitants Tegil.

## p00980 · sig. 72b

But Mercer upon Genesis conceives rightly of these rivers: for Euphrates and Tigris( says he) stream into four branches, two of which keep their ancient names, and the other two are called Pishon and Gihon. The reason, why these two rivers joined in one( below Apamia) lose their names, and are called Pisi-Tigris, and the memory of Euphrates extinguished, is, because the best part of Euphrates running through the channel of Gihon, sinks into the Lakes of Chaldea *Chaldaea*, not far from Ur, the City of Abraham, and fall not entirely into the Persian Sea, as Tigris accompanied with Pishon does.

## p00981 · sig. 72b

This error that Pishon was Ganges, was first broached by Josephus, ( whose fields, though they be fertile, yet are they exceeding full of weeds) and other men( who take his authority to be sufficient in matter of description, whereupon depended no other important consequence) were not curious in the examination thereof. For *Epiphanius* *Epiphantus**, Augustine*, and Jerome, take this for currant; whereof it followed, that as Pishon was transported into the East India, to find out Havilah: So was Gihon drawn into Africa, to compass Ethiopia. But if Havilah, whereof Muses speaks in the description of Paradise, be found to be a Region, adjoining to Babylon on the one side, and Cush( which is falsely interpreted Ethiopia) fastened to it on the other side, we shall not need then to work wonders( that is) to impose upon men the transportation of rivers, from one end of the World to the other, which( among other uses) were made to transport men. Now it was in the valley of Shinar, where Cush the son of Ham first sate down with his sons *Shebah, Havilah, Sabtah, Raamah, Nimrod, & c. *and of Havilah, the son of Cush, did that Region take name, which Pishon compasses; and the land( called Cush) which Gihon waters, took name of Cush himself. For as the sons of *Joktan, Ophir *and Havilah, seated themselves as near together as they could in India, So did the sons of Cush in Shinar or Babylonia, where Nimrod built Babel: for Havilah or Chavilah *Chauilah* was first Chusea of Cush; then *Chusa, Susa*, and 〈◊ 〉.

## p00982 · sig. 72b

From this Havilah unto the Deserts *Desarts* of Sur, did the Israelites and Amalechites possess all the interjacent Countries: for Saul smote the Amalechites from Havilah to Sur: which Sur, the Chaldean *Chaldaean* Paraphrast converts Hagra, and Hagra borders the red Sea; but this was not meant from Sur upon the red Sea, to Havilah in the East India, for Saul was no such traveller or Conqueror, and therefore Havilah must be found nearer home, where the sons of Ismael inhabited, and which Country Saul wasted: for Amalek and the Amalechites possessed that neck of Country, between the Persian Sea, and the red Sea; Havilah being the extreme of the one towards the East, and Sur of the other, towards Egypt and the West, leaving that great body of Arabia Felix *foelix* towards the South, and they spread themselves with the Midianites and Idumeans *Edumaeans*, from the East part, or back side of the Holy Land, to the banks of Euphrates, comprising the best parts of Arabia Petraea, and Deserta.
