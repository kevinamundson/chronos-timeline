# Book II, Part I, Chapter IV, §. 9

## p01763 · sig. 191a

§. IX. Of the Moral, Judicial, and Ceremonial Law, with a note prefixed, How the Scripture speaks not always in one sense, when it names the Law of MOSES.

## p01764 · sig. 191a

Now as the word( Law) in general, as is before said, has divers significations, and is taken for all doctrine which does prescribe and restrain: So this Law, called the Law of Moses in particular, is taken by S. Paul diversely; as sometime for all the old Testament, as, Now we *know whatsoever the Law says, it says to them which are under the Law.*

## p01765 · sig. 191a

When it is opposed, or differenced from the Prophets and Psalms *Psalmes*, it is there taken for the five Books *Bookes* of Moses. For So Saint Luke has distinguished them; as, *All must be fulfilled which are written of me in the Law, in the Prophets, and in the* Psalms.

## p01766 · sig. 191a

When it is opposed to the Gospel, then it is taken for the Law Moral, Ceremonial, and Judicial; as, *Therefore we conclude, that a man is justified by faith without the* *works of the Law*.

## p01767 · sig. 191a

When it is opposed to Grace, it signifies the declaration of Gods wrath, and our guilt of condemnation; or the extremity of the law, and summum ius: as, *For ye are* *not under the Law, but under grace.*

## p01768 · sig. 191a

When it is opposed to the Truth, namely, where the Ceremonies or signs are taken for the things signified; as the Sacrifice for Christ, and the like: then it signifies but shadows and figures; as, *The Law was given by *MOSES, *but grace and truth* came by JESUS *IESVS* CHRIST.

## p01769 · sig. 191a

Lastly, when it is opposed to the time of Christs coming, it signifies the whole policy of the Jews *Iewes* Common-weal *Common-weale*; as, *Before faith came, we were kept under the* *Law, & c*. or the Law of the order and institution of the Aaronic Priesthood; as, *All the Prophets and the Law, *or the Priests, prophesied unto JOHN *IOHN*. And if the Priesthood be changed, the Law also, to wit, of the Priesthood, must needs be changed.

## p01770 · sig. 191a

The word( Law) is sometime also taken by the Figure Metonymy, for Interest, Authority, and Empire, or for constraining force; as *The Law of the Spirit of life,* *the law or the force of sin and death, the enforcements of concupiscence, &c.*

## p01771 · sig. 191a

But the *Written Law of *MOSES, or the Law of the Old Testament, of which we now speak, is thus defined. The Law is a doctrine, which was first put into the 〈◊ 〉 of men by God, and afterwards written by Moses, or by him repeated, commanding Holiness and Justice, promising eternal life conditionally, that is, to the observers of the law, and threatening death to those which break the law in the least. For according to Saint *James* *Iames**, Whosoever* *Whosoeuer** shall keep the whole, and fails in* *one point, is guilty of all. *The definition used by the Schoolmen *Schoolemen*, in which both the Old and New law are comprehended, is thus given. *Lex divina est divinam decretum, hominibus praescribens modum necessarium ut apt peruenire possint ad supernaturalem beatitudinem, quae est ultimus humanae vitae finis; The divine law*( say they) *is the decree of God, prescribing unto men a necessary mean, where by they may aptly attain supernatural beatitude, which is the last end of mans life.*

## p01772 · sig. 191b

The law of Moses has three parts: Moral, Ceremonial, and Judicial. The Moral part commands this or that good to be done, and this or that evil to be avoided, in particular; as also it declares, for whose sake it is to be done; as *Do this, for I am the Lord, *whereas the law of nature commands it but in general. Again, the Moral law entreats of virtue and goodness; The Ceremonial of divine service, and of holiness; ( for external worship, and the order of hallowing our selves unto God is called Ceremony) and the Judicial teaches the particular government, fit for the Common-wealth of the Jews, and prescribes orders for justice and equity. And therefore was it said of Saint *Paul, The commandment is just, holy* and good: just, or justice being referred to the Judicial: holy, or holiness to the Ceremonial; good, or honest to the Moral. The Judicial part is touching the government of the commonwealth of the Jews, in which many things must needs be proper to that estate, as such as were instituted either in respect of place or persons.

## p01773 · sig. 191b

The Ceremonial is divided into four parts, according to the four kinds of things of which it speaks, to wit, Sacrifice, Holy things, Sacraments, and Observances *Obseruances*. To Sacrifices belong beasts, and the fruits of the earth; to Holy things the Tabernacle, Temple, Vessels, Altars, and the like; to Sacraments, Circumcision, the Passover *Passeouer*, & such like. For the Observances, they consisted either in prohibition of certain meats, as not to 〈◊ 〉 the blood and fat of beasts: or in some other outward things, as in washings, purifyings, anointings, and attire, as not to wear mixed garments of Linen and Woollen; as also it prohibits other unnatural and improper mixtures, as *Thou shalt not yoke together in a plough an Oxe and an Ass, *or cast mingled seed in one field. It also exhorts natural compassion, and forbids cruelty even to beasts, birds, and plants, whereby the creatures of God might be destroyed without any profit to man. For So some refer these precepts, *Thou shalt not kill the bird sitting on her nest; nor beat down the first buds of the tree, nor muzzle the labouring Oxe, *and the like, to the Ceremonial Law.

## p01774 · sig. 191b

Neither is there any of these three parts of the Law of Moses, but it has as yet in some respects, the same power which it had before the coming of Christ. For the Moral lives still, and is not abrogated or taken away: saving in the ability of justifying or condemning; for therein are we commanded to love and worship God: and to use charity one towards another: which for ever shall be required at our hands. Therein also are we in particular directed, how this ought to be done: which power of directing by special rules and precepts of life, it retains still. For these things also are commanded in both Testaments to be observed: though principally for the fear of God in the one, and for the love of God in the other.

## p01775 · sig. 191b

The Ceremonial also lives in the things which it fore-signified. For the shadow is not destroyed, but perfected, when the body it self is represented to vs. Besides, it still lives, in that it gives both instruction and testimony of Christ, and in that it gives direction to the Church, for some Ceremonies and Types of holy signification, which are still expedient; though in a far fewer number than before Christs coming and in a far less degree of necessity.

## p01776 · sig. 191b

Lastly, the Judicial lives in substance, and concerning the end, and the natural and universal equity thereof.

## p01777 · sig. 192a

But the Moral fails in the point of justification, the Ceremonial as touching the use and external observation( because Christ himself is come, of whom the ceremonies were signs and shadows) and the Judicial is taken away, as far forth as it was peculiar to the Jews Common-weal, and policy.
