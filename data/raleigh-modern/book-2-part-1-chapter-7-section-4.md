§. IIII. *THE TRIBE OF Naphtali* *NEPHTALIM*.

## p02083 · sig. 224a

†. I. *Of the bounds of Naphtali, and of Heliopolis, and Abila.*

## p02084 · sig. 224a

The next Portion of the Land of Canaan bordering Asher, was the upper Galilee: the greatest part whereof fell to the lot of Naphtali, the Son of Jacob *Iacob* by Bilhah *Billa*, the hand-maid of Rachel: who while they abode in Egypt *Aegypt*, were increased to the number of 53400. persons, able to bear arms, numbered at Mount Sinai: all which leaving their bodies in the Deserts *Desarts*, there entered the Holy Land of their Sons 45400. besides, Infants, Women, and Children, under twenty years of age. The Land of Naphtali took beginning on the North part, from the Fountains *Fountaines* of Jordan, and the Hills of Lebanon *Libanus* adjoining, as far South as the Sea of Galilee, bounded on the West by Asher, and on the East and South-east by Jordan.

## p02085 · sig. 224a

On the North-side of Lebanon, and adjoining to this Territory of Naphtali, did the Amorites( or Emorites) also inhabit, in which Tract and under Lebanon, was the City of Heliopolis: which the height of the Mountains *Mountaines* adjoining shadowed from the Sun, the better part of the day. POSTELLUS *POSTELLVS* calls it Baalbek *Balbec*; NIGER, Baalbek; and Leunclavius *LEONCLAVIVS*, Baalbek.

## p02086 · sig. 224a

Of this name of Heliopolis, there are two great Cities in Egypt: the first called On, by the Hebrews *Hebrewes*, and the Chaldean *Chaldaean* Paraphrast, otherwise Bethsemes, or after the *Latins* *Latines**, Solis oppidum* ("Upon this Rock, etc."), or *Domus Solis; The City of the Sun: *into which, says *Ulpian* *Vlpian**, Severus* *Seuerus** *the Roman Emperor sent a Colony: the other Gestelius names Dealmarach: and of this name Stephanus also finds a City in Thrace, and Glycas in Phrygia.

## p02087 · sig. 224a

There is also in the same Valley adjoining to *Naphtali, Chalcis*, and *Abila. Chalcis*, of whom the Region towards Palmyrena has the name of Chalcidice *Chalcidica*, over which *Herod, Agrippa*, and Berenice the Queen commanded.

## p02088 · sig. 224a

Abila also gave name to the Region adjoining, of which Lysanius the Son of Herod the elder, became Tetrarch or Governor: whereof Ptolemy *Ptolomie* gave it the addition of Lysanij, and called it *Abila Lysanij. Volaterran *names it Aphila, of which he notes that one Diogenes, a famous Sophister, was native, who by Volaterran is entitled Aphileus, not Abileus. After that this City of Abila or Aphila, had received the Christian Faith, Priscillinus became Bishop thereof: slain afterward by our British Maximus at Trier. For distinction of this City( if it be not the same, as it may be thought to be the same) it is to be remembered that in the Tribe of a Manasseh *Manasse*, joining upon the bounds of the Tribe of Naphtali, there is another City of the same name, saving that it is written with an( E) for an( I) and called Abela, remembered in the 20. Chapter of the second of Samuel. The same Josephus *Iosephus* calls Abel-beth-maachah *Abelmachea*, and Jerome *Hierome* Beth-maachah *Bethmacha*. In the place of Samuel for distinction sake it is written, Abel Beth-Mahaca, ( for probably it was the Town of Mahaca, the Wife of Machir *Macir*, the Son of Manasseh, the Father of Gilead) in the Chronicles it is called Abel-Maijm. This City Joab *Ioab* besieged: because Seba the son of Bichri, who rebelled against David *Dauid*, fled into it for succour: but a certain wise woman of the City persuading the people to cast Seba his head over the wall, Joab retired his Army. The same City was afterward taken by the King of *Damascus, Benadad*: and after a while by Tiglath-Pileser *Teglatphalasar*.

## p02089 · sig. 224a

The word Abel may be expounded, either to signify bewailing, or a plain ground, and therefore no marvel, that many Towns *Townes*( with some addition for distinction sake) were thus called: for even of bewailing many places took name, as *Bochim, Judg* *Iudg*. 2. 4. and So doubtless * *Abel-Misraim, Gen. *50. 11. and yet Junius *Iunius* in his note upon Num. 33. 49. thinks that Abel-Sittim was So called, rather by reason of the plain ground there( to wit, in the Land of Moab, and So perhaps Abel-Meholah in the Tribe of Ephraim: the Town of Elisha the Prophet: also Abel-Vinearum of the 〈◊ 〉, whither Jephthah *Iephta* pursued them.

## p02090 · sig. 224b

†. II. Of Hazor.

## p02091 · sig. 224b

IN this Tribe of Naphtali, was that famous City of Jabin *Iabin*, in Joshua's *Iosua's* time called Asor( or after the *Chaldean Paraphrast, Hazor* *Haszor*) by *Josephus, Hazor*; by Junius * Chatzor: which Laicstan names Hezron *Hesron*; the Regal City, and Metropolis of Canaan: seated in the West part of Naphtali, towards Asher. In this City was that great Rendezvous, and assembly of those four and twenty Kings against Joshua *Iosua*: who being all overthrown, slain, and scattered, this their powerful City was by Joshua taken and burnt to dust. But in process of time the same being rebuilt by the Canaanites, a second King Jabin, 137. years after the death of this first Jabin, invaded the Israelites: and being ordained of God to punish their Idolatry, he prevailed against them, and held them in a miserable servitude twenty years: till Debora the Prophetess overthrew *Sisera, Jabins* *Iabins** *Lieutenant, and his Army, near the Mountain Tabor. This City Solomon *Salomon* restored at such time as he also rebuilt Gezar, burnt by Pharaoh *Pharao* of Egypt, with a *Megiddo* *Megido**, Bethoron*, and other Cities; but about 260. years after, it fell into the hands of Tiglath-Pileser, King of the Assyrians. It is now, says Adrichem *Adrichomius*, called Antiopia: it was one of the principal Cities of Decapolis. There is another City of this name in the Territory of Benjamin *Beniamin*, seated on the confines of Ashkelon *Ascalon*, called the new Hazor, b says Jerome.

†. III. *Of Caesarea* *Caesaria** Philippi*.

## p02093 · sig. 224b

There was also on the border, and within the Territory of Naphtali, that renowned City of Lais, or Laish *Laijsch*, as Junius writes it, or Laish; which City the children of Dan( being straitened in their Territory under Judah *Iuda*) invaded and mastered; and gave it the name of their own Parent Dan: and by that name it is written in Gen. the fourteenth, at which place Abraham surprised Chedorlaomer and his confederates, and followed his victory as far as Zobah *Sobah*, formerly remembered in the division of Syria, otherwise called Sophena. And after the 〈◊ 〉 of the Danites, it had the joint name of *Leshem-Dan* *Leschem-Dan**. Weissenburg *writes it Laish, the 〈◊ 〉 Laish, JOSEPHUS, Dan *Dana*; Benjamin, Dan; BREITENBACH, Dan: but the now Inhabitants know it by the name of Dan to this day: witness *of Newburgh* *Neubrigensis**, Tyrius, Volaterranus, Brochard *the Monk, and Postellus: who also takes this City to be the same, which in Matthew the 15. v. 39. in the Vulgar is called Magedan, for which the Greek *Greeke* Text has Magdala in that place, and in S. Mark speaking of the same story, Dalmanutha. At such time as the children of Dan obtained this place, it seems that it was either a free City, of the alliance and confederacy of the Sidonians *Zidonians*, or else subject unto the Kings thereof; for it is written in the eighteenth of Judges *Iudges*, *And there was none to help, because Lais was far from Sidon* *Zidon**: and they had no business with other men, *for it was above thirty English miles from the Mediterranean *Mediterran* Sea, and from Sidon.

## p02094 · sig. 225a

In after times when these Regions became subject to the State of Rome, it had the name of Paneas, from a Fountain adjoining So called: and therefore Ptolemy calls it *Caesarea Paniae. Hegesippus *calls it Paneas *Parnium*, says Weissenburg: but he had read it in a corrupt copy: for in Hegesippus set out by Badius, it is written Paneas without an( R) : and at such time as Philip the son of the elder Herod, brother to *Herod, Tetrarch *of 〈◊ 〉, became Governor of Trachonitis *Traconitis*, sometime Bashan *Basan*; this City was by him amplified and fortified; and both to give memory to his own name, and to flatter Tiberius Caesar, he called it * Caesarea Philippi: and So it became the Metropolis, and head City of Trachonitis: and one of the first Cities of Decapolis. And being by Agrippa in the succeeding age greatly adorned: by him in honour of Nero, it was called *Neronia, or Neroniada*. But as nothing remained with that Emperor, but the memory of his impiety: So in S. Jerome's *Hieromes* time the Citizens remembered their former Paneas, and So recalled it, with the Territory adjoining by the ancient name. Of this City was that woman whom Christ healed of a bloody issue, by touching the hem of his Garment with a constant Faith: who afterward, as she was a woman of great wealth and ability, being mindful of Gods goodness, and no less grateful for the same, as Eusebius and Nicephorus report, caused two Statues to be cast in pure Copper: the one representing *Christ, as near as it could be moulded: *the other made like her self, kneeling at his feet, and holding up her hands towards him. These she mounted upon two great Bases or Pedestals of the same Metal, which she placed by a Fountain near her own house: both which( says Eusebius) remained in their first perfection, even to his own time: which himself had seen, who lived in the Reign of Constantine the Great. But in the year after Christ 363. that Monster Julius *Iulius* Apostata, caused that worthy Monument to be cast down, and defaced: setting up the like of his own in the same place: which Image of his was with fire from heaven broken into fitters: the head, body, and other parts, sundered and scattered, to the great admiration of the people at that time living. The truth of this accident is also confirmed by Sozomen of *Sozomenus* Salamis *Salaminius*, in his fifth book, and twentieth Chapter.

## p02095 · sig. 225a

This City built by the Danites, was near the joining together of those two Rivers *Riuers* which arise from the springs of Jor *Ior* and Dan, the two a apparent Fountains of Jordan: in a soil exceeding fruitful, and pleasant; for, as it is written, Judg. 18. It is a place *which does want nothing that is in the World. *In the fields belonging to this City, it was that S. Peter acknowledged Christ to be the Son of God: whereupon it was answered, Tues PETRUS *PETRVS*, & *Super hanc Petram, &c* ("neighbouring to Galilee of the Gentiles")*.* After this City received the Christian faith, it was honoured with a Bishops seat: and it ran the same fortune with the rest, for it was after taken and retaken by the Saracens, and Christians: under Fulk *Fulch* the fourth, King of Jerusalem *Hierusalem*, and after the death of Godfrey *Godfry* of Bullion, the King of Damascus wrested it from the Christians; and shortly after by them again it was recovered. Lastly, now it remains with all that part of the World subjected to the Turk *Turke*.

†. IIII. *Of Capernaum, and the Cities of Decapolis.*

## p02097 · sig. 225a

Among the remarkable Cities within this Tribe, Capharnaum is not the least: So often remembered by the Evangelists *Euangelists*. This City had the honour of Christs presence three years: who for that time was as a Citizen thereof, in which he first preached and taught the Doctrine of our salvation: according to that notable Prophecy of Isaiah *Esay* 9. *The people that walked in darkness, have seen a great light: they that dwelt in the Land of the shadow of death, upon them has the light shined.*

## p02098 · sig. 225a

Capharnaum was seated on Jordan, even where it enters into the Sea of Galilee: in an excellent and rich soil: of whose destruction Christ himself prophesied in these words. *And thou Capernaum* *Caphernaum** which art lifted up unto heaven, shalt be brought down to Hell, & c. *which showed the pride and greatness of that City: for it was one of the principal Cities of Decapolis, and the Metropolis of Galilee. And although there were some marks of this Cities magnificence in S. Jerome's time, as himself confesses: it being then a reasonable Burg or Town: yet those that have since, and long since seen it, as *Brochard, Breidenbach* *Breidenbech*, and Salignac *Saliniac* affirm, that it then consisted but of six poor Fisher-men's *Fisher-mens* houses.

## p02099 · sig. 225b

The Region of ten principal Cities called Decapolitana or Decapolis, is in this description often mentioned, and in S. *Matthew, Mark*, and Luke, also remembered; but I find no agreement among the Cosmographers, what proper limits it had: and So Pliny *Plinie* himself confesses; for Marius Niger speaking from others, bounds it on the North by the mountain Casius in 〈◊ 〉: and ends it to the South at Egypt and Arabia; by which description it embraces Phoenicia, a part of Coelesyria, all Palestine *Palaestina*, and Judea *Iudaea*.

## p02100 · sig. 225b

Pliny also makes it large, and for the ten Cities of which it takes name, he numbers four of them to be situated towards Arabia: to wit, first these three, *Damascus, Opotos, Raphana*, then Philadelphia( which was first called Amana, says Stephanus, or as I guess Amona rather, because it was the chief City of the Ammonites, known by the name of Rabbah, before Ptolemy. 〈◊ 〉 gave it this later and new name. ) Then Scythopolis, sometime Nisa, built( as is said) by 〈◊ 〉, in memory of his Nurse, who died therein, anciently known by the name of Beth-shean *Bethsan*; for the sixth he sets Gadara( not that Gadara in Coelesyria, which was also called Antioch and 〈◊ 〉: ) but it is Gadara in Bashan, which Plinte in this place means, seated on a high hill, near the River of Hieromaix. This River Ortelius takes to be the River Jabbok *Iaboc*: which bounds Gad and Manasseh over Jordan: but he mistakes it; for Hieromaix falls into the Sea of Galilee, between Hippos and Gerasa, whereas Jabbok enters the same Sea between Ephron and Phanuel. For the seventh *seuenth* he names * Hippos, or Hippos *Hippion*, a City So called of a Colony of Horsemen there garrisoned by Herod, on the East side of the 〈◊ 〉 Sea, described hereafter in the Tribe of Manasseh over Jordan. For the eighth Pella, which is also called Butis, and Berenice, seated in the South border of the Region over Jordan, called Peraea. For the ninth Gerasa *Gelasa*, which Josephus takes to be Gerasa: and Gerasa is found in Coelesyria by *Josephus, Hegesippus*, and Stephanus: but by Ptolemy( whom I rather follow) in Phoenicia. The tenth and last, Pliny names Canatha, and So does Suetonius and Stephanus, which Volaterran calls Gamala, but Hegesippus rightly Gamala *Camala*, a City in the Region of Bashan over Jordan, So called, because those two Hills on which it is seated, have the shape of a Camel. But the collection of these ten Cities, whereof this Region took name, is better gathered out of *Brochard, Breidenbach*, and Salignac, which make them to be these; Caesarea Philippi, and Asor, before remembered, *Cedes Naphtali, Sephet, Corazin, Capharnaum, Bethsaida, Jotapata* *Iotapata**, Tiberias, *and Scythopolis, or Beth-shean. For all other Authors disagree herein and give no reason for their opinion. One place of the Evangelist Saint Matthew makes it manifest, that this Region called Decapolitana, was all that Tract between Sidon, and the Sea of Galilee. For thus it is written: *And he departed again* *from the coasts of Tyre* *Tyrus** and Sidon, and came unto the Sea of Galilee, through the midst of the coasts of Decapolis: *So that it was bounded by Damascus and Lebanon on the North: by the Phoenician Sea, between Sidon and Ptolemais *Ptolomais* on the West: by the Hills of Gilboa *Gelbo* and Beth-shean on the South: and by the Mountains Trachonitis, otherwise *Hermon, Sanir*, and Gilead *Galaad*, on the East: which is from East to West the whole breadth of the Holy Land: and from the North to the South, near the same distance which may be each way forty English miles.

†. VI. *Of Reblatha and Rama, and divers other Towns.*

## p02111 · sig. 226b

IN the border of Hamath or Hamath towards Jordan stands the City Reblatha, or Ribla, watered from the fountain Daphnis: which falls into the lake of Meron. Hereunto was Zedekias brought prisoner, after his surprise in the fields of Jericho *Iericho*: and delivered to Nebuchadnezzar *Nabuchodonosor*: who to be avenged of Zedekia's infidelity, beyond the proportion of piety, first caused the Princes his children to be slain in his presence: and to the end that this miserable spectacle might be the last that ever he should behold in this World, and So the most remembered, he commanded both his eyes presently to be thrust out: and binding him in iron chains, he was led a slave to Babylon, in which estate he ended his life. Of which seldom-exampled calamity, though not in express words, Jeremiah *Hieremie* the Prophet fore-told him in Jerusalem not long before: But Ezekiel thus directly, speaking in the person of God, *I will bring him to Babel to the Land of the Chaldeans* *Chaldaeans**, yet shall he not see it, though he shall die therein.*

In the body of the Land they place Galgala to the South border: of which * Mac. 1 9. 2. also divers others named, Josh.. 19. as Hukkok *Vcuca* or *Hukkok: Horem *&amp; *〈◊ 〉- tabor*( which they place towards the East parts) and out of the same place of *〈◊ 〉: 〈◊ 〉, Lakkum Jabneel* *Iepnaell**, Heleph* *Heleb**, *and a Rakkath *Reccath*, which two last they place near Coesaria Philippi: To these they ad out of *Joshua, Nekeb*, and Adami: for which two Junius reads Fossa Adamah, making it no Town but a Ditch cast by some of Adamath, as it seems; or at least the custody of which March or Limit belonging to the Town. To these out of Num. 34. 10. they ad Shepham *Sephana* which 1. Sam.. 30. 21. seems to be called Sipmoth. As for Hatticon *Tichon* and Helon, whereof the former they fetch out of 〈◊ 〉. 47. 16. and the latter out of Joshua 19. 33. it may appear by Junius his Translation, that neither are to be taken for Cities: for the former he reads Mediani, and for the latter Quercetum. The City of b Nephthalim which they make the native place of Tobias *Tobie*, and Naasson near unto it, they fetch out of the Vulgar Translation, Tob. 7. 7. but in the Greek Text there is no sign, neither of the one nor of the other.
