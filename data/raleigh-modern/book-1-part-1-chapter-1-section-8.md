§. VIII. Of the Firmament, and of the waters above the Firmament: and whether there be any crystalline Heaven, or any Primum mobile.

## p00838 · sig. 48b

After that the Spirit of God had moved upon the waters, and light was created, God said, *Let there be a Firmament in the midst of the waters,* *and let it separate the waters from the waters: *that is, those waters which by rarefaction and evaporation were ascended, and those of the Earth and Sea.

## p00839 · sig. 48b

But these waters separate above this extension, which the Latine Translation calls firmamentum, for expansum ("expanse")( for So *〈◊ 〉, Pagninus*, and Junius *Iunius* turn it) are not the crystalline Heavens, created in the imaginations of men; which opinion Basil the Great *Basilius Magnus* calls a childish supposition, making in the same place many learned arguments against this fancy. For the waters above the Firmament, are the waters in the Air above us, where the same is more solid and condense, which God separated from the nether waters by a Firmament, that is, by an extended distance and vast space: the words Raqia *Raquia*, which Montanus writes Rakiagh, and Shamayim *Shamaijm*, being indifferently taken for the Heaven & for Air, and more properly for the Air & ether, then for the Heavens, as the best Hebraists *Haebricians* understand them, *quo suprema actenuia* ("by which the highest and most tenuous [things]") *ab infimis crassis diducta, interfectaque distarent, *for that *whereby the supreme and thin bodies were placed in distance, being severed and cut off from low and gross matters: *and the waters above the Firmament, expressed in the word Maijm, are in that tongue taken properly for the waters above the Air, or in the uppermost Region of the same.

## p00840 · sig. 48b

And that the word Heaven is used for the Air, the Scriptures every-where witness, as in the blessings of Joseph *Ioseph*, and in the 104. Psalm. * *By these springs shall the* *fowl of the Heaven dwell*; and a *upon Sodom and 〈◊ 〉 it rained brimstone and fire out* *of the Heaven*; and in Isaac's *Isaacs* blessing to Jacob *IACOB*, *God give thee therefore of the dew of Heaven*; and in 〈◊ 〉 the 11. *But the land whither you go to possess it, is a land,* *that drinks water of the rain of Heaven*; and in JOB *IOB*, *Who has engendered the frosts of* Heaven; and in S. MATTHEW *MATTHEVV*, *Behold the Fowls* *Fowles** of Heaven, for they sow not.* So as in all the Scriptures of the old Testament throughout, is the word Heaven very often used for air, and taken also hyperbolically for any great height, as, Let us *build us a Tower, whose top may reach to Heaven, &c. *And in this very place Basil avouches, that this appellation of Heaven for the Firmament, is but by way of similitude: his own words be these; *Et vocavit Deus firmamentum calum. 〈◊ 〉 appellatio alij quidem proprie accommodatur, huic autem nunc ad similitudinem; And God called the firmament Heaven: This appellation* ("And God called the firmament Heaven. [illegible] This name is indeed properly applied to something else, but here it is now applied by way of likeness.")( says *Basil) is properly applied to another*( that is, to the Starry Heaven) *but to this*, ( that is, to the Firmament dividing the waters) *it is imposed by similitude*: and if there were no other proof, that by the Firmament was meant the Air, and not the Heaven, the words of MOSES in the eighth Verse, conferred with the same word Firmament in the twentieth Verse, makes it manifest: for in the eighth Verse it is written, that God called the Firmament, which divided waters from waters, Heaven; and in the 20. Verse he calls the Firmament of Heaven, Air, in these words: *And let the foul 〈◊ 〉 upon the earth in the open firmament of Heaven: *and what use there should be of this icy, or crystalline, or watery Heaven, I conceive not, except it be to moderate and temper the heat, which the Primum mobile would otherwise gather and increase: though in very truth, in stead of this help, it would ad an immeasurable greatness of circle, whereby the swiftness of that first Movable would exceed all possibility of belief. *〈◊ 〉 tenetur adimpossibilia; but no man ought to be held to impossibilities*; and faith it self( which surmounts the height of all humane reason) has for a forcible Conductor, the Word of Truth, which also may be called *lumen omnis 〈◊ 〉 & intellectus; the light of all reason and understanding. *Now that this supposed first Movable, turns it self So many hundred thousand miles in an instant( seeing the Scriptures teach it not) let those that can believe men's imaginations, apprehend it, for I can not: but of these many Heavens, let the Reader that desires satisfaction, search Orontius, and of this watery Heaven, Basil the Great in his Hexaemeron *Hexam*. fol. 40. 41. & c. and Matth. Beroaldo *Beroaldus*, his second Book, and sixth *sixt* Chapter. For my self, I am persuaded, that the waters, called, The waters above the Heavens, are but the clouds and waters engendered in the uppermost Air.
