## p03503 · sig. 400a

§. VI. *The end of *CYRUS *CYRVS*.

## p03504 · sig. 400a

The last war, and the end of this great King Cyrus, is diversely written. Herodotus and Justin *Iustine* deliver, That after the Conquest of Asia the less Cyrus invaded the Massagetae *Massagetes*, a very warlike Nation of the Scythians, governed by Tomyris their Queen: and that in an encounter between the Persians and these Northern *Nomads* *Nomades**, Tomyris *lost 〈◊ 〉 Army, and her Son Spargapises, that commanded it: In revenge whereof, this Queen making new levies of men of war, and following the war against Cyrus, in a second battle beat the Persian army, and taking Cyrus prisoner, cut off his head from his body, and cast the same into a bowl of blood, using these words; *Thou that hast all thy life time thirsted for blood, now drink thy fill, and satiate* thy self.

## p03505 · sig. 400a

It should hereby seem, that Cyrus knowing the strength and multitude of those frozen Nations, was persuaded to abate their fury by some forcible invasion and depopulation, because in the time of Cyaxares *Syaxares*, father to Astyages, those Scythians invaded Media and Asia the less, and held the same in a servile subjection eight and twenty years.

## p03506 · sig. 400a

This war which Metasthenes calls Tomyrique, lasted( says he) six years, and took end at the death of Cyrus.

## p03507 · sig. 400a

But in this particular I 〈◊ 〉 with Viginer, that this Scythian war was rather the same which Cyrus made against the Sacians, before the Conquest of Lydia, according to Ctesias before cited, who calls *Tomyris, Sparetha*, though he deliver the success of that war otherwise then Herodotus does: The rather( says Viginer) because Strabo in his eleventh book recites, That Cyrus surprised the Sacians by the same stratagem by which Justin says, 〈◊ 〉 defeated the son of Tomyris. And the same Ctesias also reports, That the last war which Cyrus made was against 〈◊ 〉, King of the Derbices *Derbicians* a Nation( as the rest) of 〈◊ 〉; whom though he overcame, yet he then received the wound of his death, which he suffered three days after.

## p03508 · sig. 400a

Strabo also affirms, That he was buried in his own City of Pasargadae *Pasagardes*, which himself had built, and where his Epitaph was to be read in his time; which is said to have been this: *O vir quicunque es, & 〈◊ 〉 advenis, neque enim te adventurum ignor avi: Ego sum* ("O man, whoever you are, and [illegible] you come, for I did not ignore that you would come: I am…")* *CYRUS *qui 〈◊ 〉 imperium constitui, pusillum 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 quo meum 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 mihine inuideas; O thou man, whosoever thou art, or whencesoever thou come; for I was not ignorant that thou should come: I am *CYRUS *that founded the Persian Empire, do not envy unto me this little earth, with which my body is covered.*

## p03509 · sig. 400b

This Tomb was opened by Alexander, as Qu. 〈◊ 〉 reports, either upon hope of treasure, supposed to have been buried with him, or upon desire to honour his dead body with certain ceremonies; in which there was found an old rotten Target, two Scythian Bows *Bowes*, and a Sword. The Coffin wherein his body lay, Alexander caused to be covered with his own garment, and a Crown of gold to be set upon it. These things well considered, as they give credit to the reports of Xenophon and Zonaras, So they derogate much from Herodotus, who leaves his body in the hands of Tomyris.

## p03510 · sig. 400b

And surely, had Cyrus lost the Army of Persia in Scythia, it is not likely, that his son would So soon have transported all his remaining forces into Egypt *Aegypt*, So far off from that quarter: the Scythian Nation then victorious, and bordering Media; neither had Cambyses been able in such haste to have undertaken and performed So great a Conquest. Wherefore I rather believe Xenophon, saying, That Cyrus died aged and in peace: and that finding in himself, that he could not long enjoy the World, he called unto him his Nobility, with his two sons, Cambyses and Smerdis; or after *Xenophon, Tanaoxares*: and after a long Oration, wherein he assured himself, and taught others, of the immortality of the Soul, and of the punishments and rewards following the good and ill deserving of every man in this life; he exhorted his sons by the strongest arguments he had, to a perpetual concord and agreement. Many other things he uttered, which make it probable, that he received the knowledge of the true God from Daniel, when he governed Susa in Persia; and that Cyrus himself had read the prophecy of Isaiah *Esay*, wherein he was expressly named, and by God( for the 〈◊ 〉 of his people) preordained. Which act of delivering the Jews *Iewes* from their 〈◊ 〉, and of restoring the holy Temple and City of Jerusalem *Hierusalem*, was in true consideration the noblest work that ever Cyrus performed. For in other actions he was an instrument of Gods power, used for the chastising of many Nations, and the establishing of a Government in those parts of the World, which was not long to continue. But herein he had the grace to be an instrument of Gods goodness, and a willing advancer of his Kingdom upon earth; which must last for ever, though heaven and earth shall perish.
