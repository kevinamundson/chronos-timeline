§. X. *That Nature is no *Principium per se; *nor form the giver of being: and of our ignorance, how second causes should have any proportion with their effects.*

## p00844 · sig. 49b

AND for this working power, which we call Nature, the beginning of motion and rest, according to Aristotle, the same is nothing else, but the strength and faculty, which God has infused into every creature, having no other self-ability, then a Clock, after it is wound up by a mans hand, has. These therefore that attribute unto this 〈◊ 〉, any first or sole power, have therein no other understanding, then such a one has, who looking into the Stern of a Ship, and finding it guided by the Helm and Rudder, does ascribe some absolute virtue to the piece of wood, without all consideration of the hand, that guides it, or of the judgement, which also directs and commands that hand; forgetting in this and in all else, that by the virtue of the first act, all Agents work whatsoever they work: *Virtute primi actus agunt agentia omnia quicquid agunt* ("By the power of the first act, all agents do whatever they do.")*: *for as the mind of man sees by the Organ of the eye, hears by the ears, and makes choice by the will: and therefore we attribute sight to the eye, and hearing to the ears, &c. and yet it is the mind only, that gives ability, life, and motion to all these his instruments and Organs; So God works by Angels, by the Sun, by the Stars *Starres*, by Nature, or infused properties, and by men, as by several organs, several effects; all second causes whatsoever being but instruments, conduits, and pipes, which carry and disperse what they have received from the head and fountain of the Universal. For as it is Gods infinite power, and every-where-presence( compassing, embracing, and piercing all things) that gives to the Sun power to draw up vapours, to vapours to be made clouds, clouds to contain rain, and rain to fall: So all second and instrumental causes, together with Nature it self, without that operative faculty which God gave them, would become altogether silent, virtueless, and dead: of which excellently ORPHEVS; *Per te virescunt* *omnia, All things by thee spring forth in youthful green. *I enforce not these things, thereby to annihilate those variable virtues, which God has given to his creatures, animate and inanimate, to heavenly and earthly bodies, &c. for all his works in their virtues prays him: but of the manner how God works in them, or they in or with each other, which the Heathen Philosophers, and those that follow them, have taken on them to teach: I say, there is not any one among them, nor any one among us, that could ever yet conceive it, or express it, ever enrich his own understanding with any certain truth, or ever edify others( not foolish by self-flattery) therein. For( says Lactantius, speaking of the wisdom of the Philosophers) *si facultas inueniendae veritatis huic studio subiaceret, aliquando esset innenta; cum vero* ("If the faculty of finding truth were subject to this study, it would at some time have been found; but whereas…") *tot temporibus, tot ingenijs in eius inquisitione contritis, non sit comprehensa, apparet nullam ibiesse sapientiam; If in this study*( says he) *were means to find out the truth, it had ere this been found out: but seeing it is not yet comprehended, after that So much time,* *and So many wits have been worn out in the inquiry of it, it appears, that there is no wisdom there to be had. Nam si de unare praecisa scientia haberetur, omnium rerum scientia necessario haberetur: If the precise knowledge of any one thing were to be had, it should necessarily follow, that the knowledge of all things were to be had. *And as the Philosophers were ignorant in Nature, and the ways of her working: So were they more curious, then knowing, in their first matter and Physical form. For if their first matter had any being, it were not then the first matter: for, as it is the first matter, it has only a power of being, which it altogether leaves, when it does subsist. And seeing it is neither a substance perfect, nor a substance inchoate, or in the way of perfection, how any other substance should thence take concrescence, it has not been 〈◊ 〉 neither are these forms( says a learned Author) anything, *si ex ea exprimantur potentia, quae nihil est* ("if they are drawn forth from that power than which there is nothing [greater].")*. *Again, how this first matter should be subiectum formarum ("the subject of forms"), and passive, which is understood to precede the form, it is hard to conceive: for to make form which is the cause, to be subsequent to the thing caused( to wit, to the first matter) is contrary to all reason, divine and humane: only it may be said, that originally there is no other difference between matter & form, then between heat and fire, of which the one cannot subsist without the other, but in a kind of rational consideration. Leaving therefore these Riddles to their Lovers *Louers*, who by certain scholastic distinctions wrest and 〈◊ 〉 the truth of all things, and by which Aristotle has laboured to prove a false eternity of the World, I think it far safer to affirm with Saint AVGUSTINE *AVGVSTINE*, *That all species and kinds are from God, from whom,* *whatsoever is natural proceeds, of what kind or estimation soever, from whence are the seeds of all forms, and the forms of all seeds and their motions; A quo est omnis species, a quo est quicquid naturaliter est, cuiuscunque generis est, 〈◊ 〉 aestimationis est, a quo sunt semina formarum, formae seminum, motus seminum atg formarum. *And thus much Averroes *Auerrois* is forced to confess. *For all forms*( says he) *are in primo motore*; which is also the opinion of Aristotle in the twelfth of his Metaphysics. and of Albertus upon Dionysius.
