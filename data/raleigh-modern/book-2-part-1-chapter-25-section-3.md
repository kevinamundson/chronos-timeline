§. III. Of 〈◊ 〉 his sickness and recovery; and of the Babylonian King that congratulated him.

## p03143 · sig. 352b

After this marvellous delivery, Hezekiah *Ezekias* sickened, and was told by Isaiah, that he must die: but after he had besought God with tears for his delivery, Isaiah, as he was going from him, returned again, and had warrant from the Spirit of God to promise him recovery after three days, and a prolongation of his life for fifteen years. But Hezekiah somewhat doubtful of this exceeding grace, prays a sign to confirm him: whereupon, at the prayer of Isaiah, the shadow of the Sun cast it self the contrary way, and went back ten degrees, upon the Dial of Ahaz *Achaz*. The cause that moved Hezekiah to lament( says Saint Jerome *Hierome*) was, because he had as yet no son, and then in despair that the Messias should come out of the house of David *Dauid*, or at least of his Seed. His disease seems to be the Pestilence, by the medicine given him by the Prophet, to wit, a mass of Figs *Figges*, laid to the Botch or Sore.

## p03144 · sig. 352b

This wonder when the Wise-men of Chaldea *Chaldaea* had told to Merodach, King of Babylon, the first of that house, he sent to Hezekiah, to be informed of the cause: at which time Hezekiah showed him all the Treasure he had, both in the Court and in the Kingdom: for which he was reprehended by the Prophet Isaiah, who told him; *The days are at hand, that all that is in thine house, and whatsoever thy fathers have laid* *up in store to this day, shall be carried into Babel*; nothing shall be left, says the Lord. It may seem strange, how Hezekiah should have got any treasure worth the showing: for Sennacherib *Senacherib* had robbed him of all, the year before. But the spoil of the same Sennacherib his Camp repaid all with advantage, and made 〈◊ 〉 richer upon the sudden then ever he had been: which unexpected wealth was a strong temptation to boasting. After this time Hezekiah had rest, and spending without noise that addition which God had made unto his life, he died, having reigned nine and twenty years. One only offensive War he made, which was against the Philistines *Philistims* with good success. Among his other acts( shortly remembered in Ecclesiasticus) he devised to bring water to Jerusalem *Ierusalem*.

## p03145 · sig. 352b

In two respects they say that he offended God: the one, that he rejoiced too much at the destruction and lamentable end of his enemy; the other, that he So much gloried in his riches, as he could not forbear to show them to strangers. But the reason which moved Hezekiah( speaking humanely) to entertain the Ambassadors *Embassadors* of Merodach in this friendly and familiar manner, was, because he came to visit him, and brought him a present, congratulating the recovery of his health; as also in that Merodach had weakened the house of Sennacherib, his fearful enemy. For Merodach, who was Commander and Lieutenant under Sennacherib in Babylon, usurped that State himself, in the last year of that King, and held it by strong hand against his son Assarhaddon; who was not only simple, but impaired in strength, by the molestation of his brothers. This advantage Merodach espied, and remembering, that their ancestor Pul *Phul* Belochus had set his own master Sardanapalus besides the cushion, thought it as lawful for himself to take the opportunity which this Kings weakness did offer, as it had been for Belochus to make use of the others 〈◊ 〉: and So, finding himself beloved of the Babylonians, and sufficiently powerful, he did put the matter to hazard, and prevailed. The assertion of this history is made by the same arguments that were used in maintaining the common opinion of Writers, touching Pul Belochus; which I will not here again rehearse. So of this new Race, which cut a-sunder the Line of Ninus, there were only five Kings.

But inasmuch as the last year of Shalmaneser was also the first of Sennacherib his son, we reckon the time, wherein the house of Pul held the Assyrian Kingdom, to have been an hundred and one years, of which, the last five and twenty were spent with Hezekiah, under *Shalmaneser, Sennacherib*, and Assarhaddon.
