§. IX. *The difficulties which the Greek* *Greeke** Army found in passing through the Land of the Carduchi.*

## p03799 · sig. 441b

Entering upon the Land of the Carduchi, they were encountered with many difficulties of ways, but much more afflicted by the fierce Inhabitants, who, accustomed by force to defend themselves against the huge Armies of the Persian, were no way inferior to the Greeks *Greekes* in daring, but only in the Art of war. They were very light of foot, skillful Archers; and used the Sling well; which weapons in that mountainous Country were of much use against these poor travellers, afflicting them in seven days which they spent in that passage, far more than all the power of the great King had done. Between the Territory of these Carduchi, and the parts of Armenia confining them, ran Centrites a great River, upon which the Greeks refreshed themselves one day, rejoicing that they had So well escaped these dangers, and hoping that the remainder would prove easy. But the next morning they saw certain troupes of Horse, that lay to forbid their passage. These were levied by the Kings Deputies in those parts; Tissaphernes and his Companies having taken their way towards Ionia. The River was broad and deep, So that it was not possible for such as would enter it, to make resistance against those which kept the opposite banks. To increase these dangers the Carduchi following upon them, lay on the side of a Mountain, within less than a mile of the water. But it was their good hap to discover a Ford, by which the greater number of them passing over, did easily chase away the Subjects *Subiects* of the Persian, and then sending back the most expedite men, gave succour to the Rear-ward *Reare-ward*, against which the Carduchi being slightly armed, could not on plain ground make resistance hand to hand. These Carduchi seem to have inhabited the Mountains *Mountaines* of Niphates, which are not far from the Spring of Tigris *Tygris*; though Ptolemy *Ptolomie* place them far more to the East upon the River of Cyrus in Media, wherein he differs much from Xenophon, whose relation being grounded upon his own knowledge, does best in this case deserve credit. Of the River Centrites( as of many other Rivers *Riuers*, Towns *Townes*, and Places, mentioned by Xenophon) I will not labour to make a conjecture, which may endure the severity of a Critic. For Ptolemy, and the whole Nation of Geographers, ad small light to this expedition: only of this last, I think it the same which falls into Tigris, not much above Artasigarta springing out of Niphates, and running by the Town of Sardeva *Sardeua* in Gordyene *Gordene*, a Province of Armenia the Great, wherein the Greeks having passed Centrites did arrive.
