§. X. That the seat of CUSH *CHVSH* the eldest son of HAM, was in Arabia, not in Ethiopia *Aethiopia*: and of strange Fables, and 〈◊ 〉 Translations of Scripture, grounded upon the mistaking of this point.

## p01265 · sig. 121b

†. I. Of JOSEPHUS *IOSEPHVS* his Tale of an Aethiopesse wife to MOSES, grounded on the mistaking of the seat of CUSH.

## p01266 · sig. 121b

That Ham was the Father of the Egyptians, it is made manifest in many Scriptures, as in the 105. Psalm verse 51. Then ISRAEL *came to Egypt, and *Jacob *IACOB* *was a stranger in the land of *HAM: and in the 78. *Psalm, He slew all the first-borne in Egypt, even the beginning of their strength, in the Tabernacles of *HAM. There is also found a great City in Thebaida, called Cheramis: ( as it were the City of Ham) of which name 〈◊ 〉 also discovers an Island in the same Region. But because Cush is the elder son of Ham, it agrees with order to speak first of him. Now though I have already in the description of Paradise handled this question, and( I hope) proved that Cush could not be Ethiopia: yet seeing it comes now to his turn to speak for himself, I will ad some farther proof to the former. For the manifestation hereof sets many things straight, which had otherwise very crooked constructions, and senseless Interpretations. Surely, how soever the Septuagint and Josephus have herein failed, yet it is manifest that Cush could not be Ethiopia, but Arabia: ( to wit) both that Arabia called Petraea, and a part of Arabia the Happy and the Desert: which Regions Cush and the Cushites *Chusites* presently planted, after they left Babylonia to Nimrod, wherein they first sate down altogether. And there is nothing which So well clears this Controversy, as the true interpretation of the place, Num. 12. v. 1. where Moses his Wife is called a Chusite; together with some places which speak of Nebuchadnezzar's *Nabuchodonosors* Conquests. For whereas Josephus and the Septuagint in the place, Num. 12. v. 1. as also elsewhere, understand Cush for Ethiopia, we must give credit to Moses himself herein; and then it will appear that Josephus was grossly mistaken, or vainly led by his own invention. For Josephus presuming that Cush was Ethiopia, and therefore that the Wise of Moses( which in Scripture, Num. 12. verse 1. is called a woman of Cush) was a woman of the land of Ethiopia, feigns that Tharbis the Daughter of the King of Ethiopia, fell in love with the person and fame of Moses, while he besieged Sheba *Saba* her Fathers City; and to the end, to obtain Moses for her Husband, she practised to betray both her Parents, Country, and friends, with the City it self, and to deliver it into Moses hands. The Tale( if it be worth the reciting) lies thus in Josephus. After he had described the strength of the Ethiopian *Aethiopian* City Meroe, which he says at length Cambyses called So from the name of his Sister, ( the old name being Sheba) he goes on in these words: Hic cum ("Here, when [he];") MOSES desidere *exercitum 〈◊ 〉 aegre ferret, hoste non audente manus 〈◊ 〉, tale quiddam accidit. Erat 〈◊ 〉 Regi 〈◊ 〉, nomine* ("The army [illegible] bore with difficulty, the enemy not daring to join battle [illegible], something of this sort happened. There was [illegible] to the king [illegible], named…")* *THARBIS, & c. which Tale has this sense in English: When MOSES *was 〈◊ 〉 that his Army lay idle, because the Enemy besieged, durst not sally and come to handy strokes, there happened this accident in the mean while. The Ethiopian King had a Daughter called *THARBIS, *who at some 〈◊ 〉 given beheld the person of *MOSES, *and withal admired his valour. And knowing that *MOSES *had not only up-held and restored the falling estate of the Egyptians, but had also brought the conquering 〈◊ 〉 to the very brink of subversion: these things working in her thoughts, together with her own affection, which daily increased, she made means to send unto him by one of her trustiest servants to offer her self unto him, and become his Wife*; Which MOSES *on this condition entertained, that she should first deliver the City into his possession: where unto she 〈◊ 〉, and *MOSES *having taken oath to perform 〈◊ 〉 contract, both the one and the other were instantly performed.*

## p01267 · sig. 122a

†. II. A dispute against the Tale of JOSEPHUS.

## p01268 · sig. 122a

This Tale( whereof Moses has not a word) has Josephus fashioned, and therein also utterly mistaken himself, in naming a City of Arabia for a City of Ethiopia: as he names Ethiopia itself to have been the Country of Moses his Wife, when( indeed) it was Arabia. For Sheba is not in Ethiopia, but in Arabia, as both Strabo and all other Geographers, ancient and modern teach us, saying that the Sabaeans are Arabians and not Ethiopians *Aethiopians*; except Josephus can persuade us, that the Queen of Sheba which came from the South to hear the wisdom of Solomon *Salomon*, were a Negro, or Black-Moore *Blacke-Moore*. And though *Damianus a Goes *speak of certain Letters to the King of Portugal from Prester John *Iohn*, of the Abyssinians *Abissines*: wherein that Ethiopian King would persuade the Portugals that he was descended of the Queen of Sheba, and of Solomon; yet it does no where appear in the Scriptures, that Solomon had any Son by that great Princess: which had it been true, it is likely that when Shishak *Sishac* King of Egypt invaded Roboam, and sacked Jerusalem *Hierusalem*, his Brother( the Son of Sheba and Solomon) who joined upon Egypt, would both have impeached that enterprise, as also given aide and succour to Roboam against Jeroboam *Ieroboam*, who drew from him ten of the twelve Tribes to his own obedience. Neither is it any thing against our opinion of Moses his Wife, to have been an Arabian, that the Scriptures teach us, that Moses married the daughter of Jethro Priest of Midian or Midian *Madian*: which standing on the North Coast of the Red Sea, over against the body of Egypt, and near Esion Gaber, where Solomon provided his Fleet for India, in the Region of Edom, may well be reckoned as a part of Arabia, as the Red Sea is called Sinus Arabicus. For Idumea *Edumaea* joins to the Tribe of Judah *Iuda* by the North, to Arabia Petraea by the East, to the Mediterranean *Mediterran* by the West, and to the Red Sea by the Southeast. And if we mark the way which Moses took when he left Egypt, and conducted Israel thence, it will appear that he was no stranger in Arabia: in the border whereof, and in Arabia itself, he had formerly lived forty years; where it seems, that besides his careful bringing up in Egypt, he was instructed by Jethro in the Egyptians learning. For Josephus confesses, and Saint Stephen confirms, that he was learned in all the wisdom of the Egyptians. But on the other side this Text makes much against Josephus, where it is written in Exodus the second, Therefore MOSES fled from Pharaoh *PHARAO*, *and dwelt in the Land of Midian *or Midian, and not in Ethiopia. And in the third Chapter it is as plain as words can express, in what Region Midian was, where it is written, When MOSES *kept the sheep of *JETHRO *his Father in Law, Priest of Midian, and drove the Flock to the Desert, and came to the Mountain of *GOD in 〈◊ 〉. Now that Mount Horeb is not in Ethiopia, every Infant knows. And if we may believe Moses himself, then was not the Wife of Moses purchased in that manner which Josephus reports( which was for betraying her Country and Friends) neither had she the name of Tharbis, but of Zipporah *Sippora*, or Zippora: neither was she a Negro, but a Madianitish. And as God works the greatest things by the simplest means: So it pleased him from a Shepherd to call Moses, and after him David *Dauid*, and by them to deliver his people first and last. For Moses sitting by a Well( as disconsolate and a stranger) defended the daughters of Reuel *Reguel* from the other shepherds, and drew them Water to water their sheep: upon which occasion( by God ordained) he was entertained by 〈◊ 〉, whose Daughter he married: and not for any betraying of Towns *Townes* or Countries.

## p01269 · sig. 122b

From hence also came Jethro to Moses at 〈◊ 〉, not far from Idumea, and finding the insupportable government of such a multitude, he advised him to distribute this weighty charge, and to make Governors *Gouernours* and Judges *Iudges* of every Tribe and Family. And if Jethro had been an Ethiopian, it had been a far progress for him to have passed through all Egypt with the Wife and Children of Moses, and to have found Moses in the border of 〈◊ 〉: the Egyptians hating Moses and all that favoured him. But the passing of Moses through Arabia Petraea( which joins to Midian) proves that Moses was well acquainted in those parts: in which the second time he wandered forty years, and did by these late travails of his, seek to instruct the children of Israel in the knowledge of one true God, before he brought them to the Land of plenty and rest. For he found them nourished up with the milk of Idolatry, and obstinate in the Religion of the Heathen, and finding that those stiff plants could not be bowed or declined, either by persuasion or by miracle, he ware them out in the Deserts *Desarts*, as God directed, and grafted their branches a new, that from those he might receive fruit, agreeable to his own desire, and Gods Commandments *Commandements*.

## p01270 · sig. 122b

Lastly, this opinion of Josephus is condemned by Augustinus Chrisamensis, where also he reprehends Apollinaris, who avowed that Moses had married both Tharbis and Sephora: His own words have this beginning: Mentitur ettam APOLLINARIS *duas uxores habuisse* ("to have had two wives")* *MOSEN, &c. APOLLINARIS *also lies in affirming that *MOSES *had two wives: and who does not perceive these things feigned by them? for it is* *〈◊ 〉 that the wife of *MOSES was Zipporah, *Daughter to the Priest or President of Midian: and that Midian cannot be taken for Ethiopia beyond Egypt; being the same that joins to Arabia: *So far Chrisamensis.

## p01271 · sig. 122b

†. III. Cush ill expounded for Ethiopia, *EZECH. 29. 10.*

## p01272 · sig. 122b

Now as Cush is by the Septuagint converted Ethiopia, and the wife of Moses 〈◊ 〉 called Aethiopissa: So in the conquest of Nebuchadnezzar *Nabuchodonosor* is Ethiopia written for Arabia. For by the words of Ezekiel *Ezechiel* it is manifest that Nebuchadnezzar was never in *Ethiopia. Behold*( says Ezekiel, speaking of the person of this great *Assyrian) I come upon thee and upon thy Rivers* *Riuers**, and I will make the Land of Egypt utterly waste and desolate, from the Tower of Seveneh* *Seueneh**, even to the borders of the Black-moors* *Black-moores**: *which last words should have been thus converted: *From the Tower of Seveneh to the borders of the Cushites *or Arabians: between which two is situated all Egypt. For to say, from the borders of Seveneh to the Ethiopians, has no sense at all. Seveneh itself being the border of Egypt, confronting and joining to Ethiopia, or the Land of the black-Moors. So as if Nebuchadnezzar's conquest had been but between Seveneh and the border of Ethiopia, it were as much to say, and did express no other victory then the conquest of all that Land and Country, lying between Middlesex and Buckingham, where both the Countries join together; or all the North parts of England, between Berwick *Barwick* and Scotland: for this has the same sense with the former, if any man sought to express by these two bounds, the Conquest of *England: Berwick *being the North border of England, as Seveneh or Syene is the South bound of Egypt *Aegypt*, seated in Thebaida which touches Ethiopia. But by the words of Ezekiel it appears, that Nebuchadnezzar never entered into any part of Ethiopia, although the Septuagint, the Vulgar, the Geneva, and all other( in effect) have written Ethiopia for Cush.

## p01273 · sig. 123a

†. IIII. Another place of Ezekiel, cap. 30. verse. 9. in like manner mistaken.

## p01274 · sig. 123a

And as the former, So is this place of Ezekiel mistaken, by being in this sort converted: *In dieilla 〈◊ 〉 nuncij a facy mea in trieribus ad 〈◊ 〉 Aethiopiae confidentiam* ("In that day [illegible] messengers from my face in ships to [illegible] the confidence of Ethiopia:")*: *Which place is thus turned in English by the *Genevans* *Geneuans**: In that day shall there Messengers go forth from me in ships to make the careless Moors afraid. *Now the Latine for( ships) has the Greek *Greeke* word Triers *Trieres* for Triremes which are Galleys *Gallyes* of three banks, and not ships. But that in this place the Translation should have been( as in the former) amended by using the word Cush, or Arabia for Ethiopia or the black Moors, every man may see which meanly understands the Geography of the World, knowing, that to passe out of Egypt into Ethiopia there need no Galleys nor ships, no more than to passe out of Northampton into *Leicestershire: Ethiopia* being the conterminate Region with Egypt, and not divided So much as by a River. Therefore in this place of Ezekiel it was meant, that from Egypt Nebuchadnezzar should send Galleys along the coast of the Red Sea, by which an Army might be transported into Arabia the Happy and the Stony( sparing the long wearisome march over all Egypt, and the Deserts of Paran *Pharan*) which Army might thereby surprise them unawares in their security and confidence. For when Nebuchadnezzar was at Seveneh within a mile of Ethiopia, he needed neither Galley nor Ship to passe into it: being all one large and firm Land with Egypt, and no otherwise parted from it, then one In-land shire is parted from another; and if he had a fancy to have rowed up the River but for pleasure, he could not have done it: for the fall of Nile *Nilus*( tumbling over high and steep Mountains *Mountaynes*) called Catadupae Nili, were at hand.

Lastly, as I have already observed, the sons of every father seated themselves as near together as possibly they could, Gomer and his sons in Asia the less; Javan *Iauan* and his sons in Greece, and the Islands *Ilands* adjoining; Shem in Persia and Eastward. So the Sons and Grand-children of Cush from the River of Gihon *Gehon*( their Fathers first seat) inhabited upon the same, or upon some other contiguous unto it, as Nimrod and 〈◊ 〉 on the one side, and *Sheba, Sheba*, and Sabtecha( with the rest) did on the other side. And to conclude in a word, the Hebrews *Hebrewes* had never any acquaintance or fellowship, any war, treaty of peace, or other intelligence with the Ethiopian black Moors, as is already remembered in the Chapter of Paradise.

## p01276 · sig. 123a

†. V. A place, Isaiah *ESAY* 18. v. 1. in like manner corrupted, by taking Cush for Ethiopia.

## p01277 · sig. 123a

And as in these places before remembered, So in divers other is the word Ethiopia put for Arabia or Cush, which puts the story( where it is So understood) quite out of square; one Kingdom thereby being taken for another. For what sense has this part of Scripture Isaiah 18. *Vae terrae Cymbalorum alarum quae est trans flumina Aethiopiae* ("Woe to the land of the whirring of wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia,")*, *or according to the Septuagint in these words: *Vae terrae navium alarum 〈◊ 〉 est trans 〈◊ 〉 Aethiopiae? Woe to the land shadowing with wings, which is beyond the Rivers of Ethiopia, sending Ambassadors by sea, even in vessels of reeds upon the waters. Vae terrae 〈◊ 〉; Woe to the Land of the shady coast, *says Junius *Iunius*. The former Translators understand it in this sense. That the waters are shadowed with the sails, which are significatively called the wings of the ships, the other, that the Coast of the Sea was shadowed by the height of the Land.

## p01278 · sig. 123b

But to the purpose: That this Land here spoken of by the Prophet Isaiah, is Egypt, no Interpreter has doubted. For they were the Egyptians that sent this message to the Israelites which Isaiah repeats, and by the former translation every man may see the transposition of Kingdoms *Kingdomes*: for hereby Egypt is transported unto the other side of Ethiopia, and Ethiopia set next unto Judea *Iudaea*, when it is the Land of Cush and Arabia indeed that lies between 〈◊ 〉 and Egypt, and not Ethiopia, which is seated under the Equinoctial line. And of this Beroaldo *Beroaldus* asks a material question, ( to wit) what Region that should be, of which the Prophet speaks, and places it beyond the Rivers of Ethiopia: *Nam de ignotaagi regione dici nequit; For it cannot be said that he treats of an unknown Region. *Now if Ethiopia itself be under the Equinoctial line, with whom the Jews *Iewes* had never any acquaintance, why should any man dream that they had knowledge of Nations far beyond it again, and beyond the Rivers of Ethiopia? except we shall impiously think that the Prophet spoke he knew not what, or used an impertinent discourse of those Nations, which were not discovered in 2000. years after, inhabiting as far South as the *Cape of good Hope*, commonly known by the name of Bona esperanza.

## p01279 · sig. 123b

†. VI. That upon the like mistaking, both Tirhakah *TERRHAKA* in the story of Sennacherib *SENACHERIB*, and ZERA in the story of ASA are unadvisedly made Ethiopians.

## p01280 · sig. 123b

And by this translation is the story of Sennacherib utterly mistaken in the cause of his retreat. For Sennacherib was first repulsed at Pelusium, at the very entrance of Egypt from Judea: when having certain knowledge that 〈◊ 〉, ( which all the Interpreters call King of Ethiopia) was on the way to set on him, he began to with-draw himself: and fearing to leave his Army in two parts, he sent threatening Messengers to Ezechia King of Judah, persuading him to submit himself: the Tenor whereof is set down in the second of Kings in these words: *Have any of the gods of the Nations delivered his Land out of the hands of the King of 〈◊ 〉? Where is the god of Hamah? &c. *By which proud Embassy, if he had obtained entrance into Jerusalem, he then meant to have united that great Army before Jerusalem, commanded by Rabshakeh *Rabsekeh*, with the other which lay before Pelusium, a great City upon the branch of Nile next Arabia. For Sennacherib had already mastered the most part of all those Cities in Judea and Benjamin *Beniamin* with a third Army, ( which himself commanded) being then at the siege of 〈◊ 〉. But upon the rumor of that Arabian Army led by their King Thirrbakeh( whom Josephus calls 〈◊ 〉) Rabsakeh hasted from the siege of Jerusalem, and found Sennacherib departed from Lachish *Lachis* &amp; set down before Lebna, which was afterwards called Eleutheropolis *Eleuthoropolis*, as some have supposed. But while he had ill success at Pelusium and feared Thirrbakeh, God himself whom he least feared, struck his Army before Jerusalem by the Angel of his power, So as 185000. were found dead in the place, as in the life of Hezekiah *Ezechias* is hereafter more largely written. And that this Army of Tirhakah was from *Arabia, Josephus *himself makes it plain. For he confesses in the tenth Book the first Chapter of the Jews Antiquities, that it was come to Senacheribs knowledge, that the Army which was afoot ( both to relieve the Egyptians *Aegyptians* and the Jews) marched towards him by the way of the Desert: Now the Desert which lay indifferent between Jerusalem and 〈◊ 〉, was that of Paran or Sur, which also touches on the three Arabias *Arabiaes*, to wit, the 〈◊ 〉, of which it is a part: the Desert, and the Happy; and by no other way 〈◊ 〉 could the Arabians come on to succour either Pelusium or Jerusalem. But, that there is any Desert between Pelusium and the South part of Egypt, has never yet been heard of, or described by any Cosmographer or Historian. So then this Scripture of the second of Kings, Verse the ninth, has the same mistaking as the rest. For here the word( Cush) is also translated Ethiopia; and in this sense have all the Interpreters, ( but Junius) expressed the beginning of the ninth Verse: *He heard also men say of *THIRRHAKEH *King of Ethiopia, & c. *whereas it should have been thus converted with IVNIVS: *Audiens autem de* ("But hearing of [it];")* *THIRRHAKEH *Rege Chushi; He heard also of *THIRRHAKEH *King of the Cushites*. For they were the Cushites and Arabians, whose Houses and Cities were next the fire, and upon whom the very smoke of Judah flaming was blown, being their nearest Neighbours: and So were not the Ethiopian black-Moors under the Equinoctial, whom neither war nor peace( which discovers all Regions) ever found out, says 〈◊ 〉. For this King was no more King of Ethiopia then Zerah was, who invaded Asa King of Judah, with an Army of a Million and three thousand Charriots. Indeed, how such an Army and those Chariots *Charriouts* should passe through all Egypt, ( the Kings of Egypt being mighty Kings) let all men that know how these Regions are seated, and how far distant, judge. For Princes do not easily permit Armies of a Million to run through them; neither was there ever any such strength of Black-Moors heard of in that part of the World, or else where. Neither are these Ethiopians such travellers or Conquerors; and yet is this King Zerah also called King of Ethiopia. But the word Cush being first So converted for Ethiopia, the rest of the Interpreters( not looking into the seats of Kingdoms, or the possibilities of attempts, or invasions) followed one another in the former mistakes.

## p01281 · sig. 124a

†. VII. A farther exposition of the place, Isaiah 18. 1.

## p01282 · sig. 124a

Concerning these words in that eighteenth Chapter of Isaiah, Navium *Nauium* alarum; Winged ships, ( So the 〈◊ 〉 turn it) or Cymbalo alarum( according to the Latine) *sails whistling in the winds*, or *terr aevmbrosae orae*( after IVNIVS) *the Land of a shadowed coast, *or *the Land shadowing with wings*, as our English Geneva has it. The two first interpretations of the Septuagint and S. Jerome *Hierome* have one sense in effect. For the sails are commonly called the wings of a ship; and we use to say ordinarily when our ships sail slowly, that she wants wings: ( that is) when her sails are either worn or too narrow; and we also use the same phrase of the wind whistling in the sails. And it may be that the Egyptians employed So many of those small ships, as their sails were said to give a shadow over the Red Sea. But to make both interpretations good, Pintus( upon Isaiah) affirms, that the word( Sabal) does signify both to shadow and to jingle( which is) to make a kind of cymbal-like *Cymbaline* sound: So as the meaning of this place( says Pintus) is this: *Woe to thee, O Egypt, which do promise to others safeguard, under the shadow of thy wings, *which( indeed) seems to agree with the argument of the eighteenth Chapter of Isaiah: and this phrase is often else where used, as in the sixteenth Psalm: *Sub umbra alarum tuarum protege me; Defend me under the shadow of thy wings. *The Boats *Boates* of reed spoken of are of two kinds; either of basket-willow covered with hides( as anciently in 〈◊ 〉) or a Tree made hollow in the bottom, and built upon both sides with Canes. Of the one sort I have seen in Ireland, of the other in the Indies.
