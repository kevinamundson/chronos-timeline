§. II. A brief rehearsal of two opinions, touching the beginning of the Captivity *captiuitie*: with an answer to the cavils of PORPHYRIE, inveighing against S. MATTHEW, and DANIEL, upon whom the later of these opinions is founded.

## p03365 · sig. 382b

Many Commentators, and other Historians and Chronologers find, that the Captivity then began, when Jechonias *Iechonias* was carried prisoner into Babylon, eleven year before the final destruction of Jerusalem *Ierusalem* under Zedekias. This they prove out of divers places in Ezekiel, especially out of the fourteenth chapter, where he makes a plain distinction between the beginning of the Captivity, and utter destruction of Jerusalem by Nabuzaradan, in these words: *In the five and twentieth year of our being in Captivity, in the beginning of the year, in the tenth day of the month, in the fourteenth year after that the City was smitten. *In which words he begins the Captivity in plain terms, eleven years before the City was destroyed. Beroaldo *Beroaldus* is of opinion that it began in the first of Nebuchadnezzar *Nabuchodonosor*, and the fourth of Jehoiakim *Ioakim*, which he endeavours to prove out of the second of Chronicles, but more especially out S. Matthew, and Daniel, whose words afford matter of long disputation, but serve not to make good So much as Beroaldo would enforce. That place of S. Matthew, and the whole book of Daniel, have ministered occasion of scoffing and railing at the 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 to that wretched man Porphyrie, who, not understanding how the sons of King Josiah *Iosias* were called by divers names, as Epiphanius has showed at large, thought that the Apostle had spoken he knew not what in reckoning the sons, or, according to some translations, the Son and Nephews *Nephewes* of that good King, begotten about the time of the Captivity. Upon Daniel also the same Porphyrie does spend the twelfth of his malicious books written against the Christians, affirming that these prophecies & visions remembered by Daniel, were written long after his death, and at, or near the time of Antiochus Epiphanes. This fond supposition of his, *〈◊ 〉, Apollonius*, and others, have sufficiently answered. For the seventy Interpreters, who converted the old Testament about an hundred year before Epiphanes, did also turn this book of Daniel out of Hebrew into Greek *Greeke*, as a part of Scripture received. And were there no other argument to confound Porphyrie, than that of Alexander Macedon, it were sufficient, who lived divers years before Antiochus Epiphanes. For Jaddus *Iaddus* the high Priest showed that great Conqueror, when he came towards Jerusalem to have destroyed it, this book of Daniel, wherein he beheld his own glory foretold, as the same was plainly expounded unto him; which not only stayed his hand from the harm of that City and people, but his assurance and resolution was So confirmed and strengthened thereby, as despising all future peril and 〈◊ 〉, he conquered Darius, and the Eastern Empire in a shorter time than Nebuchadnezzar had done one City, to wit, Tyre in Phoenicia.

It is true indeed that the Jews *Iewes* them selves give less authority to Daniel, than to Moses, and the Prophets, accounting his book among those which they call Ketuvim *Cetaphim*, or Hagiographa, or holy Writings, which they say Ezra *Esdras* and the Seniors of the Synagogue compiled after their return from Babylon. But first, that the book of Daniel( I mean So much as is found in the Hebrew) is Canonical: secondly, that it was written by Daniel himself, and not by Ezra and the Seniors; we may assure our selves by testimony of Councels, and Fathers. For in the Council of 〈◊ 〉 held about the year of our Lord 368. after the death of Jovian *Iouinian* the Emperor, and after the Nicene Council three and forty years, this book of Daniel was received verified and confirmed among the other 〈◊ 〉 Scriptures, as in the Epitome of the same Council it may be seen, and So does Melito *Meliton* the most ancient Bishop of Sardis number it, witness Eusebius in his Ecclesiastical history, the fourth book, and five and twentieth chapter, So does the same 〈◊ 〉 in the Catalogue of Canonical books upon Origen, So does Hilarius in his Preface upon the 〈◊ 〉, and Epiphanius in his book of Weights *Waights* and 〈◊ 〉, &c. To these I may ad S. *Jerome* *Hierome**, Gregory* *Gregorie** Nazianzene*, and others. For the Hagiographa *Hagiographae* books or holy Writings, the 〈◊ 〉 and Rabbins *Rabbines* 〈◊ 〉 to be these, *Daniel, Psalms* *Psalmes**, Proverbs* *Prouerbs**, Job* *Iob**, Canticles, Ruth, 〈◊ 〉, Ecclesiastes, Hester, Ezra, Nehemiah, *and the Chronicles. And that it was Daniel, and not Ezra, that wrote this book, Gods commandment unto him by his Angel, to seal up the same to the time appointed, is an unanswerable testimony. Yea that which exceeds all strength of other proof, our Saviour Christ who cites no Apocryphal Scripture, in Matthew *Mathew* &amp; Mark alleges Daniel the Prophet, to wit, the last verse of his nineteenth chapter. Further, in the fifth *fift* of John *Iohn*, Christ distributes the risen from the dead, as in Daniel the twelfth, verse the second. S. Paul describes Antichrist out of Daniel, and the Revelation is wholly an interpretation of Daniels visions.
