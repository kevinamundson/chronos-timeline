§. VI. *How it is to be understood that the Spirit of God moved upon the waters, and that this is not to be searched curiously.*

## p00822 · sig. 46a

After the creation of Heaven and Earth, then void and without form, the Spirit of God moved upon the waters. The Seventy Interpreters use the word super-ferebatur, moved upon or over: incubabat, or 〈◊ 〉( says Jerome *Hierome*) out of Basil; and Basil out of a Syrian Doctor; 〈◊ 〉 *non meam tibi, sed viri cuiusdam Syri* ("I [give] you not my own [opinion], but [that] of a certain Syrian man [illegible] [illegible]")* 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉*( says Basil) which words incubare or 〈◊ 〉 importing warmth, hatching, or quickening, have a special likeness. *Verbum translatum est ab avibus pullitiei 〈◊ 〉 incubantibus, 〈◊ 〉 spirituali* ("The word is taken from birds brooding over their young [illegible], [illegible] spiritual,")*,* *& plane inenarrabili, non 〈◊ 〉 corporali modo; The word is taken of birds hatching their young, not corporally, but in a spiritual and inexpressible manner.*

## p00823 · sig. 46a

Some of the Hebrews *Hebrewes* convert it to this effect, *Spiritus Dei 〈◊ 〉, The Spirit of God did flutter: *the Chaldean *Chaldaean* Paraphrast in this sense, *ventus a 〈◊ 〉 Dei sufflabat* ("a wind from [illegible] of God was blowing,")*, *or as other understand the Chaldean, *flabat, pellebat, removebat: the wind from the face of God did blow under, drive, or remove, *or *did blow upon*, according to the 147. *Psalm. He caused his wind to blow, and the waters increase: but there was yet no wind nor exhalation:* Arias Montanus in these words, *Et spiritus 〈◊ 〉 Merachefet, idest, efficaciter motitans, 〈◊ 〉, ac agitans super facies gemini liquoris; The Spirit of God effectually* *and often moving, keeping warm, and cherishing, quickening and stirring upon the face of this double liquor. *For he makes four originals, whereof three are agents, and the last passive and material, to wit, causa, which is the divine goodness: 〈◊ 〉, which is, *fiat, sive erit, let it be, *or *it shallbe. Quae vox verbo Dei prima prolata fuit: which voice*( says he) *was the first that was uttered by the word of God. *The third *Spiritus Elohim, the Spirit of God, idest, vis quaedam divina, agilis ac praesens per omnia pertingens, omnia complens* ("The Spirit of God — that is, a certain divine power, swift and present, reaching through all things, filling all things.")*, *that is, *a certain divine power, or strength every where, 〈◊ 〉 and extending, and stretching through all, filling and finishing all things. *The fourth he calls *Maim, idest, materies adomnem rem conficiendam habilis; matter apt to become every thing. *For my self I am resolved *( Cum Deus sit superrationale omni ratione, Seeing* *God is in all reason above reason) *that although the effects which follow his wonderful ways of working, may in a measure be perceived by mans 〈◊ 〉, yet the manner and first operation of his divine power cannot be conceived by any mind, or spirit, compassed with a mortal body. *Animalis 〈◊ 〉 quae Dei sunt 〈◊ 〉 percipit: For my thoughts*( says the Lord in *Isaiah* *Esay**) are not your thoughts, neither are your* *ways my ways*. And as the World has not known God himself: So are his ways( according to S. Paul) *past finding out. Orighteous Father, the World has not known thee, *says CHRIST. And therefore, whether that motion, vitality and operation, 〈◊ 〉 by incubation, or how else, the manner is only known to God, *〈◊ 〉 in omnibus sit rebus vel per essentiam, vel per potentiam, intellect us noster non capit; For bow God*( says S. Augustine, speaking of his Ubiquity) *is in all things, either by essence, presence, or power, our understanding cannot comprehend. Nihil inter Deum 〈◊ 〉 distaret, si consilia, & dispositions illius maiestatis aeternae, cogitatio assequeretur humana: There* *would be no difference between God and Man, if mans understanding could conceive the counsels and disposing of that eternal Majesty*; and therefore to be over-curious in searching how the all-powerful Word of God wrought in the creation of the World or his all-piercing and operative Spirit distinguishing, gave form to the matter of the Universal, is a labour and search like unto his, who not contented with a known and safe ford, will presume to passe over the greatest River in all parts, where he is ignorant of their depths: for So does the one lose his life, and the other his understanding. We behold the Sun, and enjoy his light, as long as we look towards it, but tenderly, and circumspectly: we warm our selves safely, while we stand near the fire; but if we seek to outface the one, to enter into the other, we forth with become blind or burnt.

But to eschew curiosity: this is true, that the English word( moved) is most proper and significant: for of motion proceeds all production, and all whatsoever is effected. And this omnipotent Spirit of God, which may indeed be truly called, Principium motus, and with *Mirandula, vis causae efficientis, The force of the 〈◊ 〉 cause, *Saint Augustine sometimes takes for the holy Ghost; sometime for a wind or breath, *Sub nomine Spiritus, under the name of a Spirit, *which is sometimes So taken: or for *virtualis 〈◊ 〉, for a created virtuality: Tertullian *and Theodoret call it also a breath or wind: Mercury *Mercurius* names it, *Spiritum 〈◊ 〉 intelligibilem, a pure or thin intelligible spirit: *ANAXAGORAS, mentem: TOSTATUS *TOSTATVS*, *〈◊ 〉 & mentem Dei, The will and mind of God*; which *mens, Plato in Timaeo* ("mind — Plato in the Timaeus"), makes animam mundi ("makes [it] the soul of the world"), *The soul of the World*: and in his sixth *sixt* Book de Republica, he calls it *the law of Heaven*; in his Epistles, *the leader of things to come, and the presence of things past. *But as Cyprian wrote of the Incarnation of CHRIST our Saviour, *Men's* *Mens** deficit, vox silet, & non 〈◊ 〉, sedetiam Angelorum: My mind fails, my voice is silent, and not mine only, but 〈◊ 〉 the 〈◊ 〉 of Angels: *So may all men else say in the understanding, and utterance of the ways and works of the Creation; for to him( says Nazianzenus) there is not one substance by which he is, and another, by which he can, *Sed 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 est, quicquid eius est, & quicquidest, Whatsoever attribute of him there is, and whatsoever he is, it is the very same substance that himself is.*

## p00825 · sig. 46b

But the Spirit of God which moved upon the waters, cannot be taken for a breath or wind nor for any other creature, separate from the infinite active power of God, which then 〈◊ 〉 and distinguished, and which how sustains, and gives continuance to the Universal. For the Spirit of the Lord fills all the World; and the same is it *which maintains all things*, says Solomon *SALOMON*. *If thou send forth thy Spirit, *( says DAVID) *they are created*: And Gregory *GREGORIE*, *Deus suo praesentiali esse, 〈◊ 〉 omnibus rebus esse, ita quod, sise rebus subtraheret, sicut de 〈◊ 〉 factae sunt omnia, sic in 〈◊ 〉 defluerent universa; God gives being to all things, by being present with all things, So as if he should withdraw himself from them, then, as of nothing the World was made, it would again fall away and vanish into nothing. *And this working of Gods Spirit in all things, Virgil has expressed excellently:

## p00826 · sig. 46b

And this was the same Spirit, which moved in the Universal, and thereby both distinguished and adorned it. *His Spirit 〈◊ 〉 garnished the 〈◊ 〉, *says Job *Iob*. So then the Spirit of God moved upon the waters, and created in them their spirituality, and natural motion; motion brought forth heat; and heat rarefaction, and subtlety of parts. By this Spirit, ( which gave heat and motion, and thereby, operation to every nature, while it moved upon the waters, which were in one undigested lump, and Chaos, disposed to all forms alike) was begotten air: an element superior, as lighter then the waters, through whose vast, open, subtle, diaphanic, or transparent body, the light afterwards created might easily transpierce: light, for the excellency thereof, being the first creature which God called good, whose creation immediately followed. This Spirit Chrysostom *Chrysostome* calls a vital Operation, *aquis a 〈◊ 〉 insitam, ex qua aquae non solum motionem, sed & vim procreandi animalia habuerint* ("[a power] implanted in the waters by [illegible], from which the waters had not only motion, but also the power of producing animals.")*. *He calls it, *A vital Operation given by God unto the waters, whereby the water, had not only motion, but also power to procreate or bring forth living creatures.*
