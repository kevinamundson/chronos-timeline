# Book I, Part I, Chapter XI, §. 1

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p01463 · sig. 148b

§. I. That ZOROASTER was not HAM *CHAM*, nor the first Inventor of Astrology, or of Magic: and that there were divers great Magicians of this name.

## p01464 · sig. 148b

ZOROASTER King of the *Bactrians, Vincentius *supposes to be Ham, the son of Noah: A fancy of little probability. For Ham was the Paternal Ancestor of Ninus, the Father of Cush *Chus*, the Grand-father of Nimrod, whose son was Belus, the Father of Ninus. It may be that Vincentius had heard of that book which was called Scripturae Ham, devised by some wicked Knave, and So entitled: of which Sixtus Senensis has made the due mention.

## p01465 · sig. 148b

It is reported by Cassianus, that Serenus Abbas gave the invention of Magic to Ham, the son of Noah: So did Comestor in his Scholastic History: which Art( says he) with the seven liberal Sciences he writ in fourteen Pillers: seven of which were made of brass, to resist the defacing by the waters of the Flood *Floud*; and seven of brick against the injury of fire. There was also another devised discourse, which went under the title of *Prophetia Ham. Cassianus *out of Serenus has somewhat like unto this of Comestor: These be Cassianus words: HAM( filius NOAH) *qui superstitionibus istis & sacrilegis fuit artibus infectus, sciens nullum se posse super hijs memorialem librum in Arcam 〈◊ 〉 infer, in qua erat cum patre justo, &c. *HAM *( the son of *NOAH) *who was infected with these superstitions, and sacrilegious Arts, knowing that he could not bring any book or memorial of that nature into the Ark, wherein he was to remain with his godly* *Father, caused the Precepts and Rules thereof to be graven in metal and hard stone.*

## p01466 · sig. 148b

S. Augustine notes that Zoroaster was said to have laughed at his birth, when all other children weep; which presaged the great knowledge which afterward he attained unto: being taken for the Inventor of natural Magic and other Arts; for the Corrupter, says Pliny *Plinie* and Justin *Iustine*. But I do not think that Zoroaster invented the doctrine of the Horoscopes or Nativities *Natiuities*; or first found out the nature of herbs, stones, and minerals, or their Sympathetical or Antipathetical workings; of which, I know not what King of Chaldea *Chaldaea* is also made the Inventor. I rather think that these knowledges were far more ancient, and left by Noah to his sons. For Abraham who had not any acquaintance with Zoroaster, ( as Josephus *Iosephus* reports) was no less learned herein then any other in that age, if he exceeded not all men then living: differing from the wisdom of after-times in this, that he knew and acknowledged the true cause, and giver of life and virtue to nature and all natural things; whereas others( forgetting Gods infinite, dispersed, and universal power) admired the instruments, and attributed proper strength to the things themselves, ( from which the effects were sensible) which belonged to that wisdom, *Which being one*, *and remaining in it self, can do all things and renews all.*

## p01467 · sig. 149a

Now whether this Zoroaster( over-thrown by Ninus) were the same which was So excellent a Naturalist, it is doubted. For Zoroaster the *Magician, Ctesias *calls Oxyartes, whom Pliny finds of a later time. And if Zoroaster were taken away by a Spirit( being in the midst of his Disciples) as some Authors report, then Zoroaster, slain by Ninus, was not the Magician: which is also the opinion of 〈◊ 〉.

## p01468 · sig. 149a

Again, Josephus and Cedrenus affirm, that Seth first found out the Planets, or wandering Stars *Starres*, and other Motions of the Heavens: for if this art had been invented by Zoroaster, he could not have attained to any such excellency therein, in his own life time; but being a man( as it seems) of singular judgement, he might ad somewhat to this kind of knowledge, and leave it by writing to posterity.

## p01469 · sig. 149a

But of this Zoroaster there is much dispute: and no less jangling about the word and art of *Magic. 〈◊ 〉 *remembers four, to whom the name of Zoroaster, or Zoroastres was given: which by Hermodorus and Dinon seems to be but a cognomen, or name of art, and was as much to say, as astrorum cultor. The first, Arnobius calls the Bactrian, which may be the same that Ninus overthrew: the second, a Chaldean *Chaldaean*, and the Astronomer of Ninus: the third was Zoroaster Pamphylius, who lived in the time of Cyrus, and his familiar: the fourth, Zoroaster Armenius, the Nephew of Hystaspes *Hostianes*, which followed Xerxes into Greece: between whom and Cyrus there past threescore and eighteen years. Suidas remembers a fifth *fift*, called *Per somedus sapiens* ("Through [his] dream [he became] wise"): and Plato speaks of Zoroaster the son of Oromasdes; which Picus Mirandula confirms.

## p01470 · sig. 149a

Now of what Nation the first and chief Zoroaster was, it is doubted. Pliny and Laertius make him a *Persian. Gemisthius *or *Plethon* *Pletho**, Ficinus *and 〈◊ 〉, make him a Chaldean. But by those books of one Zoroaster, found by Picus Mirandula, it appears plainly, that the Author of them was a Chaldean by Nation, though the word( Chaldean) was as often given to the learned Priests peculiarly, as for any distinguishment of Nations. Porphyry *Porphyrius* makes the Chaldaei and Magi divers; Picus the same. But that this Zoroaster was a Chaldean both by Nation and Profession, it appears by his Books *Bookes*, which( says Picus) were written in the Chaldean tongue; and the Comment in the same language. Now that the Magi and they were not differing, it may be judged by the name of those books of Zoroaster, which in an Epistle of Mirandula to Ficinus, he says, to be entitled, Patris EZRE ZOROASTRIS, & MELCHIOR magorum oracula.
