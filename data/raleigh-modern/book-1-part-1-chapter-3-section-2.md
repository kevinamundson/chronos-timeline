## p00904 · sig. 60a

§. II. *A recital of strange opinions, touching Paradise.*

## p00905 · sig. 60a

Now as touching Paradise, first it is to be enquired, whether there were a Paradise, or no? or whether Moses description were altogether mystical, and allegorical? as *Origen, Philo, Francis* *Fran**. Georgius, *with others have affirmed, and that under the names of those four Rivers *Riuers*, *Pishon* *Pison**, Gihon* *Gehon**, Hiddekel* *Hidekel*, and *Euphrates* *Perath**. The tree of life, and the tree of Knowledge, *there were delivered unto us other mysteries and significations; as, that by the four Rivers were meant the four Cardinal virtues, *Justice, Temperance, Fortitude*, and Prudence, or( by other) Oil, Wine, Milk, and Honey. This Allegorical understanding of Paradise by Origen divulged, was again by Francis Georgius received( says Sixtus Senensis) whose frivolous imaginations Sixtus himself does fully and learnedly answer, in the 34. Annotation of his fifth *fift* Book, fol. 338. the last Edition.

## p00906 · sig. 60a

S. Ambrose also leaned wholly to the Allegorical construction, and set Paradise in the third heaven, and in the virtues of the mind, *& in nostro principali* ("and in our ruling [part]"), which is, as I conceive it in mente, or in our souls: to the particulars whereof he alludes in this sort. By the place or garden of Paradise, was meant the soul or mind; by *Adam, Men's* *Mens*, or Understanding; by Eve, the Sense; by the Serpent, Delectation; by the Tree of good and evil, Sapience; and by the rest of the Trees, the virtues of the mind, or in the mind planted, or from thence springing. Notwithstanding all which, upon the first of the *Corinthians, cap. 6*. he in direct words allows both of a celestial and terrestrial Paradise; the one, into which S. Paul was rapt; the other, into which Adam was put by God. Aug. Chrysamensis was of opinion, that a Paradise had been, but that there was not now any mark thereof on the earth: the same being not only defaced, but withal the places now not So much as existing. To which Luther seems to adhere.

## p00907 · sig. 60a

The Manichees also understood, that by Paradise was meant the whole Earth; to which opinion Vadianus inclines, as I conceive his words in two several places. First, upon this: *Fill the earth*, GEN. 10. Of which he gives this judgement. *Hoc ipso etiam quod dixit, Replete terram, dominamini universis animantibus, subijcite terram, 〈◊ 〉 docet, totam terram extantem, & omnigenis( ut tum er at) fructibus consitam, sedem & hortum illud* ("By this very fact that he said, 'Fill the earth, have dominion over all living creatures, subdue the earth,' [illegible] teaches that the whole earth as it then stood, planted with all kinds of fruits (as it then was), was the seat and garden [of man];")* *AD ae, *& posteritatis futurae fuisse; These words*( says he) in which *God said, Bring forth fruit and multiply, and fill the earth, and subdue it, and rule over every creature, do clearly show, that the Universal earth set or filled with all sorts of fruits( as then it was) was the garden and seat of *Adam, *and of his future posterity*. And afterward he acknowledges the place, out of the Acts the 17. *Apostolus ex uno sanguine* ("The Apostle: [God made] from one blood [all nations of men]") *omne genus humanum adeo factum docet, ut habitarent super universam facicm terrae: tota igitur terra Paradisus ill er at; The Apostle*( says he) *teaches, that God has made of one blood all mankind, to dwell over all the face of the earth: and therefore all the earth*( says he) *was that Paradise*. Which conjectures I will answer in order. Goropius Becanus differs not much from this opinion, but yet he acknowledges that Adam was first planted by God in one certain place, and peculiar Garden; which place Goropius finds near the River of Acesines, in the confines of India.

## p00908 · sig. 60b

*Tertullian, Bonaventure*, and Durandus, make Paradise under the 〈◊ 〉, and Postellus, quite contrary, under the North pole: the Chaldeans *Chaldaeans* also for the most 〈◊ 〉 and all their Sectators, followed the opinion of Origen, or rather Origen theirs, 〈◊ 〉 would either make Paradise a figure, or Sacrament only, or else would have it 〈◊ 〉 out of this sensible World or raised into some high and remote Region of the Air. Strabo *Strabus*, and Rabanus, were both sic of this vanity, with Origen, and Philo: So was our venerable Bede *Beda*, and Pet. Comestor, and Moses Barcephas the Syrian, translated by Masius. But as Hopkins says of Philo Iudaeus, that he wondered, *Quo malo genio afflatus; By what evil Angel he was blown up into this error: *So can I not but greatly marvel at these learned men, who So grossly and blindly wandered; seeing Moses, and after him the Prophets, do So plainly describe this place, by the Region, in which it was planted, by the Kingdoms *Kingdomes* and Provinces *Prouinces* bordering it, by the Rivers which watered it, and by the points of the Compass upon which it lay, in respect of Judea *Iudaea*, or Canaan.

## p00909 · sig. 60b

Noviomagus *Nouiomagus* also, upon *Bede, De naturarerum* ("On the Nature of Things"), believes that all the Earth was taken for Paradise, and not any one place. For the whole Earth( says he) has the same beauty ascribed to Paradise. He adds, that the Ocean was that Fountayne, from whence the four Rivers, *Pishon, Gihon, Tigris*, and Euphrates, had their beginning: for he could not think it possible, that these Rivers of *Ganges, Nile* *Nilus**, Tigris*, and Euphrates, ( whereof the one ran through India, the other through Egypt *Aegypt*, and the other two through Mesopotamia and Armenia) could rise out of one Fountayne, were it not out of the Fountayne of the Ocean.
