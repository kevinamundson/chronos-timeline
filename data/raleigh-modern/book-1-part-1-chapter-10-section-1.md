# Book I, Part I, Chapter X, §. 1

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p01417 · sig. 141b

§. I. That Nimrod *was the first after the Flood* *Floud** that reigned like Sovereign Lord: and that his beginning seems to have been of just authority.*

## p01418 · sig. 141b

THE first of all that reigned as Sovereign Lord after the Flood was Nimrod, the Son of Cush, distinguished by Moses from the rest( according to Saint Augustine) in one of these two respects: either for his eminence, and because he was the first of fame, and that took on him to command others: or else in that he was begotten by Cush *Chush*, after his other children were also become Fathers; and of a later time then some of his Grand-children and Nephews *Nephewes*. Howsoever, seeing Moses in express words calls Nimrod the Son of Cush, other men's conjectures to the contrary ought to have no respect. This Empirie of Nimrod, both the Fathers and many later Writers call tyrannical: the same beginning in Babel, ( which is) confusion. But it seems to me that Melanchthon *Melanchton* conceived not amiss hereof: the same exposition being also made by the Author of that work called Onomasticum Theologicum, who affirms that Nimrod was therefore called *Amarus Dominator, A bitter or severe Governor, *because his form of rule seemed at first far more terrible then Paternal authority. And therefore is he in this respect also called *a mighty Hunter*; because he took and destroyed both Beasts and Thieves *Theeues*. But Saint Augustine understands it otherwise, and converts the word( ante) by( contra) affirming therein, that Nimrod was a mighty Hunter against God: *Sic ergo intelligendus est Gigas ill, Venator contra Dominum; So is that Giant to be understood, a Hunter against the Lord. *But howsoever this word *( a mighty Hunter*) be understood; yet it rather appears, that as Nimrod had the command of all those, which went with him from the East into Shinar *Shinaar*: So, this charge was rather given him, then by him usurped. For it is no where found, that Noah himself, or any of the Sons of his own body came with this troupe into Babylonia: no mention at all being made of Noah( the years of his life excepted) in the succeeding Story of the Hebrews *Hebrewes*: nor that Shem *Sem* was in this disobedient Troupe, or among the builders of Babel.

## p01419 · sig. 142a

The same is also confirmed by divers ancient Historians, that *Nimrod, Suphne*, and 〈◊ 〉 were the Captains *Captaines* and Leaders of all those which came from the East. And though Shem came not himself So far West as Shinar( his lot being cast on the East parts) yet from his Sons Nephew Eber *Heber*, the name and Nation of the Hebrews ( according to the general opinion) took beginning, who inhabited the Southermost parts of Chaldea *Chaldoea* about the City of Ur; from whence Abraham was by God called into Haran *Charran*, and thence into Canaan.

## p01420 · sig. 142a

And because those of the Race of Shem which came into 〈◊ 〉, were no partners in the unbelieving work of the Tower: therefore( as many of the Fathers conjecture) did they retain the first and most ancient language, which the Fathers of the first Age had left to Noah; and Noah to Shem and his Issues: In familia Eber *remansit 〈◊ 〉 lingua; In the Family of *Eber *this Language remained*( says Saint Augustine out of Epiphanius; ) and this Language Abraham used: yea it was anciently and before the Flood the general speech: and therefore first called( says *Coelestinus) lingus* ("Coelestinus) [the] tongue") *humana: the humane tongue*.

## p01421 · sig. 142a

We know that 〈◊ 〉 Becanus following *Theodoret, Rabbi Moses, 〈◊ 〉, Vergara, *and others, is of another opinion; but howsoever we determine of this point, we may with good probability resolve, that none of the godly seed of Shem were the chief Leaders of this presumptuous multitude. And seeing it is not likely but that some one was by order appointed for this charge, we may imagine that Nimrod rather had it by just authority, then by violence of usurpation.
