§. VII. *Of divers ways by which the Devil seems to work his wonders.*

## p01518 · sig. 154a

BUT to the end that we may not dote with the Manichees, who make two powers of gods: that we do not give to the Devil any other dominion then he has( not to speak of his ability, when he is the Minister of Gods vengeance, as when Egypt *Aegypt*, according to David *Dauid*, was destroyed by evil Angels) he otherwise works but three ways. The first is by moving the cogitations and affections of men: The second by the exquisite knowledge of Nature: and the third by deceit, illusion, and false semblance. And that they cannot work what they would, G. Parisiensis gives three causes: the first, a natural impotency: the second, their own reason dissuading them from daring overmuch, or indeed( and that which is the only certain cause) the great mercy of the Creator, *Tenens eas ligatas*( says the same Author) *velut immanissimas bellu as*. Saint Augustine was of opinion that the Frogs *Frogges* which 〈◊ 〉 Sorcerers produced were not natural, but that the Devil( by betraying of their senses that looked on) made them appear to be such. For as Vairus observes, those Frogs of the Enchanters *Inchanters* were not found corrupted as those of Moses were, which might argue that they were not creatures indeed. Hereof says Saint AVGUSTINE *AVGVSTINE*: *Nec sane Daemons* *Daemones** naturas creant, sed quae a Deocreatae sunt commutant, ut videantur esse quodnon sunt: The Devils* *Deuils** create not any natures, but So change those that are created by God, as they seem to be that which they be not: *of which in the 83. Question he gives the reason. *〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 nebulis implet omnes meatus intelligentiae, per quos aperire lumen rationis radius mentis solet* ("[illegible] [illegible] fills with mists all the passages of understanding, through which the ray of the mind is wont to open the light of reason,")*, *( that is) *The Devil fills with certain clouds all passages of the understanding, by which the beam of the mind is wont to open the light of reason.*

## p01519 · sig. 154a

And as Tertullian in his Book de anima ("On the Soul") rightly conceives, if the Devil can possess himself of the eyes of our minds, and blind them, it is not hard for him to dazzle those of the body. For( out of doubt) by the same way that God passes out, the Devil enters in, beginning with the fantasy, by which he does more easily betray the other faculties of the soul: for the fantasy is most apt to be abused by vain apprehensions.

Aquinas on the contrary held that those Frogs were not imaginary, but such indeed as they seemed: not made *Magicae 〈◊ 〉 ludibrio*, which indeed agrees not with the Art but( according to THOMAS) *Per aptam & idoneam agentium & patientium applicationem: By an apt and fit applying of agents and patients. *And this I take to be more probable. For Moses could not be deceived by that sleight of false semblance; and Saint Augustine in another case like unto this, ( to wit) of the turning of Diomedes his Companions into Birds, *per activa cum passivis* ("through active [things] with passive [things]"), inclines rather to this opinion: though I am not persuaded that Saint Augustine believed that of Diomedes. And this opinion of *Thomas, G. Parisiensis *a man very learned also confirms. For speaking of natural Magic he uses these words: *De 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 est* ("Concerning [illegible] [illegible] [illegible] it is [said];") *subita generatio 〈◊ 〉, & pediculorum, & vermium, aliormque animalium quorundam: in quibus omnibus sola natura operatur, verum adhibitis adiutorijs, 〈◊ 〉 semina naturae confortant & acuunt, it a ut opus generationis tantum accelerent, ut 〈◊ 〉 qui 〈◊ 〉 nesciunt non opus natur & videatur( quae tar dius 〈◊ 〉 efficere consuevit) sedpotentia Daemonum, & c* ("The sudden generation of [illegible], and of lice, and of worms, and of certain other animals: in all of which nature alone works, but with aids applied, [illegible] strengthen and sharpen the seeds of nature, so that they only hasten the work of generation, so that [illegible] who do not know [illegible] it may seem not the work of nature (which is accustomed to accomplish it more slowly) but the power of demons, etc.")*. *to which he adds: *Qui autem in hijs docti sunt talia non mirantur, sed solum Creatorem in hijs glorificant: In such works*( says he) *the sudden generation of Frogs, and Lice, and Worms* *Wormes**, and some other creatures is: in all which Nature alone works; but by means* *strengthening the Seeds of Nature, and quickening them; in such wise that they So hasten the work of generation, that it seems to the ignorant not to be the work of Nature, which usually works more 〈◊ 〉, but they think it is done by the power of Devils. But they who are learned in these Arts marvel not at such working, but glorify the Creator. *Now by these two ways the Devills *Deuills* do most frequently work, ( to wit) by knowing the uttermost of nature; and by illusion: for there is no incomprehensible or unsearchable power, but of God only.

## p01521 · sig. 154b

For shall we say, he causes sometimes thunders, lightnings, and tempests; and can infect the air, as well as move it or compress it; who knows not that these things are also natural? Or may it be objected that he foretells things before they happen, which exceeds nature, and is no illusion? it is true, that he sometimes does it; but how? In elder ages he stole his knowledge out of the predictions of the Prophets: and he fore-told the death of Saul, at such time as he was in his own possession and power to dispose of. And he that has lived from the infancy of the World to this day, and observed the success of every counsel: he that by reason of his swift motions can inform himself of all places, and preparations: he that is of counsel with all those that study and practise subversion and destruction: he that is Prince of the air, and can thence better judge, then those that inhabit the earth: if he should not sometimes, yea if he should not very oftentimes guess rightly of things to come( where God pleases not to give impediment) it were very strange. For we see that wise and learned men do oftentimes by comparing like causes conceive rightly of like effects, before they happen: and yet where the Devil doubts and would willingly keep his credit, he evermore answers by Riddles, as

Which answer may be taken either way: either for the overthrow of his own Kingdom, or of his Enemies. And thus far we grant the Devil may proceed in predictions, which( otherwise) belong to God only; as it is in Isaiah *ESAY*: *Show the things that are to come hereafter, that we may know that ye are Gods: show us at all times* *and certaynely what is to come. Solius enim divinae intelligentiae ac 〈◊ 〉 est, 〈◊ 〉 nosse* ("and certainly what is to come. For it belongs to divine intelligence alone and [illegible] to know [the future];") *& revelare; It is only proper to Gods understanding and wisdom, to know and reveal hidden things.*
