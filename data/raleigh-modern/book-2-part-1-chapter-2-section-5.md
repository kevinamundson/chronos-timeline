# Book II, Part I, Chapter II, §. 5

## p01628 · sig. 170a

§. V. Of the time when OSIRIS reign ended: and that Jacob *IACOB* came into Egypt *Aegypt* in the time of HORUS *ORVS* the son of OSIRIS.

## p01629 · sig. 170a

The death of Osiris, when it was, none can certainly affirm. The only conjecture that I know is made thus: Lehabim the son of Mizraim called Hercules Lybius, made war in Italy, to revenge his Fathers death, on the associates of Typhon, in the 41. year of Baleus King of Assyria: before which year he had made many great wars in *Egypt, Phoenicia, Phrygia, Crete, Libya* *Lybia**, *and Spain *Spaine*: and having ended his Egyptian *Aegyptian* wars, left the Kingdom to Horus. Thus far Berosus, or authors following Berosus. That Horus last of all the Gods( as they were styled) held the Kingdom of *Isis, Diodorus Siculus* plainly says: and Plutarch as much; to which all old Histories agree. 〈◊ 〉 hereupon infers, that six years may be allowed to the wars, which Hercules made in So many Countries, after the Egyptian wars were ended: So should the death of Osiri, have been the 34. of Baleus, when himself had reigned 297. years. I think that Krentzhemius was a greater Scholar than Soldier. For surely in those days when commerce was not such as now, but all Navigation made by coasting, a far longer time would have been required, to the subduing of So many Countries. An allowance of more time though it would alter his computation, yet would it well agree with his intent: which was( doubtless) to find the truth. If according to his account the death of Osiris had been the 34. of Baleus, then must Israel have come into Egypt but seven years before the death of Osiris: and have lived there in the reign of Typhon. A thing not easily believed. For it was the same king who advanced Joseph *Ioseph*, bad him send for his Father; and gave him leave to go into Canaan, to the performance of his Fathers funeral: as may easily be gathered out of the book of Genesis. Whereas therefore the reign of Osiris cannot be extended by any possible allowance in account of times, beyond the seventh *seuenth* year of Israels coming into Egypt: we must needs cut off 23. years from that number, which Krentzhemius conjectures his reign to have continued: namely seven which he should have lived after Jacobs *Iacobs* coming into Egypt: nine in which Joseph had there flourished, ere his fathers coming: and other seven in which Typhon and Hercules had reigned after the death of Osiris, yet before Josephs *Iosephs* advancement.

## p01630 · sig. 170b

Neither will this disagree with the time of Hercules Lybius his wars. For the war which Hercules made in Italy *Italie*, is said to have endured 10. years: After which proportion we may well give not only six years, as Krentzhemius does, but 23. more to So many wars in So many and So far-distant Countries, as are named before: yea, by this proportion we may attribute unto Horus the 13. years, which passed between the time of Josephs being sold into Egypt, unto his advancement: considering that Putiphar who bought him, and whose daughter he may seem to have married, continued all that while chief Steward unto Pharaoh: a thing not likely to have been, if So violent alterations had happened the whilst in Egypt, as the tyrannous usurpation of Typhon must needs have brought in. If citing some fragment of a lost old author, I should confidently say, that Putiphar for his faithfulness to Horus, the son of Osiris, was by him in the beginning of his reign made his chief Steward: at which time buying Joseph and finding him a just man, and one under whose hand all things did prosper, he rather committed his estate into Josephs hands, than unto any of his Egyptian followers( many of whom he had found either false-hearted, or weak and unlucky in the troublesome days of Typhon) I know not what could be objected against this. Perhaps I might proceed further & say, That when the saying of Joseph pleased Pharaoh, and all his servants: then Putiphar Priest of On, being chief Officer to Pharaoh, did acknowledge in Joseph, the ancient graces of God & his injurious imprisonment: whereupon he gave him his daughter to wife: and being old, resigned his office of chief Steward unto him: who afterward in regard of Putiphar, did savour the Priests, when he bought the lands of all other Egyptians *Aegyptians*. This might appear to some a tale not unlike to the friarly book of *Asenath, Potiphar's* *Putiphars** *daughter: but unto such as consider that God works usually by means: and that Putiphar was the Steward of that king, under whom Jacob died: it would seem a matter not improbable, had it an Author of sufficient credit to avouch it. Concerning the wars of Hercules, in which by this reckoning he should have spent 42. years after he left Egypt, ere he began in Italy, it is a circumstance which( the length of his Italian wars considered, and his former enterprises & achievements proportioned to them) does not make against us, but for us: or if it were against us, yet could it not So weaken our supposition, as these probabilities collected out of the indisputable truth of Scripture do confirm it. Nevertheless I freely grant that all these proofs are no other, than such as may be gathered out of Authors, not well agreeing, nor to be reconciled in such obscurity, otherwise than by likelihoods, answerable to the holy Text.
