§. VII. Of HOMER and HESIOD, and many changes in the World, that happened about this age:

## p02665 · sig. 291b

About this time that excellent learned Poet Homer lived, as many of the best 〈◊ 〉 affirm. He was by race of the Maeones, descended( as Funck *Functius* imagines) of Berosus his Anamaeon, who gave name to that people. But this Funck imagines Homer the Poet to have been long after these times, rashly framing his 〈◊ 〉 according to a Archilochus in the tract, or rather fragment de temporibus; and makes seven more of this name to have flourished in divers Cities in Greece. Whence, perhaps, sprang the diversity of opinions, both of the time and of the native City of Homer. According to this *Archilochus, Funck *finds Homer about the time of Manasseh *Manasse* King of Judah *Iuda*, and Numa of Rome. He was called Melesigenes from the place of his birth, and at length Homer, because blind men follow a guide, which signification among others, is in the verb 〈◊ 〉: for this Homer in his latter time was blind. * Clemens 〈◊ 〉 recites many different opinions touching the question of the time when Homer lived. So also b Aulus Gellius, and 〈◊ 〉 Assyrius in his Oration *ad gentes. Paterculus* ("The confession of the accused is not to be held as proved crime, unless other approbation instructs [the judge's] religion [i.e. conscience].")* *reckons that Homer flourished 950. years before the Consulship of Marcus Vinicius *Vinutius*: which Mercator casts up in the worlds year 3046. and after Troy taken, about 260. years: and about 250. years before the building of Rome, making him to have flourished about the time of Ichosaphat King of Judah. But Clemens Alexandrinus and Tatianus above named, mention authors that make him much ancienter. The difference of which authors in this point is not 〈◊ 〉 the readers consideration, that by this one instance he may guess of the difficulty, and So pardon the 〈◊ 〉 in the computations of ancient time: seeing in such diversity of opinions a man may hardly find out what to follow. For Crates the Grammarian( as Clemens Alexandrinus reports) gave being to Homer about 80. years after Troy taken, near the time that the Heraclidae returned into Peloponnesus: and * Eratosthenes after Troy 100. years. Theopompus 500. years after the army of Greece sailed into 〈◊ 〉 for the war of *Troy. 〈◊ 〉 *makes him contemporary with Gyges, who began to reign in the 18. Olympiad( which was 45. years after Rome was built) and Sosibius says, that he was 90. years before the first Olympiad: which 〈◊ 〉 seeks to prove by the times of Charillus and his son *Nicander; Philochorus* *Philocorus** *places him 180. after *Troy: Aristarchus *140. in the time of the seating of the Colonies in *Ionia. Apollodorus* affirms that he lived while Agesilaus governed 〈◊ 〉; and that Lycurgus in his young years, about 100. years after the Ionian plantations, came to visit him, near 240. years after Troy taken. Herodotus finds Homer flourishing 622. years before Xerxes enterprise against the Grecians *Graecians*: which Beroaldo *Beroaldus* accounts at 168. years after the Trojan war. Eusebius seems to make him to have been about the time of Joash *Ioas* King of Judah 124 years before Rome built: though elsewhere in his Chronology he notes, that some place him in the time of Samuel, and others in the end of David *Dauid*, and others in other ages. In his Evangelical preparation, where out of Tatianus Assyrius he cites sundry opinions touching the time when Homer lived, he reckons many other Greek *Greeke* writers more ancient than Homer; as *Linus, Philammon, Epimenides, Phemius, Aristaeus, Orpheus, Musaeus, Thamyris* *Thamyras**, Amphion, *and others.

## p02666 · sig. 292a

Now whether Homer or Hesiod *Hesiodus* were the elder, it is also much disputed. Aulus Gellius reports that Philochorus and Xenophanes affirm, that Homer preceded Hesiod: and on the contrary, that Luc. Accius the Poet, and Ephorus the Historian make Hesiod of an elder time than *Homer. Varro *leaves it uncertain which of these learned fablers was first borne: but he finds that they lived together some certain years, wherein he confirms himself by an Epigram, written upon a Trivet, and left by Hesion in Helicon.

## p02667 · sig. 292a

Cornelius Nepos reports that they both lived 160. years before Rome built: while the 〈◊ 〉 reigned in Alba, about a 140. years after the fall of Troy. b Euthimenes finds them both 200. 〈◊ 〉 after Troy taken, in the time of Acastus the son of Pelias, King of Thessaly. For my self, I am not much troubled when this Poet lived; neither would I offend the Reader with these opinions, but only to show the uncertainty and disagreement of Historians, as well in this particular, as in all other questions and dispute of time. For the curiosity of this mans age is no less ridiculous, than the inquisition why he began his Iliads *Iliades* with the word Menin, as perhaps containing some great mystery. In derision whereof Lucian feigning himself to have been in hell, and to have spoken with Homer, there asked him the cause why he began his book with that word: who answered, That he began in that sort, because it came in his head So to do.

It seems that Senyes, or after Macrobius Senemires, ruled Egypt *Aegypt* at this time: for Tanephersobris was his successor, who preceded Apries *Vaphres*, father in law to Solomon *Salomon*.

About the end of Sauls government, or in the beginning of Davids *Dauids* time according to Cassiodorus, the Amazons *Amazones* with the Cymmerians invaded *Asia, 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 *then ruling in Italy *Italie*. And besides the overthrow of that famous State of Troy( which fell 103. years before Davids time) there were many other changes in the middle part of the World, not only by reason of those Northern Nations: but there sprung up, somewhat nearly together, 〈◊ 〉 Kingdoms *Kingdomes* into greatness not before erected. In Italy, that of the Latins *Latines*: in the South part of Greece, those of Lacedaemon, 〈◊ 〉, and the Achaei. In *Arabia, Syria* *Syrja** Zobah* *Soba*, and Damascus, the Adads made themselves Princes, of which there were ten Kings, which began and ended with the King of Israel in effect: and somewhat before these, the State of the Israelites *Israeelites* having now altered their form of Government, began to flourish under Kings, of which David, in a few years, became master of all those neighbouring Nations, who by interchange of times had subjected the Judeans *Iudaeans*, corrupted their religion, and held them under in a most abject, and grievous slavery; to wit, the *Idumeans* *Edumaeans**, Moabites, Ammonites, Midianites, Ituraeans, *and the rest of the Arabians, with the *Philistines* *Philistims**, Jebusites* *Iebusites**, Geshurites, Machathites, *all which acknowledged David for their Sovereign Lord, and paid him tribute.
