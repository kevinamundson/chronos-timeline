## p02779 · sig. 304b

§. III. Of SALOMONs sending to Ophir, and of some seeming contradictions about SALOMONs riches, and of Pineda's *PINEDAES* conceit of two strange passages about Africa *Africke*.

## p02780 · sig. 304b

Here Solomon *Salomon* prepared his 〈◊ 〉 of Ships for India, with whom Hiram joined in that voyage, and furnished him with Mariners and Pilots, the Tyrians being of all other the most expert sea-men. From this part of Arabia, which at this time belonged to Edom, and was conquered by David *Dauid*, did the 〈◊ 〉 passe on to the East India, which was not far off, namely to Ophir, one of the Islands of the Moluccas, a place exceeding rich in gold: witness the Spaniards, who notwithstanding all the abundance which they gather in Peru, do yet plant in those Islands of the East at Manilia, and recover a great quantity from thence, and with less labour than they do in any one part of Peru, or new Spain *Spaine*.

## p02781 · sig. 304b

The return which was made by these Ships amounted to four hundred and twenty talents, but in the second of Chronicles the eight, it is written four hundred and fifty talents: whereof thirty talents went in expense for the charge of the fleet, and wages of men, and four hundred and twenty talents, which makes 〈◊ 〉 and twenty hundred and twenty thousand Crowns *Crownes* came clear. And thus must those two places be reconciled. As for the place 1. Reg. 10. 14. which speaks of six hundred sixty and six talents of gold, that sum, as I take it, is of other receipts of Salomons which were yearly, and which came to him besides these profits from Ophir.

## p02782 · sig. 304b

My opinion of the land of Ophir, that it is not Peru in America( as divers have thought) but a country in the East Indies; with some reason why at those times they could not make more speedy return to Jerusalem *Ierusalem* from the East Indies then in three years; and that 〈◊ 〉 in Scripture is divers times taken for the Ocean, has been already declared in the first book.

## p02783 · sig. 305a

Only it remains that I should speak somewhat of Pineda his strange conceits, who being a Spaniard of Baetica, would fain have Cadiz *Gades* or Cadiz-malis *Calis-malis*, in old times called Tartessus, which is the South west corner of that Province, to be the Tarshish *Tharsis* from whence Solomon fetched his gold; for no other reason, as it seems, but for love of his own country, and because of some affinity of sound between Tarshish and Tartessus. For whereas it may seem strange that it should be three year 〈◊ 〉 they, that took ship in the red sea, should return from the East Indies to Jerusalem, this has been in part answered already. And further the intelligent may conceive of sundry lets, in the digging and refining of the metal, and in their other traffic, and in their land-carriages between Jerusalem and the red sea, and perhaps also elsewhere: So that we have no need to make Salomons men to go many thousand miles out of their way to Cadiz, round about all Affrick, that So they might belong a coming home.

## p02784 · sig. 305a

For the direct way to Cadiz( which if Solomon and the Israclites knew not, the Tyrians which went with them, could not have been ignorant of) was along the Mediterranean *Mediterran* sea, and So( besides many wonderful 〈◊ 〉 and terrible navigation in rounding Africa) they should have escaped the troublesome land-carriage between Jerusalem and the red sea through dry, desert, and thievish Countries: and within 30. mile of Jerusalem at Joppa *Ioppe*, or some other haven in Salomons own Country, have laden and unladen their ships.

## p02785 · sig. 305a

But this direct course they could not hold( says Pineda) because the huge Island of Atlantis in largeness greater than all Africa and Asia being swallowed up in the Atlantic *Atlantik* Ocean 〈◊ 〉 Salomons ships from passing through the straits of Gibraltar: for this he alleges Plato in Timaeo. But that this calamity happened about Salomons time, or that thereby the Straights of Cadiz were filled with mud and made impassable, that there could be no coming to Cadiz by the Mediterranean sea: or that this indraught where the sea runs most violently, and most easily scours his channel, should be filled with mud, and not also the great Ocean in like manner, where this huge Island is supposed to have stood: or that Salomons ships being in the red sea, should neglect the golden mines of the East Indies( which were infinitely better and 〈◊ 〉 to the red sea, than any in Spain) to seek gold at Cadiz by the way of compassing Affrica, it is most ridiculous to imagine. For the Spaniard himself that has also the rich Peru in the West, 〈◊ 〉 in the East Indies, and inhabits some part thereof, as in Manilia, finding in those parts no less 〈◊ 〉 of gold( the small territory which he there possesses considered) than in Peru.

## p02786 · sig. 305a

The same Pineda has another strange passage round about all Africa, which elsewhere he dreams of: supposing whereas Jonas *Ionas* sailing to Tarshish the City of Cilicia, was cast out in the Mediterranean sea, and taken up there by a Whale, that this Whale in three days swimming above twelve thousand English miles, along the Mediterranean seas, and So through the straits of Cadiz, and along all the huge seas round about Africa, cast up 〈◊ 〉 upon the shore of the red sea, that So he might have perhaps some six miles the shorter( though much the worse) way to Nineveh *Niniuie*. This conceit he grounds only upon the ambiguity of the word Suph, which oftentimes is an Epitheton of the red sea( as if we should call it *mare algosum, the sea full of weeds) *for the red sea. But in Jonas 2. 5. it is generally taken in the proper signification for weeds, and not as Pineda would have it, who in this place against his own rule( which elsewhere he gives us) supposes strange miracles without any need. For this long voyage of the Whale finished in three days, is a greater miracle, than the very preservation of Jonas in the belly of the Whale: and therefore seeing there is no necessity of this miracle, we send it back unto him, keeping his own rule which in this place he forgets; *Miracula non sunt multiplicanda* ("There are not [illegible] [miracles];"). And again, *Non sunt* ("they are not")* 〈◊ 〉* *danda, nec pro arbitrio 〈◊ 〉, Miracles are not to be multiplied without 〈◊ 〉, nor delivered without cause, nor feigned at pleasure. *Therefore to leave this man in his dreams, which( were he not otherwise very learned and judicious) might be thought unworthy the mentioning. But to proceed with our Story of Solomon.

## p02787 · sig. 305b

The Queen of Sheba *Sabaes* coming from far to Solomon, ( as it seems from Arabia Felix *foelix*, and not as some think from Ethiopia *Aethiopia*) and her rich presents, and Salomons reciprocal magnificence, and his resolving of her difficult 〈◊ 〉, those are set down at large in the text. But herein Josephus *Iosephus* is greatly mistaken, who calls this Queen of Sheba Nicaules, the successor( says he out of Herodotus) of those thirty and eight Egyptian *Aegyptian* Kings which succeeded Menes *Mineus* the founder of Memphis; adding that after 〈◊ 〉 Egyptian, and the father in Law of Solomon, the name of Pharaoh was left off in Egypt *Aegypt*. For as it is elsewhere proved that the Queen was of Arabia, not of Egypt and Ethiopia; So were there other Pharaohs after the father in Law of Solomon. Yea, above three hundred years after *Solomon, Pharaoh* *Pharao** 〈◊ 〉 *slew Josiah *Iosias* King of Judah *Iuda*.

## p02788 · sig. 305b

It is also written of Solomon, that he kept in garrisons 〈◊ 〉 thousand Chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, that he spent in Court every day thirty measures of fine flower, threescore measures of wheat, one hundred sheep, besides stags and fallow dear, bugles and fowl; four thousand stalls of horses he had for his chariots and other uses, and for the twelve thousand horsemen of his guard. For, the ten thousand stalls in the first of Kings the fourth, are to be taken but for So many horses, whence in the second of Chronicles the ninth, it is written but four thousand stalls or teems, and in every teem ten horses, as Junius *Iunius* and the Geneva understand it. He was said to be wiser than any man, yea than were Ethan the Ezrahite, than *Heman, Calcol* *Chalcal*, or than Darda, to which Junius adds a fifth *fift*, to wit, Ezrahite *Ezrak*. For the Geneva makes Ethan an Ezrahite by Nation. Josephus writes them *Ethan* *Athan**, Heman* *Aeman**, Calcol *and Darda *Donan* the sons of Mahol *Hemon*. He spoke three thousand Proverbs *Prouerbes*, and his songs were one thousand and five, whereof either the most part perished in the 〈◊ 〉 of Babylon, or else because many acts of Salomons were written and kept among the public Records of Civil causes and not Ecclesiastical, therefore they were not thought necessary to be inserted into Gods book.
