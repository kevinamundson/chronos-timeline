§. VI. *THE TRIBE OF Issachar* *JSACHAR*.

## p02133 · sig. 228b

The next adjoining Territory to Zebulun *Zabulon*, to the South and Southwest, was Issachar, who inhabited a part of the nether Galilee, within Jordan: of whom there were increased in Egypt *Aegypt*, as appeared by their musters at Mount Sinai 54400. able and warlike men, who leaving their bodies with the rest in the Deserts *Desarts*, there entered the Holy Land, 64300.

## p02134 · sig. 228b

The first City of this Tribe near the Sea of Galilee, was Tarichea, distant from Tiberias eight English mile, or somewhat more, a City wherein the Jews *Iewes*( by the practice of a certain mutinous upstart, John *Iohn* the son of Levi *Leui*) took arms against Josephus *Iosephus* the Historian, then Governor of both Galilees. This City was first taken by Cassius, and 3000. Jews carried thence captive; and afterward with great difficulty by Vespasian: who entered it by the Sea side, having first beaten the Jews in a sea fight upon the Lake or Sea of Galilee: he put to the sword all sorts of people, and of all ages: saving that his fury being quenched with the Rivers *Riuers* of blood running through every street, he reserved the remainder for slaves and bond-men.

## p02135 · sig. 228b

Next to Tarichea is placed Cession, or Kishion *Cishton*, of the Levites *Leuites*, and then Issachar, remembered in the first of *Kings, c*. 4. v. 17. then Ebez *Abes* or *Ebez, Josh.*. 19. 20. and Remeth of which Josh.. 19. 21. otherwise Ramoth. 1. Chron. 6. 73. or *Jarmuth* *Iarmuth**, Josh.*. 21. 29. this also was a City of the Levites, from whose Territory the Mountains *Mountaines* of Gilboe take beginning: and range themselves to the Mediterranean *Mediterran* Sea, and towards the West as far as the City of Jezreel *Iezrael*, between which and Ramoth, are the Cities of Beth-pazzez *Bethpheses*, or Beth-pazzez, according to Ziegler, and 〈◊ 〉, or Hen-chadda: near which Saul slew himself: under those Aphec or Aphek *Apheca*, which Adrichem *Adrichomius* places in Issachar: between which and 〈◊ 〉, he says, that the Philistines *Philistims* encamped against Israel, and afterward against Saul: a Land thirsty of blood, for herein also, says he, the Syrians with two and thirty Reguli assisting Benhadad, encountered Ahab *Achab*: and were overthrown and slaughtered: to whom the King of Israel made a most memorable answer, when 〈◊ 〉 vaunted before the victory: which was, Tell, BENHADAD, *Let not him that girds his harness boast himself, as he that puts it off: *meaning that glory followed after victory, but ought not to precede it. In the year following in the fields, as they say, adjoining to this City, was the same vain-glorious Syrian utterly broken and discomfited by Ahab: and 100000. footmen of the Aramites or Syrians slain: before which overthrow the servants and Counsellors *Counsailors* of Benhadad( in 〈◊ 〉 of the God of Israel) told him, *That the Gods of Israel were Gods of the* *Mountains: and therefore 〈◊ 〉 they fought with them in the plains, they should over-come them.*

Under Aphec towards the Sea they set the City of Esdrelon, in the plains of Galilee, called also the great field of Esdrelon, and Megiddo *Maggedo*: in the border whereof are the ruins of 〈◊ 〉 to be seen, says Brochard, and Breidenbach. After these are the Cities of 〈◊ 〉, of which, 1. Maccabees. 9. 2. Anem or Hen-En-gannim *Hen-Gannim* of the Levites, and Shahazimah *Seesima* or Shahazimah, the West border of Issachar, of which Josh.. 19. 22. From hence ranging the Seacoast, there is found the Castle of Pilgrims *Pilgrimes*: a strong Castle environed with the Sea, sometime the store-house and Magazine of the Christians, and built by the Earle of S t. Giles or Toulouse *Tolouse*.

## p02137 · sig. 229a

From the Castle of Pilgrims the Sea makes a great Bay towards the North, and the 〈◊ 〉 shore begins Mount Carmel, not far from the River Kishon *Chison*: where Elijah assembled all the Prophets, and Priests of Baal, and prayed King 〈◊ 〉 and the people assembled, to make trial whether the God of Israel, or the Idol of Baal, were to be worshipped, by laying a sacrifice without fire on the Altar: which done, the Priests of Baal prayed, and cut their own flesh after their manner, but the fire kindled not, while Elijah in derision told them that their God *was either in pursuit of his enemies not at leisure, or perchance a-sleep, & c. *but at the prayer of Elijah his fire kindled, notwithstanding that he had caused the people to cast many Vessels *Vesselis* of water thereon: by which miracle the people incensed, slew all those I do laters on the banks of Kishon adjoining.

## p02138 · sig. 229a

At the foot of this Mountain to the North stands Caiphas, built, as they say, by 〈◊ 〉 the high Priest. It is also known by the name of Porsina and Porphyria, sometime a Suffragan Bishops seat. Returning again from the Seacoast towards Tiberias by the banks of Kishon, there are found the City of Hapharaim or Hapharaim *Aphraim*, and the Castles of Mesra, and Sheba *Saba*: of which Brochard and Breidenbach: and then Naim on the River Kishon: a beautiful City while it stood, in the Gates whereof Christ raised from death the widows only son.

Then Shihon *Seon* or Shihon named Joshua *Iosua* 19. between the two Hills of Hermon, in Issachar: beyond it stands Endor, famous by reason of the Enchantress that undertook to raise up the body of Samuel at the instigation of Saul.

## p02140 · sig. 229a

Beyond it stands Anaharath and Rabbith named Josh.. c. 19. v. 19. 20. Then 〈◊ 〉 as it is named, Josh.. 21. 28. or Daberath *Dobratha*, as it is named, 1. Chron. 6. 72. This City( which stretches it self over Kishon) was a City of refuge belonging to the Levites.

## p02141 · sig. 229a

Next to Daberath is Arbela situate, near the Causes of those two 〈◊ 〉 which So greatly molested Galilee in Herod *Herodes* time. It joins on one side to the Mountain of Issachar or Hermon, and on the other to the Valley of Jezreel: which valley continues it self from Beth-shean *Bethsan* or Scythopolis, the East border of Issachar, even to the Mediterranean Sea: two parts whereof are enclosed by the Mountains of Gilboe on the South, and by Hermon, and the River Kishon on the North. In these * plains Gideon *Gedeon* overthrew the Midianites *Madianites*, and herein, they think, Saul fought against the Philistines: 〈◊ 〉 against the Syrians, and the Tartars against the Saracens.
