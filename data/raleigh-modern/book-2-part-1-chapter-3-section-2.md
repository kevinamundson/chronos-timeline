§. II. *Of divers Cities and places in Egypt* *Aegypt**, mentioned in this Story, or elsewhere in the Scripture.*

## p01653 · sig. 174a

This City which the Hebrews *Hebrewes* call Zoan, was built seven years after *Hebron. Ezekiel *calls it Taphnes; and So does Jeremiah *Hieremie*; the Septuagint, *Tanis; Josephus* *Iosephus**, Protaidis*; after the name of an Egyptian *Aegyptian* Queen; Antonius gives it the name of *Tanis* *Thanis**; Hegesippus, Thamna*; and William *Tyrius, Tapius*. It adjoins to the land of Gosen, and is the same, wherein Jeremiah the Prophet was stoned to death, for preaching against the Egyptian and Jewish Idolatry.

## p01654 · sig. 174a

Zoan or Tanis was in Moses time the Metropolis of the lower Egypt, in which their Pharaohs then commonly resided; and not unlikely to be the same City, where Abraham in his time found him. But Eusebius out of Artapanus affirms, that Abraham read Astronomy in Heliopolis, or On, to Pharetates King of *Egypt. Alex. Polyhistor*. out of Eupolemus has it otherwise, saying, that Abraham instructed the Egyptian Priests, and not the King; both which authorities Eusebius cites. The Septuagint, and the Vulgar edition, for Zoan write *Heliopolis. Pagnin, Vatablus, Junius* *Iunius**, *and our English call it On; and *Ptolemy* *Ptolomie**, Onium*. There are two Cities of that name; the one on the frontier of the lower Egypt, towards the South; the other some what lower on the Easter-most branch of Nile *Nilus* falling into the Sea at Pelusium *Peleusium*. And it it may be that Heliopolis to the South of the river Tratan, was the same which Vatablus and our English call Aven *Auen*. Of the latter it is, that the Scriptures take certain knowledge: the same, which *Pomp. Mela*, and Pliny *Plinie*, call *Solis opptdum; Tyrius* ("the city of the Sun; Tyrius [says so];")* *in the *Holy War, Malbec*; the *〈◊ 〉, Bahalbeth*; and *〈◊ 〉 Sethi, Fons Solis. *Of this Heliopolis, or On, was 〈◊ 〉 Priest, or Prince, whose daughter Joseph *Ioseph* married. In the Territory adjoining Jacob *Iacob* inhabited, while he lived in Egypt. In the confines of this City, Onias, the high Priest of the Jews *Iewes*, built a Temple, dedicated to the eternal God; not much inferior to that of Jerusalem *Hierusalem*( Ptolemy Philopater then governing in Egypt) which stood to the time of Vespasian, 333. years after the foundation by Onias, whom Josephus falsely reports, herein to have fulfilled a prophecy of *〈◊ 〉. c*. 19. *In die illa erit Altare Domini in medio terrae Aegypti; In that day shall the Altar of the Lord be in the midst of the Land of Egypt. Antiochus Epiphanes *at that time of the building tyrannizing over the Jews, gave the occasion 〈◊ 〉 the erecting of this Temple in Egypt. Lastly, there it was that out Saviour CHRIST JESUS *IESVS* remained, while Joseph and the Virgin Mary feared the violence of Herod: near which( says Brochard) the fountain is still found, called Jesus well, whose streams do afterward water the Gardens of Balsamum, no where else found in Egypt. And hereof see more in Brochard, in his description of Egypt.

## p01655 · sig. 174b

There is also the City of Noph, remembered by Isa *Esai*.. Ezekiel; the same which 〈◊ 〉 the Prophet calls Moph: which latter name it took from a Mountain adjoining, So called, which Mountain Herodotus remembers. And this is that great City, which was called Memphis; and So the Septuagint write it. It is known to the Arabians by the name of Mazar. The Chaldeans name it Alchabyr; and *Tudalensis, Mizraim*.

## p01656 · sig. 174b

Pelusium, which *Vatablus, Pagnin, Junius*, and our English write, Sin; the 〈◊ 〉 call Sais; and *Montanus, Lebna*; is not the same with Damietta *Damiata*, as Gul Tyrius witnesses. In the time of Baldwin the third, Pelusium was called *Belbeis. Belbeis*( says *Tyrius) quae 〈◊ 〉 dicta est Pelusium; Belbeis, that in times past was called Pelusium.*

## p01657 · sig. 174b

The City of No, the Septuagint call Diospolis. Of which name there are two or three in *Egypt. Jerome* *Hierome** *converts it Alexandria, by anticipation, because it was So called in the future.

## p01659 · sig. 174b

To make the story the more perceivable, I have added a description of the land of Gosen, in which the Israelites inhabited; with those Cities and places So often remembered in the Scripture: as of Taphnes or *Zoan, Heliopolis *or *Bethsemes, Baal-Zephon* *Balsephon**, Succoth*, and the rest; together with Moses passage through the Deserts *Desarts* of *Arabia the Stony*. For all story without the knowledge of the places, wherein the actions were performed, as it wants a great part of the pleasure; So it no way enriches the knowledge and understanding of the Reader; neither does any thing serve to retain, what we read, in our memories, So well as these pictures and descriptions do. In which respect I am driven to digress in many places, and to interpose some such 〈◊ 〉, other wise seeming impertinent: taking for my authority, after many others more ancient, that great learned man, Arias Montanus; who in his Preface to the Story of the Holy Land, has these words: *Si enim absque locorum obseruatione res gestae narrentur, 〈◊ 〉 sine 〈◊ 〉 cognitione 〈◊ 〉 legantur, 〈◊ 〉 confusa 〈◊ 〉 perturbata erunt 〈◊ 〉, ut ex ijs nihil non obscurum, nihil non difficile elici possit; If narration* ("For if deeds are narrated without observation of places, [illegible] without [illegible] knowledge [illegible] they are read, [illegible] confused [illegible] disturbed they will be [illegible], so that nothing but what is obscure, nothing but what is difficult, can be drawn from them.")( says he) *be made of those things which are performed, without the observation of the places, wherein they were done: or if Histories be read without Topographical knowledge; all things will appear So intricate and confused, as we shall thereby under stand nothing but obscurely, nor* *draw thence any knowledge, but with the greatest difficulty.*
