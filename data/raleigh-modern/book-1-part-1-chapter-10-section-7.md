§. VII. *Of the worshipping of Images begun from *BELUS *BELVS* in Babel.

## p01445 · sig. 146a

AS for the Babylonian Belus, he was the most ancient Belus, and the Inventor of Astronomy, if Pliny *Plinie* say true: from whence the Egyptians *Aegyptians* might borrow both the name and the Doctrine. Some part of the Temple, in which his Statue or Image was honoured as a God, the same Author affirms that it remained in his time.

## p01446 · sig. 146a

Of the Sepulchre of *Belus, Strabo *writes thus. *Over the River*( says he) there *are Gardens, where they say the ruins of *BELUS *his Tomb, which *XERXES *brake up, are yet remaining. It was a square Pyramis made of Brick, a furlong high, and on* *every side it had a furlong in breadth. *It appears by Cyril against 〈◊ 〉, that he obtained divine worship yet living: for So he writes of him( calling him ARBELUS *ARBELVS*. ) ARBELUS, *vir superbus & arrogans, primus hominum dicitur a subditis Deitatis nomen accepisse: perseverarunt igitur Assyrij, & 〈◊ 〉 illis gentes sacrificants ei* ("a proud and arrogant man, the first of men is said to have received the name of Godhead from his subjects: the Assyrians therefore continued, and [illegible] the nations sacrificing to him.")*.* 〈◊ 〉, *a man very proud and arrogant, is accounted to be the first of all men that was ever honoured by their subjects with title of Deity*; ( or with the name of God. ) *The Assyrians therefore and the bordering Nations have persevered, sacrificing to him. *Even Arius also, whom Suidas calls Thuras, who succeeded next after Ninyas, was made an idol-god *Idollgod* among them, if we credit Suidas ("Suidas believes [it]").

## p01447 · sig. 146b

After Ninus( that is, after *Ninyas) Thuras *reigned( says Suidas) whom they called after the name of the Planet Mars; a man of sharp and fierce disposition, who bidding battle to Caucasus of the Stock of Japheth *Iapheth* slue him. The Assyrians worshipped him for their God, and called him Baal( that is) Mars; thus far Suidas. Neither is it unlikely but that many among Idolatrous Nations were Deified in their life-times, or soon after: though I deny not but that the most of their Images and Statuae were first erected without divine worship, only in memory of the glorious acts of Benefactors, as Glycas rightly conceives; and So afterward the Devil crept into those wooden and brazen carcasses, when Posterity had lost the memory of their first invention. Hereof Isidore speaks in this manner: *Quos autem Pagani Deos 〈◊ 〉 homines fuerunt, & pro uniuscuiasque vitae meritis vel magnificentia, 〈◊ 〉 apud suos post mortem coeperunt: sed( Daemonibus persuadentibus) quos illipro sua memoria 〈◊ 〉 averunt, minors Deos existimarunt: ad istavero magis excolenda accesserunt Poetarum figmenta; They were men*( says he) *whom the Pagans affirmed to be Gods: and every one for his merits or magnificence began after his death to be honoured of his own.* *But at length( the Devils* *Deuils** persuading) they accounted them lesser Gods, whose memories, they honoured: and the Fictions of the Poets made the opinions( concerning the honours of the dead) much more superstitious.*

## p01448 · sig. 146b

And that the worshipping of Images was brought in by the Pagans, and Heathen Nations, it is not Isidore alone that witnesses; but *Gregory* *Gregorie**: Gentilitas*( says he) *inuentrix & caput est imaginum; Gentilism is the inventress and ground of Images: *and *Ambrose; Gentes lignum adorant, tanquam imaginem Dei* ("the image of God")*; The Gentiles adore wood as it* *were the Image of God. Eusebius *also affirms as much, and calls the worshipping of Images a custom borrowed of the Heathen. The like has Saint Augustine against ADIMANTUS *ADIMANTVS*. Et verentur( says Lactantius *LACTANTIVS*) *nereligio vana sit, sinibil* *videant quod adorent; They fear their Religion would be vain, should they not see that which they worship.*

## p01449 · sig. 146b

And( out of doubt) the Schoolmen *Schoolemen* shift this fearful custom very strangely. For seeing the very workmanship is forbidden, how can the heart of a wise Christian satisfy it self with the distinction of Doulia and Hyperdoulia, which can imply nothing but some difference of worshipping of those Images after they are made? And it is of all things the most strange, why religious and learned men should strain their wits to defend the use of those things, which the Scriptures have not only no where warranted, but expressly in many places forbidden, and cursed the Practitioners *Practisers* thereof. Yet this doctrine of the Devil was So strongly and subtly rooted, as neither the express Commandment of God himself, *Thou shalt not make any graven Image, *nor all the threatenings of Moses and the Prophets after him could remove, weed it, or by fear, or by any persuasions lead the hearts of men from it. For where shall we find words of greater weight, or of plainer instruction then these? *Take therefore good heed to your selves( for ye saw no Image in the day that the Lord spoke unto you in Horeb out of the midst of the fire) that ye corrupt not your selves; and make you a graven Image, or representation of any Figure, whether it be the likeness of Male or Female.*

## p01450 · sig. 146b

And besides the express Commandment, *Thou shalt make thee no graven Image, *and the prohibition in many Scriptures, So it is written in the Book of Wisdom, That *the invention of Idols was the beginning of Whoredom: and the finding of them the corruption of life: for they were not from the beginning, neither shall they continue for ever.*

## p01451 · sig. 146b

And whereas the Schoolmen affirm, that the Prophets spoke against the worshipping of the Heathen Idols, it is manifest that Moses spoke of Images of the living God, and not of Baal and the rest of that nature, *for you saw no Image*( says MOSES) *that day that the Lord spoke unto you in Horeb. *Surely it was excellently said of BASIL; *Noli aliquam in illo formam imaginari, ne circumscrib as eum mente 〈◊ 〉: Do not imagine any form to be in God, lest thou limit or circumscribe him in thy mind too. *Now, if the great Basil thought it a presumption unlawful to represent a pattern of the infinite God to our own thoughts and minds, how far do those men presume that put him under the greasy Pencil of a Painter, or the rusty Axe or other Instrument of a Carpenter or Carver.

## p01452 · sig. 147a

For as this dishonour to the infinite and incomprehensible God began in Babel: So did the Devil transport and speed this invention into all the Regions adjoining, and into Egypt *Aegypt* and Greece.

## p01453 · sig. 147a

The Romans *Romanes* for a while resisted the erection of these Idols and Images, refusing to set them in their Temples for 170. years, observing therein the Law of 〈◊ 〉: who thought it impiety to resemble things most beautiful, by things most base. 〈◊ 〉 Tarquinius Priscus afterwards prevailing, and following the vanity of the 〈◊ 〉( a Nation of all others under the Sun most deluded by Satan) setup the Images of their Gods; which( as Saint Augustine witnesses) that 〈◊ 〉 Varro both bewailed, and utterly condemned: and which Seneca thus derides; *Simulachra deorum venerantur, illis supplicant, genu posit illa adorant, & cum haec suspiciant, fabros* ("They worship images of the gods, they supplicate them, they adore them with bended knee, and when they look upon these things, the makers…") *qui illa 〈◊ 〉 contemnunt; The Images of the Gods are worshipped, those they pray unto with bent knees; those they adore, and while they So greatly admire them, they 〈◊ 〉 the Handi-craftsmen that made them: *which also Sedulius the Poet in this sort scoffs at:

## p01454 · sig. 147a

And though this device was barbarous, and first, and many years practised by Heathen Nations only, till the Jews *Iewes* were corrupted in Egypt, yet it is not 〈◊ 〉 alone that laughs to scorn the ignorant stupidity of his Nation: but Justin *Iustin* 〈◊ 〉 remembers how the Sibyls inveighed against Images: and Hospinian, how Sophocles taught, that it was 〈◊ 〉 to the souls of men to erect and adore those Babels. Strabo and Herodotus witness, that the Persians did not erect or set up any Statue of their Gods. Lycurgus never taught it the Lacedoemonians, but thought it impiety to represent immortal natures by mortal Figures. Eusebius also witnesses in his sixth *sixt* Book deproeparatione Evangelica *Euangelica*, that it was forbidden by a Law in 〈◊ 〉, or among the Brachmans in India, that Images should be worshipped. The same do Tacitus 〈◊ 〉 Crinitus report of the ancient Germans. Many other Authors *Authours* might be remembered that witness the disdain which the Heathen themselves had of this childish Idolatry: of which Hospinian has written at large in his Tract, *de origin imaginum* ("On the Origin of Images"). And it was truly said, *Omnia mala exempla bonis initijs orta sunt, All ill examples have sprung from good beginnings. *The Heathen at first made these Statuoe and Images, but in memory of such remarkable men, as had deserved best of their Countries and Common-wealths: Effigies hominum( says Pliny) *non 〈◊ 〉 exprimi, nisi aliqua illustri causa perpetuitatem merentium: Men were not wont to make Pictures, but of men which merited for some notable 〈◊ 〉 to be 〈◊ 〉 remembered. *And though of the more ancient Papists, some have borrowed of the Gentiles( as appears in Lactantius) that defence for Images: That Simulachra are *pro 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉, ut 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 homines 〈◊ 〉 inuisibilem cognoscere: Images*( say they, and So before them the Heathen said) *are in stead of Letters, whereby men might learn to know the invisible* God: in which understanding( perhaps they no otherwise esteemed them then Pictures indeed; yet as that of Baal or Bel set up in memory of Belus the Babylonian, became afterwards the most reverenced Idol of the World, by which So many Nations( and they which were appropriate to God himself) were misled and cast away: So those very stocks and stones, and painted Canvases *Canuases*( called the pictures of Christ, our Lady, and others) were by thousands of ignorant people, not only adored, but esteemed to have life, motion, and understanding. *On these stocks we call*( says the Book of *Wisdom) when we passe through the raging waves, on these stocks more rotten then the Ship that carries vs.*

## p01455 · sig. 147b

This Heathen invention of Images became So fruitful in after-times, breeding an infinite multitude of gods, that they were forced to distinguish them into degrees and orders; as *Dij Consents* *Consentes**, seu maiorum gentium; selecti, patrilij, insigniores, dijmedij: Counselling Gods, or Gods of the mightiest Nobility, select Gods, Patrian, Gods of mark, and common Gods*( which the Romans called *Medioxumi) dij infimi*, and terrestrial Heros *Heroes*, and multitudes of other Gods: of which Saint Augustine has made large mention in his Book *de Civitate* *Ciuitate** Dei* ("On the City of God"). But( says Lactantius) among all those miserable souls and rotten bodies, worshipped by men more like to their Idols, did Epimenides Cretensis( by what good Angel moved I know not) erect in the Athenian Fields, Altars to the unknown God, which stood with the same title and dedication even to the times of Saint Paul: who made them first know to whom those Altars belonged, and opened their eyes which were capable of grace, that they might discern the difference betwixt that light which lightens every man, and the obscure and stinking mist wherein the Devil had So many years led and misled them. And it sufficed not that the multitude of these Gods was So great in general, or that every Nation had some one which took particular and singular care of them; as Jupiter in *Creete, Isis *in Egypt, in Athens Minerva, in Samos Juno *Inno*, in Paphos Venus, and So of all other parts; but every City, and almost every Family had a God a-part. For as it is written in the second of Kings: the men of Babel, made Succoth Benoth, and the men of Cuth made Nergal, and the men of Hamath made Ashima, and the Avites *Auns* made Nibhaz and Tartak *Tarrak*, and the 〈◊ 〉 burnt their children in the fire to Adramelech. All which how plainly has the Prophet Isaiah *Esay* derided? *Men cut down Trees, rind them, burn a part of them, make ready their meat, and warm themselves by the fire thereof, and of the residue he makes a God; an Idol, and prays unto it: but God has shut their eyes from sight, and their hearts from understanding. *It is therefore safest for a Christian to believe the Commandments *Commandements* of God So direct against Idolatry, to believe the Prophets, and to believe Saint Paul: who speaks thus plainly and feelingly, *My beloved, fly from Idolatry, I speak as unto them which have understanding, judge ye what I say.*
