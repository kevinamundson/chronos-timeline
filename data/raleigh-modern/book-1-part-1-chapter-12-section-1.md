# Book I, Part I, Chapter XII, §. 1

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p01530 · sig. 155b

§. I. Of the 〈◊ 〉 building of Nineveh *Niniue* by NINUS *NINVS*: and of Babylon by SEMIRAMIS.

## p01531 · sig. 155b

But to come back to Ninus the amplifier and finisher of Nineveh: whether he performed it before or after the overthrow of Zoroaster, it is uncertain. As for the City it self, it is agreed by all profane Writers, and confirmed by the Scriptures, that it exceeded all other in circuit, and answerable magnificence. For it had in compass 440. stadia, or furlongs; the walls whereof were an hundred foot upright, and had such a breadth as three Charriots might passe on the Rampart in front: these walls were garnished with 1500. Towers *Towres* which gave exceeding beauty to the rest, and a strength no less admirable for the nature of those times.

## p01532 · sig. 155b

But this City( built in the Plains *Plaines* of Assyria, and on the banks of Tigris, and in the Region of Eden) was founded long before Ninus time; and( as ancient I 〈◊ 〉 report, and more lately Nauclerus) had the name of Campsor, at such time as Ninus amplified the same, and gave it a wall, and called it after his own name.

## p01533 · sig. 155b

For these works of Babylon and Nineveh begun by Nimrod in 〈◊ 〉, and in Assyria, Ninus and Semiramis made perfect. Ninus finished *Nineveh, Semiramis Babylon*: wherein she sought to exceed her husband by far. Indeed in the first Age when Princes were moderate, they neither thought how to invade others, nor feared to be invaded: labouring to build Towns *Townes* and Villages for the use of themselves and their people, without either Walls or Towers; and how they might discharge the earth of Woods, Briars *Bryars*, Bushments, and Waters, to make it the more habitable and fertile. But Semiramis living in that Age, when Ambition was in strong youth: and purposing to follow the conquest which her husband had under-taken, gave that beauty and strength to Babylon which it had.
