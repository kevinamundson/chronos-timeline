## p03698 · sig. 423b

§. VII. Of ARTAXERXES LONGIMANUS *LONGIMANVS*, that he was Ahasuerus *AHASHVEROSH* the husband of Queen HESTER.

## p03699 · sig. 423b

These Egyptian *Aegyptian* troubles being ended, the reign of Artaxerxes continued peaceable, whereof the length is by some restrained into twenty years, but the more and better Authors give him forty, some allow unto him four and forty. He was a Prince of much humanity, and noted for many examples of gentleness. His favour was exceeding great to the Jews *Iewes*, as appears by the Histories of Ezra *Esdras* and Nehemias, which fell in his time.

## p03700 · sig. 423b

To prove that this was the King who gave countenance and aide to that great work of building the Temple, it were a needless travail; considering that all the late Divines *Diuines* have 〈◊ 〉 very much pain, to show that those two Prophets were licensed by him, and succoured in that building, in such sort as appears in their writings.

## p03701 · sig. 423b

This was likewise that King Ahashuerosh who married Hester. Whereof if it be needful to give proof, it may suffice; That Ahashuerosh lived in Susa, reigning from India to Ethiopia *Aethiopia*, and therefore must have been a Persian; That he lived in peace, as appears by the circumstances of the History, and used the counsel of the seven Princes, the authority of which Princes began under Darius, the son of Hystaspes, wherefore he could be neither Cyrus nor Cambyses.

## p03702 · sig. 424a

The continual Wars *Warres* which exercised King Darius the son of Hystaspes, together with the certainty of his marriages with sundry wives, from none of whom he was divorced, but left his first wife Atossa, the daughter of Cyrus, alive in great honour, she being mother to Xerxes the succeeding King; do manifestly prove that Hester was not his. To which is added by Philo the Jew, That at the persuasion of *Mardochaeus, Joiakim* *Ioiachim** *the high Priest the son of Jeshua *Iesua*, caused the feast of Purim to be instituted in memory of that deliverance. Now the time of Joiakim was in the reign of Artaxerxes, at the coming of Ezra, and *Nehemias: Jeshua *his father dying about the end of Darius.

## p03703 · sig. 424a

The same continuance of wars, with other his furious and tragic loves, wherewith Xerxes did consume such little time, as he had free from war, are enough to prove, that the story of Hester pertained not unto the time of Xerxes, who lived but one and twenty years, whereas the two & thirtieth of Ahasuerus or Artaxerxes *Artasastha* is expressed by Nehemias. Again, it is well known, that Xerxes in the seventh *seuenth* year of his reign( wherein this marriage must have been celebrated) came not near to Susa. Of the Princes that succeeded Artaxerxes Longimanus, to prove that none of them could be Ahashuerosh, it is enough to say, that Mardochaeus having been carried from 〈◊ 〉 captive, with Jeconiah *Iechonia*, by Nebuchadnezzar, was unlikely to have lived unto their times.

## p03704 · sig. 424a

But of this Artaxerxes it is true, that he lived in Susa, reigned from India to Ethiopia, lived in peace, was contemporary with Joiakim the high Priest: and further he had happily by his Lieutenants reclaimed the rebellious Egyptians *Aegyptians* in that seventh year of his reign; which good fortune might well give occasion to such a Royal feast, as is described in the beginning of the book of Hester. This is the sum of the arguments, brought to prove the age of Hesters story by the learned and diligent Krentzhemius, who adds the authorities of Josephus *Iosephus*, affirming the same, and of Philo, giving to Mardochaeus eighteen years more than Isaac the Patriarch lived, namely, one hundred fourscore and eighteen years in all, which expire in the five and thirtieth of year of this Artaxerxes, if we suppose him to have been carried away captive, being a Boy of ten years old.
