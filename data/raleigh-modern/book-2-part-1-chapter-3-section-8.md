§. VIII. *Of their passage over the Red Sea: and of the Red Sea it self.*

## p01684 · sig. 179b

But Moses who feared nothing but God himself, persuaded them to be confident in his goodness, who has never abandoned those, that assuredly trust in him; using this comfortable and resolved speech: *Fear not, &c. for the Egyptians* *Aegyptians** whom ye have seen this day, ye shall* *never see them again. The Lord shall fight for you. *After which Moses calling on God for succour, received encouragement, and commandment to go on, in these words: *Wherefore cry thou unto me? speak unto the children of Israel* *that they go forward; and lift thou up thy rod, and stretch out thy hand upon the Sea, and divide it, and let the children of Israel go on dry ground through the midst of the Sea. *MOSES obeying the voice of God, in the dark of the night finding the sands uncovered, past on towards the other side and coast of Arabia: two parts of the night being spent ere he entered the Ford, which it pleased God by a forcible Eastern wind, and by Moses rod to prepare.

## p01685 · sig. 179b

Pharaoh followed him even at the heels, finding the same dry ground which Moses trod on. Therefore, as it is written: *The Angel of God which went before the Host of Israel, removed, and went behind them: also the pillar of the cloud went from before them, and stood behind them*; which is, that it pleased God therein either by his immediate power, or by the ministry of his Angel, to interpose his defence between the Hebrews *Hebrewes* and their Enemies; to the end that the Egyptians might hereby be blinded, in such sort, as they could not pursue Israel with any harmful speed. But in the morning watch Moses seized the other bank of Arabia side: and Pharaoh( as the dawn of day began to lighten the obscure air) finding a beginning of the Seas return, hasted himself towards his own Coast: But MOSES stretched forth *his hand, and the Sea returned to his force, *that is, the Sea moved by the power of God, ran back towards the land with irresistible fury and swiftness, and overwhelmed the whole Army of Pharaoh, So as not one escaped. For it is written, that *God took off their Chariot wheels, *that is, when the waters began to cover the sands, the Egyptians being stricken with fear of death, ran one athwart another, and missing the path by which they had past on after the Hebrews, their wheels stuck fast in the mud and quick-sands, and could not be drawn out: the Sea coming against them with supernatural violence.

## p01686 · sig. 180a

Lyranus upon Exod. 14. and others, following the opinions or old Traditions of the Hebrews, conceived, that after Moses had by the power of God divided the Red Sea, and that the children of Israel were fearful to enter it, Aminadab Prince or Leader of the Tribe of Judah *Iuda* first made the adventure, and that therefore was that Tribe ever after honored above the rest, according to the prophecy of Jacob *IACOB*, Gen. 49. 8. *Thy fathers sons shall bow down unto thee. *But Jerome *Hierome* upon the 11. of Hose condemns this opinion. And though it be true, that Judah had the first place in all their marches in the Desert, and, as we now call it, led the Vanguard; ( whereupon it may be inferred, that he also led the way through the Red Sea) yet that Moses himself was the Conductor of Israel at that time, it is generally received. For, as it is written in the 77. PSALM: *Thou didst lead thy people like sheep by the hand of* MOSES and AARON.

## p01687 · sig. 180a

The Hebrews have also another fancy, that the Red Sea was divided into twelve parts, and that every Tribe past over in a path apart, because it is written in the 135. Psalm, according to the vulgar: *Divisit* *Diuisit** mare rubrum in divisions; He divided the Red Sea in divisions. *Also that the bottom of the Sea became as a green Field or Pasture. But *Origen, Epiphanius, Abulensis*, and Genebrard, favouring this conceit, had forgotten to consider, that there were not twelve Pillers, nor twelve Armies of the Egyptians. It is written in the 77. *Psalm, v. *16. *Thy way is in the Sea*; not thy ways; and in the last of the book of *Wisdom, verse*. 7. *In the Red Sea there was a way.*

## p01688 · sig. 180a

Now this Sea, through which Moses past, and in which 〈◊ 〉, otherwise called Chenchres, perished in the 16. year of his reign, is commonly known by the name of the Red Sea, though the same differ nothing at all in natural colour from other waters. But as Philostratus in his third book notes, and our selves know by experience, it is of a bluish colour, as other Seas are. It enters at a narrow strait between Arabia the Happy and Ethiopia *Aethiopia*, or the land of the Abyssinians *Abysins*: the mouth of the indraught from the Cape, which Ptolemy *Ptolemie* calls Possodium, to the other Land of Ethiopia, has not above six leagues in breadth, and the same also filled everywhere with Islands *Ilands*, but afterwards it extends it self 58. Leagues from Coast to Coast, and it runs up between Arabia the Happy, and Arabia Petraea, on one side, and Ethiopia and Egypt *Aegypt* on the other, as far as Sues, the uttermost end and indraught of that Sea: where the Turk *Turke* now keeps his fleet of Galleys *Gallies*. The Cosmographers commonly give it the name of the Arabian Gulf: but the North part towards Sues, and where Moses past, is called Heropolites of the City Hero, sometime Troy, and of later times *Sues. Pliny* *Plinie** *calls it Cambisu, by which name it was known, says he, before it was called Hero many years. The Arabians call this Sea towards the North *Apocopa, Eccant*, and *Eant. Artemidorus *writes it 〈◊ 〉: King Juba Laeniticum, others more properly Elaniticus *Elaniticum*, of the Port and City 〈◊ 〉: which the Septuagint call *Elath: Ptolemy, Elana: Pliny, Laena: Josephus* *Iosephus**, Ilana, *and *Marius Niger, Aila*: there is also Ilalah in Assyria, to which Shalmaneser *Salmanassar* carried the Israclites captive, Kings 2. c. 8. v. 11. which Ilalah in Assyria, the Septuagint call Elaa: and in the first of Chron. the 5. Ala. But as for this Red Sea or the parts thereof, thus diversely named, the Moors and Arabians( Vassals to the Turk) know it by no other appellation, then the Gulf of Mecca *Meca*, after the name of Mahomets Town Mecca. The Greeks *Greekes* write it the Sea Erythraeum, of a King called Erythras, or Erythraeus: and because Erythros in the Greek *Greeke* signifies Red: hence it is, that, being denominated of this Erythraeus, the son of Perseus and Andromeda, yet it took the name of the Red Sea, as Quin. Curtius conjectures: which Arrian *Arianus* and Strabo confirm. But it seems to me by the view of a discovery of that Sea in the year 1544. performed by *Estevão* *Steaen** Gama, Viceroy *of the East India for the King of Portugal, that this Sea was So called from a reflection of redness, both from the banks, cliffs, and sands of many Islands, and part of the Continent bordering it. For I find by the report of Castro, a principal Commander under Gama( which discourse I gave Master Richard Hakluyt *Hacluit* to publish) that there is an Island called Dalaqua, sometime Leques, containing in length 〈◊ 〉 Leagues, and 12. in breadth, the earth, sands, and cliffs, of which Island, being of a reddish colour, serve for a foil to the waters about it: and make it seem altogether of the same colour. Secondly, the same Castro reports, that from 24. degrees of Septentrional Latitude, to 27.( which make in length of Coast 180. mile, lying as it does Northerly and Southerly) all the cliffs and banks are of red earth, or stone, which by reflection of the Sun-beams *Sunne-beames*, give a kind of reddish luster to the waters. Thirdly, those Portugals report, and we know it by many testimonies, that there are found in the bottom of this Sea towards the shore, great abundance of red stones, on which the greatest store of Corral grows, which is carried into most parts of Europe, and elsewhere. There are also on the Islands of this Sea many red Trees, says Strabo, and those growing under water, may also be a cause of such a colour. Of these appearances of redness by the shadows of these stones, sands, earth, and cliffs, I suppose that it first took the name of the Red Sea, because in So many places it seems to be such: which João *Iohannes* Barros in his second Decade, eight Book and first Chapter, confirms.

The breadth of this Sea from Elana, or Ezion Gaber adjoining, now Toro, called by the ancient Cosmographers Sinus Elaniticus, which washes the banks of Midian *Madian* or Midian, is for 16. or 17. Leagues together, along Northward towards Sues, some three Leagues or nine English mile over, and from this Port of Toro to Sues, and the end of this Sea it is in length about 28. Leagues, of which the first 26. have nine miles breadth, as aforesaid, and afterward the lands both from Egypt and Arabia, thrust themselves into the Sea, and straighten it So fast, as for six miles together, it is not above three mile over; from thence upward the land on Egypt side, falls away and makes a kind of Bay or Cove for some ten miles together, after which the land grows upon the Sea again, and So binds it into the very end thereof, at four miles breadth or thereabout, in which tract it was that Moses past it over, though others would have it to be over-against Elana or Toro; but without judgement: for from Ramass to Pihacheroth and Baalzephon, there is not above 30. miles interjacent, or 35. miles at most, which Moses past over in three days: and between the land of Egypt opposite to Elana or Toro, the distance is above 80. miles. For Ramass, to which City Moses came( being the Metropolis of Gosen) when he left Pharaoh *Pharao* at Zoan, and took his last leave, stands in 30. degrees five minutes of Septentrional Latitude: and Migdol, or the Valley of Pihacheroth, at the foot of the Mountain Climax, or Baal-Zephon *Baalsephon*, in nine and twenty and a half, which made a difference of five and thirty English mile, the way lying in effect North and South.
