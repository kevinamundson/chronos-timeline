# Book I, Part I, Chapter I, §. 5

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p00816 · sig. 45b

§. V. *That the substance of the waters, as mixed in the body of the earth, is by *MOSES *understood in the word Earth: and that the Earth, by the attributes of unformed and void, is described as the 〈◊ 〉 of the ancient Heathen.*

## p00817 · sig. 45b

MOSES first names Heaven and Earth( putting waters but in the third place) as comprehending waters in the word Earth, but afterwards he names them apart, when God by his Spirit began to distinguish the confused Mass, and( as Basil says) *praeparare naturam aquae ad foecunditatem 〈◊ 〉; to prepare the nature of water to a vital fruitfulness.*

## p00818 · sig. 45b

For under the word Heaven, was the matter of all heavenly bodies, and natures expressed: and by the name of *Earth and Waters*, all was meant, whatsoever is under the Moon, and subject to alteration. Corrupt seeds bring forth corrupt plants; to which the pure heavens are not subject, though subject to perishing. *They shall perish* ( says David *Dauid*) *and the heavens shall vanish away like smoke, *says Isaiah *Esay*. Neither were the waters the matter of Earth: for it is written, *Let the waters under the heavens* *be gathered into one place, and let the dry land appear: *which proves that the dry land was mixed and covered with the waters, and not yet distinguished; but no way, that the waters were the matter or seed of the Earth, much less of the 〈◊ 〉. *Initio tu Domine terram fundasti, Thou, O Lord, in the beginning hast founded the Earth: *and again, *The Earth was covered with the Deep*( meaning with waters) *as with a garment*, says David. And if by natural arguments it may be proved, that water by condensation may become earth, the same reason teaches us also, that earth rarefied may become water: water, air: air, fire; and So on the contrary, *〈◊ 〉 ignis substantiam* ("[illegible] the substance of fire") *per aerem in aquam conuertit, God turns the substance of fire, by air, into water. *For the Heavens and the Earth remained in the same state, in which they were created, as touching their substance, though there was afterwards added multiplicity of perfection, in respect of beauty and ornament. *Coelum 〈◊ 〉 & terra in statu* ("Heaven [illegible] and earth in a state [illegible]")* 〈◊ 〉* *remanserunt, quantum ad substantiam, licet multiplex perfectio decoris & ornatus eis postmodum 〈◊ 〉. *And the word which the Hebrews *Hebrewes* call Maim, is not to be understood according to the Latine translation simply, and as specific water; but the same more properly signifies liquor. For( according to *Montanus) Est autem* ("Montanus: But it is [so];") *Maim liquor geminus, & hoc nomen propter verborum penuriam, Latina lingua plurali numero aquas fecit. For Maim* ("Maim is a twofold liquid, and this noun, because of the scarcity of words, the Latin language rendered as 'waters' in the plural number.")( says he) *is a double liquor*, ( that is, of divers natures) *and this name or word the Latins* *Latines** wanting a voice to express it, call it in the Plural, Aquas, Waters.*

## p00819 · sig. 45b

This Mass, or undigested matter, or Chaos created in the beginning, was without form, that is, without the proper form, which it afterwards acquired, when the Spirit of God had separated the Earth, and digested it from the waters: *And the earth* was void: that is, not producing any creatures, or adorned with any plants, fruits, or flowers. But after *the Spirit of God had moved upon the waters, *and wrought this undigested matter into that form, which it now retains, then did *the earth bud forth the herb, which seeds seed, and the fruitful tree according to his kind, and God saw that it was good*; which attribute was not given to the Earth, while it was confused, nor to the Heavens, before they had motion, and adornment. *God saw that it was good*; that is, made perfect: for perfection is that, to which nothing is wanting. *Et perfecti Dei perfecta sunt opera, The works of the perfect God, are perfect.*
