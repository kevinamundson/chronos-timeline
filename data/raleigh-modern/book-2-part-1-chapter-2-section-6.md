§. VI. Of *TYPHON, HERCULES* *HERCVLES** AEGYPTUS* *AEGYPTVS**, HORUS* *ORVS**, *and the two SESOSTRES, successively reigning after MIZRAIM: and of divers errors about the former SESOSTRES.

## p01632 · sig. 170b

Concerning the reign of Typhon, and of Hercules, I find none that precisely does define how long either of them continued. Daniel Angelocrator gives three years to Typhon, omitting Hercules. But he is So peremptory without proof, as if his own word were sufficient authority, in many points very questionable; alleging no witness, but as it were saying, *Teste me ipso*: yet herein we may think him to speak probably, inasmuch as the learned Krentzhemius affirms, that Hercules did very soon undertake his Fathers revenge: and was not long in performing it: and that leaving Egypt *Aegypt* to his brother, he followed other wars, in the same quarrel, as has been 〈◊ 〉 before. True it is, that I cannot collect( as Krentzhemius does) out of Berosus, that Hercules reigned after Typhon: yet seeing Aventinus *Auentinus* a follower of Berosus has it So, I will also believe it. That in the reign of Typhon and Hercules, seven years were spent, howsoever divided between them, I gather out of Krentzhemius only, who places the beginning of Horus seven years after the death of Osiris: forgetting to set down his reasons, which in a matter So probable I think he wanted not. Now whereas he allows 90. years of the eighteenth Dynasty to *Osiris, Typhon, Hercules*, and Horus: it seems that the reign of Horus lasted 115. years. From the death of Horus to the departure of Israel out of Egypt, there passed 122. years by our account: who( according to Beroaldo *Beroaldus* and others, ) think that Abraham was borne in the 130. year of Terah, and thereupon reckon thus. From the end of the Flood *Floud* to the birth of Abraham— 352 From that time to the vocation of Abraham— 75 departure out of Egypt— 430 Summa— 857 which sum divers other ways may be collected. Since therefore to the departure out of Egypt, there do remain( as is aforesaid) only 122. years from the death of Horus: we are now to consider how many of them are to be allowed unto 〈◊ 〉 or Sesonchosis: who is placed next unto Horus, by authority of the Scholiasts *Scholiastes* 〈◊ 〉: not without good probability. For this great king or conqueror, is by many Histories recorded to have over-run a great part of Asia: to have built a fleet of ships on the red Sea: and So to have entered into India: likewise with another fleet on the middle-earth Seas, to have passed into Europe, and subdued many Nations. This is he( as Reineccius judges) whom Justin *Iustine* erring in account of his time calls Vexoris: For Justin places Vexoris in ages before Ninus: whereby it would follow that Sesostris, if he were Vexoris, was more ancient then was Osiris( otherwise Mizraim) a thing altogether unlikely. Certain it is that after the departure of Israel out of Egypt, no one Pharaoh came into the land of Canaan( which lies in the way from Egypt into Asia) till the Fatherin law of *Solomon* *Salomon**, Pharaoh Apries* *Vaphres*, took Gerar, and gave it to his daughter: after which time Shishak *Sesac* oppressed Rehoboam, and gave it to his daughter: after which time Shishak oppressed *Rehoboam, & Necho *sought passage through the land of Israel, when he made his expedition against the Chaldeans *Chaldaeans*. Of King Apries and Necho it is out of question, that neither of them was the great King Sesostris. Of Shishak it is doubted by some, inasmuch as he came into Judea *Iudaea* with a great army. Reineccius propounding the doubt, leaves it undecided; unless it be sufficient proof of his own opinion, that he himself places Sesostris next to Horus: following the Scholiasts Apollonij. But further answer may be made to 〈◊ 〉 that they were not one. For, as Justin witnesses, Sesostris, otherwise Vexoris, made war on people far removed, abstaining from his neighbours, Shishak came up purposely against *Jerusalem* *Hierusalem**. 〈◊ 〉*, as 〈◊ 〉 witnesses, had but 24000. horse: Shishak had 60000: Sesostris had 8020. Chariots, Shishak but 1200: Sesostris made his expedition for no private purpose, but to get a great name: Shishak, as most agree, had no other purpose then to succour Jeroboam *Ieroboam*, and give him countenance in his new reign; whom he had favoured even against Solomon: therefore Sesostris must needs have reigned whilst Israel abode in Egypt.

Whereas Krentzhemius collects out of Herodotus, and Diodorus, that one Menas, or Menis, was next to Horus: because those Historians affirm that he reigned next after the Gods; it moves me nothing. For Osiris did succeed those 15. Gods, namely the twelve greater, and three lesser: himself also( as the learned Reineccius notes) being called Menas. Which name, as also Menaeus, and Menis, were titles of dignity: though mistaken by some as proper names. 〈◊ 〉 does very probably gather, that Menas was Mercury *Mercurius* Ter-maximus: the Hebrew word Meni signifying an Arithmetician: which name Ter-maximus might well be attributed to Osiris: who was a great Conqueror, Philosopher, and benefactor to mankind, by giving good Laws *Lawes*, and teaching profitable Arts *Artes*. In prowess and great undertakings Sesostris was no whit inferior to Osiris. For he sought victory not for gain, but for honour only: and being well contented, that many Nations had acknowledged his power, and submitted themselves to his will and royal disposition, leaving them in a manner to their liberty, returned into Egypt. Soon upon his return he was endangered by a great Treason, the house in which he was, being by his own brother purposely fired which nevertheless he is said to have escaped, and to have reigned in all thirty three years: after which time he chose rather to die then to live: because he fell blind. Both Herodotus, and Diodorus, affirm that Sesostris left a son, whose name was Pheron or Pheron *Pherones*: who afterwards took the name of Sesostris: but was nothing like to his Father in glory: for he shortly fell blind. The cause of his blindness Herodotus attributes to his assaulting the river Nile *Nilus* with a javelin: which tale Diodorus having likewise heard, yet reports as a fable, saying that perhaps he took the disease naturally from his Father. How long this man reigned it is no where expressed: yet inasmuch as Horus the second, ( otherwise Busiris) who succeeded him, began 14. years 〈◊ 〉 that this Sesostris had been king, it must needs be that this reigned 14. years at least. That Busiris began not until these 14. years at least were expired, the very account of time from the first of Busiris, to the departure of Israel out of Egypt plainly shows, being almost generally agreed upon, to have been 75. years. That none came between Sesostris the second and Busiris or Horus the second, it stands only upon probabilities: which are these. After Sesostris had reigned some while, he fell blind: after certain years he recovered his sight, as is said: which may have been true, but is more like to have been a fable: surely the manner of his recovery as it is set down, is very fabulous: namely that by looking upon a woman, or washing his eyes with her water, who had only known her own husband, he got his sight again. As the time of his reign, before his blindness, and when he was well again( if ever he were) may have taken up a good part of 14. years: So his works which were great, do much more strongly argue, that his reign was not very short. His words are largely set down by Herodotus, and Diodorus: a part of which may seem to have been the finishing of that which his Father had begun, about the channels and sluices of Nile: whom I think he rather frighted, ( as his Father had done) with spades and shovels, then with darts, and javelins: and by his diligent oversight of that work, was like enough to lose both his eye-sight and his peoples love: whom his Father had very busily employed in excessive labour about it.
