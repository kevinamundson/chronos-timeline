§. XIII. Of Providence:

## p00858 · sig. 52a

Now Providence( which the Greeks *Greekes* call Pronoia) is an intellectual knowledge, both fore-seeing, caring for, and ordering all things, and does not only behold all past, all present, and all to come, but is the cause of their So being, which Prescience( simply taken) is not: and therefore Providence by the Philosophers( says Saint Augustine) is divided into Memory, Knowledge, and Care: Memory of the past, Knowledge of the present, and Care of the future; and we our selves account such a man for provident, as, remembering things past, and observing things present, can by judgement, and comparing the one with the other, provide for the future, and times succeeding. That such a thing there is as Providence, the Scriptures every-where teach us, Moses in many places, the Prophets in their Predictions: Christ himself and his Apostles assure us hereof; and, besides the Scriptures, *Hermes, Orpheus, Euripides, Pythagoras, Plato, Plotinus, *and( in effect) all 〈◊ 〉 men acknowledge the Providence of God: yea, the Turks *Turkes* themselves are So 〈◊ 〉 therein, as they refuse not to accompany and visit each other, in the most 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉, nor shun any peril whatsoever, though death therein do manifestly 〈◊ 〉 it self.

The places of Scripture proving 〈◊ 〉, are So many, both in general and particular, as I shall need to repeat 〈◊ 〉 a few of them in this place. *Sing unto God*( says DAVID) *which covers the Heavens with clouds, and prepares rain for the earth,* *and makes the grass to grow upon the Mountains* *Mountaines**, which gives to beasts their food, and* *feeds the young Raven that cries: all these wait upon thee, that thou may give them* *food in due season. And thou shalt drink of the River Cherith* *Cheareth*( says God to ELIAH) *and I have commanded the Ravens to feed thee there. Behold, the Fowls* *Fowles** of the Air, they* *sow not, nor reap, and yet your heavenly Father feeds them: again, are not two Sparrows* *Sparrowes* *sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father: yea* *all the hairs of your head are 〈◊ 〉: *And Saint PETER, *Cast all your care on him, for* *he cares for you; And his judgements are written*( says DAVID. )

God therefore, who is every-where present, *who fills the Heavens and the Earth,* *whose eyes are upon the righteous, and his countenance against them that do evil, *was therefore by Orpheus called, *oculus infinitus, an infinite eye, *beholding all things, and cannot therefore be esteemed as an idle looker on, as if he had transferred his power to any other: for it is contrary to his own Word: *Gloriam meam alterinondabo: I* *will not give my glory to another. *No man commands in the Kings presence, but by the Kings direction; but God is every-where present, and King of Kings. The example of Gods universal Providence is seen in his creatures. The Father provides for his children: beasts and birds and all livings for their young ones. If Providence be found in second Fathers, much more in the first and Universal: and if there be a natural loving care in men, and beasts, much more in God, who has formed this nature, and whose Divine love was the beginning, and is the bond of the Universal: *Amor divinus rerum omnium est principium, & vinculum univer si* ("Divine love is the principle and bond of all things.")( says PLATO. ) *Amor Dei est nodus perpetuus, 〈◊ 〉, partiumque cius immobile sustentaculum, ac universae machinae fundamentum; The love of God is the perpetual knot, and link or chain of the World, and the immovable pillar of every part thereof, and the Basis and foundation of the universal. *God therefore who could only be the cause of all, can only provide for all, and sustain all; So as to absolute power; to every-where presence; to perfect goodness; to pure and divine love; this attribute and transcendent 〈◊ 〉 of Providence is only proper and belonging.
