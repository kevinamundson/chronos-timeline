## p01122 · sig. 96b

§ IIII. *Of some other records testifying the universal Flood* *floud**: and of two ancient deluges in Egypt* *Aegypt**: and of some elsewhere.*

## p01123 · sig. 96b

Saint AVGUSTINE *AVGVSTINE* out of Varro affirms, that the Greeks *Greekes* and Latins *Latines* made not any mention of the universal Flood, because they had nothing of antiquity foregoing that of Ogyges; and therefore( according to Rhodoginus before remembered) were all things among the Greeks( which antiquity had worn out of knowledge) called Ogygia, which we in English commonly call( worm-eaten) or of defaced date. But as all the parts of the earth were successively planted and peopled; and as all Nations had their proper times, and not their beginning at once and at the instant: So did every family which afterward became a great People, with whom the knowledge of divine letters was not received, find no parent of more antiquity, then such as they had themselves, nor allow of any before their own; and as the Grecians *Graecians*, So did others vaunt themselves to be Indigenae, and growing out of the earth, or invent some other profane or ridiculous beginning. But the Chaldeans *Chaldaeans* had certain knowledge of Noahs Flood, as Berosus witnesses; and Nicolaus Damascenus makes particular mention thereof( as is aforesaid) though he also affirm by hear-say, that some Giants saved themselves upon the Mountains *Mountaynes* Baris in Armenia, but speaks not thereof as from any authority approved: using the word *sermoest; That such a speech there was. *And Eusebius remembers a place out of the ancient Historian Abydenus: who writes that Xisuthrus *Sissithrus*, to preserve himself from a Flood foretold him by Saturn *Saturnus*, fled to the hills of Armenia by ship, *ad Armenian navigio confugiebat*: who the third day( after the waters were fallen) sent forth birds, that finding no land to rest on, returned again; which he also did a second time, but at the third return the birds feet were covered with mud and slime. To this effect are Eusebius words out of Abydenus, which may seem a true description( though in other terms) of Noahs Flood.

## p01124 · sig. 97a

Cyril *Cyrillus* also affirms, that *Alex. Polyhistor *makes mention of this general Flood. And Plato in Timaeo produces an Egyptian *Aegyptian* Priest, who recounted to Solon out of the holy Books *Bookes* of Egypt, the story of the Flood universal, which( says he) happened long before the Grecian inundations. Fryer Annius his Xenophon remembers a third Flood, which also Diodorus Siculus confirms, somewhat more ancient then that of Ogyges in Attica. For he named the general Flood for the first, which happened( says he) under the old OGYGES, *Sub prisco Ogyge* ("Under ancient Ogyges"), which was Noah; he calls the second *Niliaca: Hercules *and Prometheus then living, 44. years before that of Attica, in the 34. year of Belochus King of the Assyrians, though I do not believe him as touching the time. But this Flood covered a great part of the nether Egypt, especially all that Region subject to Prometheus; and hereof came the fable of the Vulture on Prometheus his liver, afterward slain by Hercules of Egypt: which fiction *Diod. Siculus *delivers in these words: *Fluvium* *Fluuium** propter cursus velocitatem, profunditatemque aquarum Aquilam tunc appellatum* ("The river, on account of the swiftness of its course and the depth of its waters, was then called the Eagle,")*, *HERCULEM *HERCVLEM* *cum consilij magnitudine* ("with greatness of counsel"), *tum virtute, volunt e vestigio compressisse, & aquarum impetum ad priorem cursum conuertisse: Unde & Graeci quidam Poetae rem gestam in fabulam vertentes* ("then by valour, they willed [it] to be at once compressed, and to turn the force of the waters back to its former course: Whence also certain Greek poets, turning the deed into a fable,")*, *HERCULEM tradunt Aquilam PROMETHEI 〈◊ 〉 *depascentem occidisse; This Flood*( meaning of Nile *Nilus*) *for the swiftness of his course, as also for the depth, was in those days called the Eagle: but *HERCULES *HERCVLES* *by his great judgement and virtue did again compress and straighten this River, So far extended and over-spread, turning it into the old channels: Whence certain Greek* *Greeke** 〈◊ 〉( converting this labour and work of *HERCULES *into a fable) devised that *HERCULES *slue the Eagle, which fed on *PROMETHEVS liver; meaning that he delivered Prometheus of that sorrow and torment, which for the loss of his People and Country( by the waters destroyed and covered over) he suffered.

## p01125 · sig. 97a

A fourth Flood chanced about Pharus in Egypt, where Alexander Macedon built Alexandria, as Annius conceives out of his Xenophon, who in this brief sort writes of all these inundations: *Inundations* *Inundationes** plures fuere: prima novimestr is inundatio* ("There were several floods: the first was a nine-month flood") *terrarum sub prisco* ("of the lands under ancient [Ogyges];")* *OGYGE: *secunda Niliaca, &c. There were many inundations*( says this same Xenophon) *the first which was universal of nine Months* *Moneths**, and this happened under the first *OGYGES: *the second was Niliaca*, and of one Months continuance in the time of Hercules and Prometheus Egyptians *Aegyptians*: a third of two Months under Ogyges Atticus: the fourth of three Months in Thessalia under Deucalion *Deucoelion*: and a fifth *fift* of the like continuance( called Pharonica) under Proteus of Egypt, about the time of Helens rape. Diodorus in his fifth Book and eleventh Chapter, taking the Samothracians *Samothraces* for his Authors, remembers a Flood in Asia the less and elsewhere, of no less destruction then any of the other particular inundations, saying, that the same happened before that of Deucalion: the Sea of Pontus and Hellespont breaking in over the land.

## p01126 · sig. 97b

But there have been many Floods in divers times, and ages, not inferior to any of these two last remembered, 〈◊ 〉 and Pharonica in Egypt: as in the year of our Redemption 590. when in October of the same year, Gregory *Gregorie* then being Bishop of Rome, there happened a marvellous overflowing in Italy *Italie*, and especially in the Venetian Territory, and in Liguria *Lyguria*, accompanied with a most fearful storm of thunder and lightning: after which followed the great plague at Rome, by reason of the many dead Serpents cast up and left upon the Land, after the waters decreased and returned. And in the year 1446. there perished 10000. people, by the breaking in of the Sea at Dordrecht *Dordroch* in Holland: of which kind I take that Flood to be of Achaia or Attica. Before that and in the year 1238. Trithemius speaks of an earthquake which swallowed many thousands: and after that of a Flood in Friesland *Friseland*, in which there perished 100000. persons. Strozius Sigog. in his Magia omnifaria, tells of an inundation in Italy, in the time of Pope Damasus, in which also many Cities of Sicily *Sicil* were swallowed: another in the Papacy of Alexander the sixth: also in the year 1515. Maximilian being Emperor. He also remembers a perilous over-flowing in Polonia, about Kraków *Cracouia*, by which many people perished. Likewise Vigenère *Viginier* a French Historian speaks of a great Flood in the South part of 〈◊ 〉, which fell in the year of our Lord 1557, with So dreadful a tempest, as all the people attended therein the very end of the World, and judgement day; saying, That by the violent descent of the waters from the Mountains, about Nîmes *Nismes* there were removed divers old heaps and mounds of ground, and many other places torn up and rent: by which accident there was found both coin of silver and gold, divers pieces of plate, and vessels of other metal, supposed to be hidden at such time as the Goths invaded that Province, in the year 1156.
