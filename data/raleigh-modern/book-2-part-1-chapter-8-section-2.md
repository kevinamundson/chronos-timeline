## p02188 · sig. 233a

§. II. *Of the Kings of Tyre*.

## p02189 · sig. 233a

But whatsoever remains of the story and Kings of Phoenicia( the books of *Zeno, Sanchuniathon* *Sachoniatho**, Mnaseas*, and others of that Nation, being no where found) the same is to be gathered out of the Scriptures, Josephus *Iosephus*, and Theophilus Antiochenus.

## p02190 · sig. 233a

Agenor lived at once with Joshua *Iosua*, to whom succeeded Phoenix, of whom that part of Canaan, and So far towards the North as Aradus, took the name of Phoenicia: what King succeeded Phoenix it does not appear: but at such time as the Grecians *Graecians* besieged *Troy, Phasis *governed Phoenicia.

## p02191 · sig. 233a

In Hieremies time and while Jehoiakim *Iehotakim* ruled in Judah *Iuda*, the Tyrians had a King apart: for Jeremiah *Hieremie* speaks of the Kings of Sidon *Zidon*, of Tyre, of *Edom, & c*. as of several Kings.

## p02192 · sig. 233a

In Xerxes time, and when he prepared that incredible Army wherewith he invaded *Greece, Tetramnestus *ruled that part of Phoenicia, about Tyre, and Sidon: who commanded, as some Writers affirm, Xerxes fleet, or rather, as I suppose, those 300. Galleys *Gallies*, which himself brought to his aide: for at this time it seems, that the Phoenicians were Tributaries to the Persian: for being broken into Reguli, and petty Kings in Hieremies time, they were subjected by Nebuchadnezzar *Nabuchodonosor*; of whose conquests in the Chapter before remembered, Jeremiah prophesied.

## p02193 · sig. 233a

Tennes, though not immediately, succeeded Tetramnestus: remembered by D. Siculus in his 14. Book.

## p02194 · sig. 233a

Strato, his successor, and King of *Sidon, Alexander Macedon *threw out, because of his dependency upon Darius, and that his Predecessors had served the East Empire against the Grecians. But divers Kings, of whom there is no memory, came between Tennes and Strato. For there were consumed 130. years and somewhat more between Xerxes and Alexander Macedon. And this man was by Alexander esteemed the more unworthy of restitution, because( says Curtius) he rather submitted himself by the instigation of his Subjects *Subiects*( who foresaw their utter ruin by resistance) than that he had any disposition thereunto, or bare any good affection towards the Macedonians *Maecedonians*.

## p02195 · sig. 233b

Of this *Strato, Athenaeus *out of Theopompus reports, that he was a man of ill living: and most voluptuous; also that he appointed certain games and prizes for Women dancers, and singers: whom he to this end chiefly invited, and assembled: that having beheld the most beautiful and lively among them, he might recover them for his own use and delights. Of the strange accident about the death of one Strato King of these coasts, S. Jerome *Hierome* and others make mention: who having heard that the Persians were near him with an Army too weighty for his strength, and finding that he was to hope for little grace, because of his falling away from that Empire, and his adhering to the Egyptians *Aegyptians*: he determining to kill himself, but fainting in the 〈◊ 〉, his wife being present wrested the sword out of his hand and slew him: which done she also therewith pierced her own body, and died.

## p02196 · sig. 233b

After Alexander was possessed of Sidon, and the other Strato *Sprato* driven thence, He gave the Kingdom to Hephaestion, to dispose of: who having received great entertainment of one of the Citizens, in whose house he lodged, offered to recompense him therewith; and willingly offered to establish him therein: but this Citizen no less virtuous than rich, desired Hephaestion that this Honour might be inferred on some one of the blood and race of their ancient Kings: and presented unto him Abdalonymus *Balonymus*, whom Curtius calls Abdalonymus, JUSTIN *IVSTINE* Abdalonymus, and Plutarch Abdalonymus: who at the very hour that he was called to this regal Estate, was with his own hands working in his Garden, setting herbs, and roots, for his relief and sustenance: though otherwise a wise man and exceeding just.

## p02197 · sig. 233b

These were the ancient Kings of Sidon: whose estate being afterward changed into Popular or Aristocratical: and by times and turns subjected to the Emperors *Emperours* of the East: there remains no farther memory of them, than that which is formerly delivered in the Tribe of Asher *Asser*.

## p02198 · sig. 233b

The Kings of Tyre, who they were before Samuels time, it does not appear: Josephus the Historian, as is said, had many things wherewith he garnished his Antiquities from the Tyrian Chronicles: and out of Josephus, and Theophilus Antiochenus, there may be gathered a descent of some twenty Kings of the Tyrians, but these Authors, though they both pretend to write out of Menander of Ephesus *Ephelius*, do in no sort agree in the times of their reigns; nor in other particulars.

## p02199 · sig. 233b

Abibalus is the first King of the Tyrians, that Josephus and Theophilus remember: whom Theophilus *Theophtlus* calls Abemalus: the same perchance that the son of Sirach mentions in his forty and sixth Chapter, speaking of the Princes of the Tyrians.

## p02200 · sig. 233b

To this *Abibalus, Suron *succeeded, if he be not one and the same with *Abibalus. David* *Dauid*( says Eusebius out of Eupolemus) constrained this Suron to pay him Tribute, of whom also David complains Psal. 83.

## p02201 · sig. 233b

Hiram succeeded Suron, whom Josephus calls Irom, and Theophilus sometime Hiram *Hieromenus*, sometime Hiram, but Tatian and Zonaras Hiram. He entered into a league with David, and sent him Cedars with Masons, and Carpenters, to perform his buildings in Jerusalem *Hierusalem*: after he had beaten thence the Jebusites *Iebusites*. The same was he that So greatly assisted Solomon *Salomon*: whom he not only furnished with Cedars, and other Materials *Materialls* towards the raising of the Temple, and with great sums of money, but also he joined with him in his enterprise of the East India, and of Ophir: and furnished Solomon with Mariners and Pilots: the Tyrians being of all Nations the most excellent Navigators *Nauigators*: and lent him 120. talents of gold. Of this Hiram there is not only mention in divers places of Scripture, but in Josephus in his Antiquities the 7. and 8. chap. 2. & 3. in Theophilus his 3 d ^ book, in Tatianus his Oration against the Greeks *Greekes*: and in Zonaras Tome the first. This Prince seemed to be very mighty and magnificent, he despised the 20. towns which Solomon offered him: he defended himself against that victorious King David 〈◊ 〉 gave his Daughter in marriage to Solomon, called the Zidonian: for whose sake he was contented to worship Ashtaroth *Asteroth*, the Idol of the *Phoenicians. Hiram *lived 53. years.

## p02202 · sig. 234a

Baleastartus whom *Theophilus. Antiochenus*, calls Baleazarus *Bazorus*, succeeded Hiram, King of Tyre and Sidon, and reigned 7. years according to Josephus.

## p02203 · sig. 234a

Abdastartus the eldest son of Baleastartus, governed 9. years, and lived but 20. years according to Josephus: but after 〈◊ 〉 he reigned 12. year, and lived 54. who being slain by the four sons of his own Nurse, the eldest of them held the Kingdom 12. years.

## p02204 · sig. 234a

Astartus brother to Abdastartus, recovered the Kingdom from this Usurper, and reigned 12. years.

## p02205 · sig. 234a

Astarimus, or Astarimus *Atharimus*, after Theophilus, a third brother followed Astartus, and ruled 9. years, and lived in all 54.

## p02206 · sig. 234a

Phelles the fourth son of Baleastartus, and brother to the three former Kings, slew Astarimus, and reigned 8. Months *Moneths*; lived 50. years.

## p02207 · sig. 234a

Ithobalus( or Ithobalus *Iuthobalus*, in Theophilus) son to the third brother Astarimus, who was the chief Priest of the Goddess Astarta, which was a dignity next unto the King, revenged the death of his father, and slaughtered his Uncle Phelles: and reigned 32. years, the same which in the first of Kings chap. 16. is called Ethbaal, whose Daughter *Jezebel, Ahab* *Achab** *married.

## p02208 · sig. 234a

Badezor or Badezor *Bazor* the son of Ithobalus or Ethbaal, brother to Jezebel, succeeded his Father, and reigned 6. years, and lived in all 45.

## p02209 · sig. 234a

Mettimus succeeded Badezor, and reigned but 9. years, ( says Josephus) he had two sons Pygmalion and Barca, and two Daughters Elisa and Anna.

## p02210 · sig. 234a

Pygmalion reigned after Mettimus his Father 40. years, and lived 56: In the 7. year of whose reign, Elisa sailed into Affrica, and built Carthago, 143. years and 8. Months, after the Temple of Solomon: which by our account was 289. years after Troy taken, and 143. before Rome: and therefore that fiction by Virgil of Aeneas and Dido must be far out of square. For Pygmalion covetous of Sychaeus *Sicheus* his riches, who had married his sister Elisa, slew him traitorously as he accompanied him in hunting: or if we believe Justin and Virgil, at the Altar: whereupon Elisa fearing to be despoiled of her husbands treasure, fled by Sea into Affrica as aforesaid: whom when Pygmalion prepared to pursue, he was by his Mothers tears, and by threats from the Oracle arrested. Barca accompanied his sister, and assisted her, in the erection of Carthage: and from him sprang that noble Family of the Barcids *Barcae* in Affrica, of which race descended many famous Captains *Captaines*, and the great *Hannibal. Servius* *Seruins** *interprets this name of Dido by Virago, because of her man-like acts, others from Jedidiah *Iedidia*, a surname of Solomon.

## p02211 · sig. 234a

Eluleus succeeded Pygmalion: and reigned 36. years: the same that overthrew the fleet of Shalmaneser *Salmanassar*, in the Port of Tyre: notwithstanding which he continued his siege before it on the Land side five years, but in vain.

## p02212 · sig. 234a

After *Eluleus, Ethobales *governed the Tyrians, who vaunted himself to be as wise as Daniel: and that he knew all secrets( says Ezekiel) of whom the Prophet writes at large in his 28. Chapter: out of whom it is gathered, that this Prince died, or was slain in that long siege of Nebuchadnezzar: who surrounded and attempted Tyre 13: years together, ere he prevailed.

## p02213 · sig. 234a

Baal followed Ethobales, and reigned 10. years a tributary, perchance, to Nebuchadnezzar: for after his death it was governed by divers Judges *Iudges*, succeeding each other: First, by Ecnibalus, then by *Chelbis, Abarus *the Priest, Mittonus, and Gerastus, who held it among them some 7. years, and odd Months: after whom Balatorus commanded therein as a King for one year: after him Merbalus sent from Babylon 4. years: after him Irom sent thence also, 20. years. In the 17. of whose reign Cyrus began to govern Persia.
