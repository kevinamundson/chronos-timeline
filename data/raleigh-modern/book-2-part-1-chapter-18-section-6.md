§. VI. Of the Contemporaries of Solomon *SALOMON*.

## p02804 · sig. 307a

〈◊ 〉 the beginning of Salomons reign, 〈◊ 〉 the third of the Heraclidae in *Corinth; Labotas* *Labotes** *in Lacedaemon; and soon after Silvius *Syluius* Alba the fourth of the 〈◊ 〉, swayed those Kingdoms *Kingdomes*: Laosthenes then governing *Assyria: Agastus *and 〈◊ 〉 the second and third Princes after Codrus ruling the Athenians.

## p02805 · sig. 307a

In the six and twentieth of Salomons reign Hiram of Tyre died, to whom Baleazarus *Baliastrus* succeeded, and reigned seventeen years, after Mercator's *Mercators* account, who reckons the time of his rule by the age of his sons. Josephus *Iosephus* gives him fewer years. Theophilus Antiochenus against Autolicus finds Bozorius the next after Hiram, if there be not some Kings omitted between the death of Hiram, and the reign of Bozorius.

## p02806 · sig. 307a

Apries *Vaphres* being dead, about the twentieth of *Solomon, Shishak* *Sesac** *or Shishak( as our English 〈◊ 〉 terms him) began to govern in Egypt *Aegypt*, being the same with him whom Diodorus calls *〈◊ 〉; Josephus, 〈◊ 〉; Cedrenus, Susesinus; Euscbius *in the column of the Egyptian *Aegyptian* Kings Smendes, and in that of the *Hebrews* *Hebrewes** Susac. Josephus *in the eight of his Antiquities reproves it as an error in Herodotus, that he ascribes the acts of Susac to Sesostris, which perchance Herodotus's *Herodotiss* might have done by comparison, accounting Shishak another Sesostris, for the great things he did.

## p02807 · sig. 307a

Of the great acts and virtues of King Sesostris I have spoken already in the 〈◊ 〉 of the Egyptian Princes: only in this he was reproved, that he caused four of his captive Kings to draw his Carriage, when he was disposed to be seen, and to ride in triumph: one of which four, says Eutropius, at such time as Sesostris was carried out to take the air, cast his head continually back upon the two fore-most wheels next him; which Sesostris perceiving, asked him what he found worthy the admiration in that motion? to whom the captive King answered, that in those he beheld the instability of all worldly things; for that both the lowest part of the wheel was suddenly carried about, and became the highest, and the upmost part was as suddenly turned down-ward and under all: which when Sesostris had judiciously weighed, he dismissed those Princes, and all other from the like servitude in the future. Of this Sesostris, and that he could not be taken for Shishak, I have spoken at large in that part of the Egyptian Kings preceding.
