## p01786 · sig. 192b

§. XI. *Of the sixth point, to wit, of the difference and agreement of the Old and New Testament.*

## p01787 · sig. 192b

The Old and New Testament differ in name, and in the mean and way proposed for attaining to salvation; as the Old by works, the New by grace: but in the thing it self, or object and remote end, they agree: which is mans happiness and salvation.

## p01788 · sig. 192b

The Old Testament, or Law, or Letter, or the Witness of Gods will, was called the Old, because it preceded the New Testament; which is an explication of the Old: from which the New takes witness. Yet the new of more excellency, in that it does more lively express, and openly and directly 〈◊ 〉 the ways of our redemption. It is also called the Old, to show that in part it was to be abrogated: *In that he 〈◊ 〉 the New Testament, he has abrogated the Old. *For the old law, though greatly extolled by the Prophets, and delivered with wonderful miracles, yet was it constituted in a policy perishable: but the New was given in a promise of an everlasting Kingdom, and therefore called in the Apocalypse *Apocalyps*, a Testament and Gospel for ever during.

## p01789 · sig. 192b

The Old Testament is called the Law, because the first and chief part is the Law of Moses, of which the Prophets and Psalms *Psalmes* are Commentaries, explicating that Law.

## p01790 · sig. 192b

The New Testament is called the Gospel, because the first and chief part thereof is the glad tidings of our Redemption: the other Books *Bookes*, as the Epistles, or Letters of the Apostles, and the Acts or Story of the Apostles, are plentiful interpreters thereof: The word Evangelion signifying a joyful, happy and prosperous message, or( as Homer used it) the reward given to the Messenger, bringing joyful news. It is also sometime taken for a sacrifice, offered after victory, or other pleasing success, as by Xenophon. In the Scriptures it has three significations; First, for glad tidings in general, as in Isaiah *Esay* the 52. v. 7. concerning peace: Secondly, by an excellency it is restrained, to signify that most joyful message of salvation, as in 〈◊ 〉. 2. 10. whence also by figure it is taken for the History of Christ: and So we understand the four Gospels.

## p01791 · sig. 192b

Lastly, for the preaching and divulging the doctrine of Christ, as Cor. 1. 9. v. 14. and Cor. 2. 8. v. 18.

## p01792 · sig. 192b

The agreement of both Testaments( taken, I think, as they are divided in volumes) is by Danaeus comprised in these four.

## p01793 · sig. 192b

In their Author. In the substance of the Covenant, or things promised. In the foundation, to wit, Christ. In the effects, that is, in righteousness and justification.

## p01794 · sig. 192b

In the Author they agree, because both are of God, and therefore both one Testament and will of God in substance of doctrine. For as there was ever one Church, So was there one Covenant, one Adoption, and one Doctrine. As the old Law does point at Christ, So does the new Law teach Christ: the old proposing him as to come, the new as already come; one and the same thing being promised in both; both tending to one, and the same end: even the salvation of our souls: which according to S. Peter is the end of our faith. For although it be said, that Moses did promise by observing the law an earthly Kingdom, a land flowing with milk and honey, the propagation of children, and other worldly blessings: yet all these were but figures to teach, and pledges to assure the Fathers of those spiritual blessings by Christ; for by the earthly he raised their minds to the hope of heavenly. And the Fathers notwithstanding these worldly goods, did yet acknowledge themselves strangers, and pilgrims, expecting the heavenly Jerusalem *Hierusalem*: according to this place of the *Hebrews* *Hebrewes**: All these died in faith, and received not the promises, but saw them a far off, and* *〈◊ 〉 them: confessing that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth. *To which purpose also Saint AVGUSTINE *AVGVSTINE*: *Omnino 〈◊ 〉 veterem legem intelligunt, non attendentes* ("Altogether [illegible] understand the old law, not attending [to its spirit];") *per promissa terrena aeterna promitti: Few*( says he) *do understand the old law: not attending that by things earthly eternal are promised. *And S. Jerome *HIEROME*: Noluit *〈◊ 〉 pascere 〈◊ 〉 more 〈◊ 〉 corporalibus donis opibusque, ut 〈◊ 〉 somniant: God would not feed the Jews* *Iewes** as beasts with corporal gifts and riches, as themselves dream. *And this may be gathered out of Gods own words, *Ego sum Deus tuus, & erovobis in Deum; I am thy God, and I will be your God. *For the words, *I will be your God*, prove that it was not for the present, or for perishable things, that God gave them this promise; but in respect of the future: to wit, the safety of their souls. For as God created both body and soul, So has he of his goodness, not left the better part uncared for, which lives ever.

## p01795 · sig. 193a

The agreement between the Old and New Testament in substance, infers also the agreement in foundation. For Christ is called the foundation of the law, laid both by the Apostles and Prophets: in whom all the promises of God in the Old and New, are assured: the fathers having eaten the same spiritual food, which we eat in our Sacraments.

## p01796 · sig. 193a

The agreement in effects is, in that the knowledge of our sin and misery, which is taught us by the law, makes way, and as it were, serves in subordination to the Gospel, the proper effects whereof are mercy and salvation: to which the Law serving as an introduction( for to those which acknowledge their sin and misery, God shows his mercy and salvation) may be said to agree with the Gospel in the effect. For otherwise if we sever the law from subordination to the Gospel, the effects are very different: the one shows the way of righteousness by works, the other by faith; the Law wounds, the Gospel heals: the Law terrifies, the Gospel allures; Moses accuses, Christ defends: Moses condemns, Christ pardons. The Old restrains the hand, the New the mind. *Data est lex quae non sanaret* ("The law was given which did not heal [but showed the disease];")( says S. AVGUSTINE) *sed 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 probaret; The Law was given, not to help but to discover sickness: *and S. Chrysostom *CHRYSOSTOME*, *Data est lex, ut se homo inueniret,* *non ut moribus sanaretur, sed ut medicus 〈◊ 〉; The law was given that man might find and know his own imperfection: not that his disease was thereby helped: but that he might then seek out the Phisician. *For Christ came to save the World, which the law had condemned. And as Moses was but a servant, and Christ a Son, So the greatest benefit was reserved to be brought, as by the worthiest person, says Cyril: for this law made nothing perfect, but was an introduction of a better hope.
