## p02765 · sig. 303b

§. II. Of SALOMONs buildings and glory.

## p02766 · sig. 303b

HE then entered into league with Hiram King of Tyre, from whom he had much of his materials, for the Kings Palace and the Temple of God: for the building whereof he had received a double charge, one from his father David *Dauid*, and another from God. For like as it is written of David, that He called Solomon *SALOMON* *his son, and charged him to build a house for the Lord God of Israel: *So does Tostatus give the force of a divine precept to these words, *Behold; a son is borne unto thee, &c. He shall build an house for my name.*

## p02767 · sig. 303b

He began the work of the Temple, in the beginning of the fourth year of his reign, at which time also he prepared his fleet at Ezion-Geber *Esiongaber* to trade for gold in the East Indies, that nothing might be wanting to supply the charge of So great a work. For that the Temple was in building, while his fleets were passing to and fro it is manifest. For the pillars of the Temple were made of the Almug *Almaggim* trees brought from Ophir. Of this most glorious building, of all the particulars( whereof the form and example was given by God himself) many learned men have written, as *Salmeron, Montanus, Ribera, Barradas, Azorius, Villalpandus, Pineda, *and others, to whom I refer the Reader.

## p02768 · sig. 303b

For the cutting and squaring of the Cedars which served that building, Solomon employed thirty thousand Carpenters, ten thousand every month by course: he also used eighty thousand Masons in the mountain, and seventy thousand labourers that bare burdens, which it is conceived, he selected out of the Proselites, besides three thousand three hundred masters of his work, So as he paid and employed in all one hundred eighty three thousand and three hundred men, in which number the Sidonians *Zidonians* which were more skillful in hewing timber than the Israelites, may( as I think) be included. For Hiram caused his servants to bring down the Cedars and Firs *Firres* from 〈◊ 〉 to the sea, and thence sent them in rafts to Joppa *Ioppe*, or the next port to Jerusalem *Hierusalem*. For in the second of Chronicles the second Chapter, it is plain that all but the thirty thousand Carpenters, and the overseers, were strangers, and as it seems the vassals of Hiram, and of Apries *Vaphres* King of Egypt. In recompense of all this timber and stone, Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand measures of wheat, and twenty measures of pure oil yearly. Eusebius out of Eupolemus in the ninth book of his preparation the last Chapter, has left us a Copy of Salomons Letter to Suron( which was the same as Huram or Hiram) King of Tyre in these words:

## p02770 · sig. 304a

King Solomon to King SURON *SVRON*, of Tyre, Sydon, and Phaenicia King, and my fathers friend, sends greeting; You may understand that I have received of the great God of my father DAVID, the Kingdom: and when my father commanded me to build a Temple to God which created heaven and earth, he commanded also that I should write to you. I write therefore to you, and beseech you, that you would be pleased to send me Artificers and Carpenters to build the Temple of God.

SURON, Tyri, Sydonis, & Phoeniciae rex ("the king of Sidon of Phoenicia"), SALOMONI regi salutem. Lectis literis gratias ("greeting to the king. Having read the letter, thanks [are given];") egi Deo, qui ("to God, who [is];") tibi regnum patris tradidit: & quoniam scribis fabres 〈◊ 〉 ad condendum Templum ("for building the Temple") esse tibi mittendos, misi ad te millia hominum octoginta, & Architectum Tyrium hominem ex matre Judea *Iudaea*, virum in rebus architecturae mirabilem ("a Tyrian architect, a man of a Jewish mother, a man marvellous in matters of architecture"). 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 ut 〈◊ 〉 non egeant, & Templo Dei condito ad nos redeant.

## p02772 · sig. 304a

SURON of Tyre, Sidon and Phoenicia King, to King Solomon greeting: when I read your letters, I gave God thanks, who has installed you in your fathers Kingdom. And because you write, that carpenters and workmen may be sent to build Gods Temple, I have sent unto you fourscore thousand men, and a Master-builder a Tyrian, borne of a Jewish woman, a man admirable in building. You will be careful that all necessaries be provided for them, and when the Temple of God is built, that they come home to vs.

## p02773 · sig. 304a

The Copies of these letters were extant in Josephus *Iosephus* time as him self affirms, and to be seen, says he, *Tam in nostris quam in Tyriorum annalibus, as well in our own as in the Tyrian annals. *But he delivers them somewhat in different terms, as the Reader may find in his Antiquities. But were this intercourse between Solomon and Hiram either by message or by writing, it is somewhat otherwise delivered in the Scriptures, than either Eupolemus or Josephus set it down, but So, that in substance there is little difference between the one and the other.

## p02774 · sig. 304a

The like letter in effect Solomon is said to have written to Apries King of Egypt *Aegypt*, and was answered as from Hiram.

## p02775 · sig. 304a

But whereas some Commentators *Commentors* upon Solomon find that Hiram King of Tyre, and 〈◊ 〉 King of Egypt, gave Solomon the title of Rex magnus ("the great king"), and cite Eupolemus *Eupolemon* in Eusebius, I do not find any such addition of magnus in Eusebius in the last chapter of that ninth book; neither is it in Josephus in the eight book and second chapter of the Jews *Iewes* antiquities: it being a vain title used by some of the Assyrian and Persian Kings, and used likewise by the Parthians, and many other after them, insomuch as in latter times it grew common, and was usurped by mean persons in respect of the great 〈◊ 〉 the first, which was honoured by that name for his noble qualities, as much or more than for his mightiness.

## p02776 · sig. 304a

After the finishing and dedication of the Temple and house of the Lord, Solomon fortified Jerusalem with a treble wall, and repaired Hazor which had been the ancient Metropolis of the Canaanites, before 〈◊ 〉 time: So did he Gaza of the Philistines *Philistims*: he built *Bethoron, Gerar *and the Millo or munition of Jerusalem. For Pharaoh ( as it seems in favour of Solomon) came up into the edge of Ephraim, and took Gerar, which the Canaanites yet held, and put them to the sword, and burnt their City. The place and territory he gave Salomons wife for a dowry. And it is probable that because Solomon was then busied in his magnificent buildings, and could not attend the war, that he entreated his father in law to rid him of those neighbours, which 〈◊ 〉 performed. But he thereby taught the Egyptians *Aegyptians* to visit those parts again before they were sent for; and in his son Rehoboams time Shishak *Sheshack* this mans successor did sac Jerusalem itself.

## p02777 · sig. 304a

Solomon also built Megiddo in Manasseh *Manasse*, on this side Jordan; and Baalah *Balah* in Dan: also Thadmor, which may be either Ptolemies *Ptolomies* Tadmor *Thamoron* in the desert of 〈◊ 〉( or as Josephus thinks Palmyra in the desert of Syria, which Palmyra because it stood on the utmost border of Salomons dominion to the northeast of Lebanon *Libanus*, and was of Davids *Dauids* conquest when he wan 〈◊ 〉; it may seem that Solomon therefore bestowed thereon the most cost, and fortified it with the best art that that age had. Josephus calls this place 〈◊ 〉, by which 〈◊ 〉( says he) given by Solomon, the Syrians as yet call it. 〈◊ 〉 in his book of Hebrew places calls it Thermeth. In aftertimes, when it was rebuilt by Adrian the Emperor, it was honoured with his name, and called Adrianople *Adrianopolis*. In respect of this great charge of building Solomon raised tribute through all his Dominions, besides an hundred and twenty talents of gold received from Hirams servants, Solomon offered Hiram twenty towns in or near the upper Galilee, but because they stood in an unfruitful and marsh ground, Hiram refused them, and thereof was the territory called Chabul.

Further, he made a 〈◊ 〉 into 〈◊ 〉 Zobah, and established his tributes; the first and last war( if in that expedition 〈◊ 〉 were driven to fight) that he made in person in all his life. He then visited the border of all his Dominions, passing from Thadmor to the North of Palmyrena, and So to the Deserts *Desarts* of 〈◊ 〉, from whence he visited Ezion-geber and Eloth, the uttermost place of the South of all his territories, bordering to the red sea: which Cities I have described in the Story of Moses.
