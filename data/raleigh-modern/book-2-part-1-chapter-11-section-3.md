## p02415 · sig. 254b

§. III. *Of the later Kings, and decay and overthrow of their power.*

## p02416 · sig. 254b

After *Hazael, Benhadad *the second, or rather the third of that name, the son of Hazael, reigned in Damascus: who fought against Israel, with ill success: for Joash *Ioas* King of Israel, the son of the unhappy Joachaz *Ioachaz*, as he was foretold by Elisha the Prophet, beat Benhadad in three several battles: and he lost all those Cities to Israel, which his Father Hazael had taken violently from Joachaz.

## p02417 · sig. 254b

After this Benhadad the son of Hazael, there succeeded three others of the same name, of whom the Stories are lost, only Nicholaus Damascenus cited by Josephus *Iosephus*, makes mention of them: and in one of these Kings times it was that Jeroboam *Ieroboam* the second, the son of Joash recovered Damascus itself, to Judah *Iudah* says the Geneva, but better in IVNIVS, *utque recuperabat Damascum, & Chamatham Iehudae pro Israel*; that is, *And how be recovered for Israel, Damascus and Chamatha of Judea* *Iudea*; for these Cities sometimes conquered by David *Dauid*, did of right belong to the Tribe of Judah.

## p02418 · sig. 254b

And it is likely that this conquest upon the Adads was performed: the first of these three Adads then living, of whom there is no Story. For when as Jehoash *Iehoas* the King of the ten Tribes had thrice overcome the Syrians in the time of Benhadad the son of Hazael, and had recovered the Cities which Hazael had won from Israel; and So left his Kingdom to his son Jeroboam the second, it seems that this 〈◊ 〉 without delay, and having nothing else left for him to enterprise, instantly followed his fathers good fortune, and invaded Damascus.

## p02419 · sig. 254b

〈◊ 〉, or Rezin, after Josephus Rezin *Rases*, after *Zonar as Rezin*, the 10. Adad, makes league with Pekah, or Pekah *Phacas* King of Israel, against Ahaz *Achaz* King of Judah; both carry away a great number of prisoners. After this they both besiege Ahaz in Jerusalem *Hierusalem*: but in vain. Then Adad alone invades Elath, and beating out the Jews *Iewes*, makes it a Colony of Syrians. Wherefore Ahaz brought 〈◊ 〉 against Razin, who took him, and beheaded him, and won Damascus: with whom ended the line of the Adads and the Kingdom of Damascus: the Assyrians becoming masters both of that and Israel. These Adads as they reigned in order are thus reckoned.

## p02420 · sig. 254b

1 Hadadezer *Adadezer* the son of Rehob. 2 Rezin the son of Eliada *Eliadad*, or Razin. 3 Hezion. 4 Tabrimmon *Tabremmon*. 5 Benhadad, who invaded Baasha. 6 Benhadad the second, taken prisoner by Ahab *Achab*. 7 Hazael, whom Elisha foretold, with tears, of his advancement; the same who overthrew Joram *Ioram* King of Israel, at Ramoth Gilead. And that there was a second Hazael which preceded Benhadad the third, it is not improbable, because that Hazael which took Gath *Geth*, and compounded the war with 〈◊ 〉, made the Expedition thirty 〈◊ 〉, and perchance more, after the first Hazael which stifled his master 〈◊ 〉, and had slain Joram the son of Ahab King of Israel. For Joash began to reign in the 7. year of Jehu *Iehu* King of Israel; and after he had reigned 23. years, the Temple was not yet repaired, after which( and how long we know not) it is said that Hazael took Gath, and turned his face towards Jerusalem. It is also some proof that Hazael which took Gath, was not the same with Hazael that murdered Benhadad, because he could not at that time but be of good years, being as it seems the second person in the kingdom, and Commander of Benhadads men of war. To 〈◊ 〉 Hazael, be he the first or second, succeeded 8 Benhadad the third, whom Joash King of Israel thrice overthrew. 9 Resin or Rezin the last, who joined with Pekah King of Israel, against 〈◊ 〉, at which time Ahaz King of Judah waged for his defence Teglatphalassar.

## p02421 · sig. 255a

Now between Benhadad the third, and Rezin the last, Nicholaus Damascenus finds three other Kings of the Adads, which make twelve in all.

## p02422 · sig. 255a

For the rest of the Princes of 〈◊ 〉, which were but reguli, as those of Hamath *Emath*, and Gessur, we find that Tohu was King of Hamath or Hamath in Davids *Dauids* time, to whom he sent his son Joram with presents, after Davids victory against Hadadezer. Also 〈◊ 〉 speaks of a King of Hamath, but names him not.
