When Shalmaneser *Salmanassar* was dead, and his son 〈◊ 〉 in possession of the Empire, in the fourteenth year of Ezcchias, he demanded of him such Tribute as was agreed on, at such time as 〈◊ 〉, the Grand-father of Sennacherib, and Father of Shalmaneser, invited by 〈◊ 〉, invaded Rezin *Rezen* King of Damascus, and delivered him from the dangerous War which Israel had undertaken against him. This Tribute and acknowledgement when Hezekiah *Ezechias* denied, 〈◊ 〉, having( as it seems) a purpose to invade Egypt *Aegypt*, sent one part of his Army to lie before Jerusalem *Ierusalem*. Now though Hezekiah ( fearing this powerful Prince) had acknowledged his fault, and purchased his peace, as he hoped, with thirty hundred talents of silver, and thirty talents of gold: wherewith he presented Sennacherib, now set down before 〈◊ 〉 in 〈◊ 〉, yet under the colour of better assurance, & to force the King of 〈◊ 〉 to deliver hostages, the Assyrian environed Jerusalem with a gross Army, and having his Sword in his hand, thought it the fittest time to write his own conditions.

Hezekiah directed his three greatest Counsellers, to parley with Rabsaces, over the Wall; and to receive his demands: who used three principal arguments to persuade the people to yield themselves to his Master Sennacherib. For though the Chancellor, Steward, and Secretary, sent by 〈◊ 〉, desired Rabshakeh *Rabsoces* to speak unto them in the Syrian tongue, and not in the 〈◊ 〉, yet he with a more loud voice directed his speech to the multitude in their own language. And for the first, he made them know, That if they continued 〈◊ 〉, and adhered to their King, that they would, in a short time, be enforced to eat their own dung, and drink their own urine: Secondly, he altogether disabled the King of Egypt, from whom the 〈◊ 〉 hoped for succour; and compared him to a broken staff, on which whosoever leans, pierces his own hand: Thirdly, that the gods who should help them, 〈◊ 〉 had formerly broken and 〈◊ 〉, meaning chiefly( as it is thought by some) the brazen Serpent, which had been preserved ever since Moses time: and withal he bade them remember the gods of other Nations: whom, notwithstanding any power of theirs, his Master had conquered and thrown down; and for God himself, in whom they trusted, he persuaded them by no means to rely on him; for he would deceive them. But finding the people silent( for So the King had commanded them) after a while, when he had understood that the King of Arabia was marching on with a powerful Army, he himself left the Assyrian forces in charge to others, and sought Sennacherib at Lebna in 〈◊ 〉, either to inform him of their resolution in Jerusalem, or to confer with him concerning the Army of Terhaca the Arabian. Soon upon this there came letters from Sennacherib to Hezekiah, whom he partly advised, and partly threatened to submit himself: using the same blasphemous outrage against the all-powerful God, as before. But Ezcchias sending those Counsellers to the Prophet Isaiah *Esay*, which had lately been sent to Rabsaces, received from him comfort, and assurance, that this Heathen Idolater should not prevail; against whom the King also besought aide from Almighty God, repeating the most insolent and blasphemous parts of Sennacheribs letter, before the Altar of God in the Temple, confessing this part thereof to be true. *That the King of* ASHUR *ASHVR* *had destroyed the Nations and their Lands, and had set fire on their gods, for they were no Gods, but the work of mans hands, even wood and stone, &c.*

The reason that moved Sennacherib to desire to possess himself in haste of Jerusalem, was, that he might into it have retreated his Army, which was departed, as it seems, from the siege of Pelusium in Egypt, for fear of Terhaca: and though the Scriptures are silent of that enterprise( which in these books of the Kings, and of the Chronicles or Paralipomenon, speak but of the affairs of the Jews *Iewes* in effect) yet the ancient 〈◊ 〉, and out of him Josephus *Iosephus*, and S. Jerome *Hierome*, together with Herodotus, remember it as follows. Herodotus calls Sennacherib King of Arabia and Assyria: which he might justly do, because Tiglath-Pileser *Tiglath* his Grand-father held a great part thereof, which he wrested from Pekah King of Israel: as Gilead over Jordan, and the rest of Arabia Petraea adjoining: the same Herodotus also makes Sethos *Sethon* King of Egypt, to be 〈◊ 〉 Priest, and reports that the reason of Sennacheribs return from Pelusium in Egypt, which he also besieged, was, that an innumerable multitude of Rats had in one night eaten in sunder the Bow-strings of his Archers, and spoiled the rest of their weapons of that kind, which no doubt might greatly amaze him: but the approach of Terhaca, remembered by Josephus and Berosus, was the more urgent. S. Jerome upon the seven and thirty of Isaiah, out of the same Berosus, as also in part out of Herodotus, whom Josephus cites somewhat otherwise than his words lie, reports Senacheribs retreat in these words. 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 Sennacherib *SENACHERIB* *Regem Assyriorum contra Aegyptios, & obsedisse Pelusium, 〈◊ 〉 extructis aggeribus, urbi 〈◊ 〉, venisse *TARACHAM *Regem Aethiopum in auxilium, & una nocte juxta Jerusalem, centum octoginta 〈◊ 〉 millia exercitus Assyrij pestilentia corruisse narrat* ("He relates that the king of the Ethiopians came to his aid, and that in one night near Jerusalem a hundred and eighty [illegible] thousand of the Assyrian army fell by pestilence.")* *HERODOTUS *HERODOTVS*: & plenissime BEROSUS *BEROSVS* *Chaldaicaescriptor Historiae, quorum fides de proprijs libris petenda est; With which destruction being terrified, and withal afraid what might become of the rest of his Army, he made great marches into his Kingdom; to his Royal City, which is called Ninus, where* *shortly after by the treason of two of the eldest of his Sons, *ADRAMELECH and Sharezer *SELENNAR* or SHAREZER, *he lost his life in the Temple dedicated to *Nisroch *ARASCES*, or NESROCH: *whom he especially worshipped. Who in the beginning of his reign sent new troupes out of Assyria and Samaria, to fortify the Colony therein planted by his grandfather Shalmaneser. What this Nesroch was, it is uncertain: Jerome in his Hebrew traditions has somewhat of him, but nothing positively. It is certain, that Venus Urania was worshipped by the Assyrians; and So was Jupiter Belus, as *Dion, 〈◊ 〉*, and Cyril *Cyrillus* witness. Many fancies there are, what cause his son had to murder him; but the most likely is, that he had formerly dis-inherited those two, and conferred the Empire on *Assarhaddon. Tobit *tells us, That it was fifty five days after 〈◊ 〉 return, ere he was murdered by his sons; during which time he slew great numbers of the Israelites in Nineve *Nineue*, till the most just God turned the Sword against his own breast.
