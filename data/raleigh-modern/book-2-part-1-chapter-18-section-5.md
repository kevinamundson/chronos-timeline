§. V. Of SALOMONS 〈◊ 〉.

## p02799 · sig. 306b

There remain of Salomons works, the Proverbs *Prouerbs*, the Preacher, and the Song of Solomon *Salomon*. In the first he 〈◊ 〉 good life and corrects manners, in the second, the vanity of humane nature, in the third he sings as it were the Epithalamion of Christ and his Church. For the Book entitled The wisdom of Solomon, ( which some give unto Solomon, and some make the elder Philo the Author thereof) Jerome *Hierome* and many others of the best 〈◊ 〉 make us think it was not Solomon that wrote it. *〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉* ( says Jerome) qui SALOMONIS ("who [was] of Solomon;") *inscribitur, 〈◊ 〉 redolet eloquentiam; The 〈◊ 〉 of the book of wisdom, which is ascribed to *Solomon, *〈◊ 〉 of the 〈◊ 〉 〈◊ 〉*; and of the same opinion was S. Augustine, and yet he confesses in the 〈◊ 〉 Book and twentieth Chapter of the City of God, that the Author of that Book has a direct fore-telling of the Passion of Christ in these words: *Circumueniamus iustum quoniam 〈◊ 〉 est nobis, &c. Let us circumvent the righteous,* *for he is 〈◊ 〉 to us, he is contrary to our doings, he checks us for offending against the Law, he makes his boast to have the knowledge of God, and he calls himself the Son of the Lord, & c. *and So does the course of all the following words point directly at Christ. The Books *Bookes* of *Ecclesiastes, Proverbs*, and *〈◊ 〉 Canticorum, Rabbi Moses* Kimchi ascribes to Isay the Prophet. Suidas and Cedrenus report that Solomon wrote of the remedies of all diseases, and graved the same on the sides of the 〈◊ 〉 of the Temple, which they say Hezekiah *Ezechias* pulled down, because the people 〈◊ 〉 help from God by prayer, repaired thither for their recoveries.

Of Salomons books of Invocations *Inuocations* and Inchantments to cure diseases, and expel evil spirits, Josephus *Iosephus* has written at large, though as I conceive, rather out of his own invention, or from some uncertain report, than truly.

Certainly So strange an example of humane frailty has never been read of as this King: who having received wisdom from God himself, in honour of whom, and for his only service, he built the first and most glorious Temple of the World: he that was made King of Israel and 〈◊ 〉, not by the law of Nature, but by the love of God, and became the 〈◊ 〉, richest, and happiest of all Kings, did in the end, by the persuasion of a few weak and wretched Idolatrous Women, forget and forsake the Lord of all the World, and the giver of all goodness, of which he was more liberal to this King, than to any that ever the World had. Of whom Siracides writes in this manner: Solomon *reigned in a peaceable time and was glorious, for God made all quiet round about, that he might build a house to his name, and prepare the Sanctuary for ever; How wise wast thou in thy youth, and wast filled with understanding, as with a Flood* *floud**: Thy mind covered the whole earth, and has filled it with grave and dark sentences. Thy name went abroad in the Isles* *Iles**, and for thy peace thou wast beloved, & c. *but thus he concludes: *Thou didst bow thy loins to Women, and wast over-come by thy body,* *thou didst stain thine honour, and 〈◊ 〉 defiled thy posterity, and hast brought wrath upon thy children, and felt sorrow for thy folly. *cap. 27.
