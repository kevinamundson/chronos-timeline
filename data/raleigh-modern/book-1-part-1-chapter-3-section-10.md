§. X. *Of divers other testimonies of the land of Eden; and that this is the Eden of Paradise.*

## p00960 · sig. 68b

And for a more particular pointing out of this Eden, it seems by the two Epistles of the Nestorian Christians, that inhabit Mesopotamia, which Epistles in the year 1552. they sent to the Pope about the confirming of their Patriarch, and Andraeas Masius has published them, translated out of Syriac into Latine. By these Epistles( I say) it seems we may have some farther light for the proof of that, which we have said about the Region of Eden in those parts. For in them both there is mention of the Island of Eden in the River Tigris, or at least, Tigris in both these Epistles is called the River of Eden. This Island, as Masius in his Preface to these Epistles says, is commonly called Gozoria( as it were, the Island, by an eminence) It has( says he) ten miles in circuit, and was sometimes walled round about, which name of the Island Eden may( doubtless) remain to this day; though in the rest of the Region So called this name be swallowed up, with the fame of those flourishing Kingdoms *Kingdomes* of *Mesopotamia, Assyria, Babylonia*, and Chaldea *Chaldaea*. This Island of Eden has up the River, and not far beyond it, the City of Hasan-Cepha, otherwise Fortis Petra: below it, it has Mosul *Mosal* or Mosul, from which( as in that which follows it shall appear out of Masius) it is not above twelve miles distant. Neither is it to trouble us, that Mosul or Mosul by Marius Niger is remembered among the Cities higher up Tigris, in these words. Juxta *Iuxta* autem *Tigrim, Civitates* *Ciuitates** sunt Dorbeta prope Taurum montem, quae nunc Mosul dicitur; magna sane, &c* ("The Tigris: There are cities, Dorbeta near Mount Taurus, which is now called Mosul; a great [city] indeed, etc.")*.*( that is) *By Tigris are these Cities, Dorbeta near unto mount Taurus( which is now called Mosul) which is a great one, &c. *This opinion of Niger, displacing Mosul, and making it to be Dorbeta( I say) needs not here to trouble us: seeing for this matter, the testimony of Masius informed by the Christians that dwelt there( the Seat of whose Patriarch it is) ought to be of credit, avowing that this Mosul( or Mozal) is in the confines of Mesopotamia and Assyria, seated upon Tigris, and in the neighbourhood of Nineveh *Niniue*; and that it is the famous Seleucia Parthorum. The Nestorian Christians in their former Epistle, call it Attur in these words: *Ex omnibus Civitatibus* *Ciuitatibus** & pagis quae sunt circum Civitatem* *Ciuitatem** Mozal( hoc est* ("that is")*) Attur in vicinia Ninives* *Niniues**; Of all the Cities and towns which are about the City of Mozal( that is) Attur in the neighbourhood of Nineveh. *As also Niger acknowledges Ctesiphon, a City thereabout to be called Asshur *Assur*( which is the same as Attur, after the Dialect of those Nations, which change Sh into T.) Neither is it much that he should mistake Ctesiphon( which is not far off Seleucia) for Seleucia, to be Asshur. By this then we may come somewhat near the end of our purpose. For the 〈◊ 〉 of Eden, which lies in the breast of Tigris, is but twelve miles from Mosul, and that ancient City, which Ptolemy *Ptolomie* and Tacitus call Ninus, and the Scriptures *Nineve* *Nineue**, Philostratus*, and *Simeon Sethi, Mosul*, and *John* *Iohn** Lean Mosul*, others Moss, ( though it be not the same with Mosul) is set but a little higher upon the same river of Tigris, near Mosul: So that we are like to find this Isle of Eden here-about. For the same Andraeas Masius, which places it above Mosul, makes it to be below Hasan chepha, which is upon the same river of Tigris.

The only difficulty is this, that some perhaps may think, that the words of the Nestorians in both their Epistles, speak not of any Isle in Tigris, called the Isle of Eden, but of an Isle in Tigris, a river of Eden. But this sense of their words in my opinion seems the more improbable. And yet if this were the meaning here, we have a testimony from the learned of those parts, that not only Euphrates, but also Tigris was a river of Eden, and that the name of Eden in those parts is not yet quite worn out, though the Region has been subject to the same change, that all other kingdoms of the World have been, and has by conquest, and corruption of other languages, received new and differing names. For the South part of Eden, which stretches over Euphrates, was after the Flood *floud* called Shinar, and then of the tower of *Babel, Babylonia*; and the North part of Eden is that Tract of *Mesopotamia, Assyria*, and Armenia, which embraces both the banks of Tigris, between Mount Taurus, and 〈◊ 〉. And of this Region of Eden. that ancient AETHICUS *AETHICVS* makes mention, ( not that latter AETHICUS, disciple of CALLINICUS *CALLINICVS*, otherwise by PLUTARCH *PLVTARCH* and Athenaeus *ATHENAEVS* called ISTRI, who lived in Egypt *Aegypt* in the reign of PHILADELPHUS *PHILADEIPHVS*, but another of a far higher and remote time) the same being made Latine out of Greek *Greeke* by S. Jerome *HIEROME*. And though by corruption of the ancient copy it be written in AETHICUS, Adonis for Edenis; yet Adonis being a river of Phoenicia, cannot be understood to be the Region named by AETHICUS. For AETHICUS makes it a Country, and not a river, and joins it with Mesopotamia and Ethiopia *Aethiopia*, calling the land of CUSH *CHVS* Ethiopia, after the Vulgar, and Septuagint. And lastly, the River which waters the Regions( says AETHICUS) falls into the Gulf of Persia: which River he calls Armodius, for *Tigris; Tigris *being but a name imposed for the swiftness thereof. And out of Armenia both Tigris and Euphrates have their original: for out of Eden came a river, or rivers, to water the Garden, both which Rivers *Riuers*( to wit) Tigris and Euphrates, come out of Armenia, and both of them traverse Mesopotamia, Regions first of all known by the name of Eden for their beauty and fertility. And it is very probable, that Eden contained also some part of Armenia, and the excellent fertility thereof in divers places, is not unworthy the name of Eden. For in some part thereof( says STRABO) the leaves are always green, and therefore therein a perpetual Spring. Also STEPHANUS *STEPHANVS* de urbibus ("On Cities") mentions the City of Adana upon Euphrates: and the name of Eden was in use in Amos time, though he speak not of Eden in the East, but of Eden in Coelesyria. But to the end I may not burden the Readers patience with too long a discourse, it may suffice to know, that Euphrates and Tigris( once joined together, and afterward separate) are two of those four heads, into which these Rivers which are said to water the Garden of Paradise, were divided: whose courses being known, Eden, ( out of which they are said to come) cannot be unknown. Now that Hiddekel and Euphrates *Perath* were Tigris and Euphrates, it is agreed by all: for the Seventy and all others convert Euphrates by *Euphrates: & Hiddekel Tigrim omnes exponunt: and all men understand Hiddekel by Tigris*( says VATABLE. ) And because that which I have said of the Isle of Eden, shall not be subject to the censure of self-invention, I have here-under set down the words out of the two general Epistles of the Nestorians, as *Masius( ad verbum* ("Masius (word for word [renders it thus]);")) has converted them into Latine. The occasion of those Letters and Supplications to the Pope were, that the Nestorian Christians which inhabit *Mesopotamia, Assyria, Persia, and Babylonia, and have to this day*( at least in Queen Mary's *Maries* time they had) fifteen Churches in one City, called 〈◊ 〉 Parthorum, or Mosul upon the River of Tigris, having no sufficient authority to choose themselves a Patriarch( which cannot be done without four or three Metropolitan Bishops at least) sent to the Bishop of Rome in the year of Christ 1552.( as aforesaid) a Petition to obtain allowance unto such an election as themselves had made: having three hundred year before that upon the like defect, sent one Marius thither to be confirmed; and in this negotiation they made known to the Bishop of Rome the state of the Christian Church in those parts: for upon the death of their Patriarch( who of a covetous desire to enrich himself had forborne to institute Metropolitan Bishops, when the places fell void) they all assembled themselves together to consult of the Church-government *Church-gouernment*. And because all the Patriarchs for 100. years had been of one house and family to the prejudice of the Church, and that there yet remained one Bishop of the same Stock and Kindred, who aspired to the same dignity, which his Predecessors *Predecessours* had held, the rest of the Professors *Professours* refused to allow him. Upon which occasion, and for the choice of a Governor more sufficient, the Teachers in all the Churches assembled themselves. The words of the general Epistle to the Pope are these, about the middle of the said Epistle: *Verum nos non acceptavimus neque proclamavimus ipsum, sed subito conuenimus ex omnibus locis Orientalibus, & ex omnibus Civitatibus & Pagis quae sunt circum Civitatem Mosul( hoc est) 〈◊ 〉, in victnia Ninives, ex Babylonia, ex Charrha, ex Arbella, ex Insula quae est in medio Tigris, fluminis Eden, ex Tauris Persiae, ex Nisibi, & c* ("But we have neither accepted nor proclaimed him; rather we suddenly assembled from all the eastern places, and from all the cities and villages which are around the city of Mosul (that is) [illegible], in the neighbourhood of Nineveh, from Babylonia, from Harran, from Arbela, from the island which is in the midst of the Tigris, the river Eden, from the Taurus of Persia, from Nisibis, etc.")*. *which is: *But we did neither accept of this man, neither pronounced him: but suddenly we assembled our 〈◊ 〉 of all parts of the East, and out of all the Cities and Villages which are about Mosul( or Attur) neighbouring* *Nineveh, and out of Babylon, Carrhae* *Carrha**, Arbella, and out of the Island which lies in the middle of Tigris, a River of Eden, *or rather, *out of the Isle of Eden, which lies in the River Tigris. *And in a second Epistle at the same time sent, they use these words: *Neque supersunt apud nos Metropolitae, quorum est ordinare Catholicum, sed soli pauct Episcopi, Episcopus Arbelae, Episcopus Salmasti, Episcopus Adurbeigan; en e vestigio conuenimus in 〈◊ 〉, quae est intra Tigrim flumen, Eden, fecimusque compactum inter nos, &c.*( which is) *Neither are there remaining among us any Metropolitan Bishops to whom it belongs to ordain a Patriarch, but only a few Bishops, as the Bishop of Arbela, the Bishop of Salmastus, and the Bishop of Adurbeigan: but lo, we assembled speedily in the Island Eden, which is in Tigris and agreed between our selves, &c.*

Now this Island of Eden Masius describes with other places; which being well conceived, the Nestorian Epistles, and the state of the Church may be in those parts( says he) the better understood. And after he has distinguished the four sorts of Christians in those parts of the World, and in the South part of Africa, which he calls *Nestorians, Jacobites* *Iacobites**, Maronites* *Maronitae*, and Copts *Cophti*, he goes on in these words: *Mox, 〈◊ 〉 morte, concurrisse aiebant tumultuario in illam quam modo dixi Tigris Insulam, quae duodecim circiter passuum millibus supra Mosul posita, decem fere millia passuum suo ambitu continet, muris undique cincta, & a paucis alijs quam Christianis hominibus habitata* ("Soon, [illegible] dead, they said that they had hastily flocked together to that island of the Tigris of which I just spoke, which lies about twelve thousand paces above Mosul, contains almost ten thousand paces in its circuit, is walled all around, and is inhabited by few other than Christian men.")*: *which is, *Now hearing of the death of the Patriarch, *( as those that came to Rome reported) *they ran tumultuously together into that Island of Tigris *or Eden before spoken of, *which Island is situated about *12. *miles above Mosul, containing very near ten miles in compass, and every where 〈◊ 〉 with a wall, inhabited by a few other men than Christians. *And afterward he makes a recapitulation of the Christian Churches; among the rest he adds the Isle of Eden by the name of *Geserta, Insula Tigris: sive Geserta.* Furthermore, describing the City of Hosan-cepha, or Fortis Petra, he places it *supra praedictam Tigris Insulam rupi asperae impositam; Above the aforesaid Island of Tigris, being seated on a steep rock. *Of this Island of *Geserta, Andrew Thevet* *Theuet** *makes mention in his tenth book of his general Cosmography in these words: *Geserta ou Gesire est 〈◊ 〉 milieu de la riviere du Tigris* *Tigre**, & pens que c'est une terre des plus fertiles de toutel ' Asia* *Asie**; Geserta or Gesire is in the middle of Tigris, the soul the most fertile of all Asia.*

By this we see that the ancient name of Eden lives, and of that Eden, which lies Eastward from Arabia Petraea, and the Desert where Moses wrote, and that Eden which borders Haran *Charran* according to Ezekiel *Ezechiel*, and that Eden which is seated according to the assertion of the said Prophet, and joined with those Nations of Reseph, Calneh *Canneh*, and Haran, and the rest which traded with the Tyrians, and is found at this day in the parting of the two Regions of Assyria and Babylonia, where the Edenites in Telassar *Thelassar* were garrisoned to resist the Assyrians, whose displantation Sennacherib *Senacherib* vaunted of( as above written) and lastly, the same Eden, which embraces Tigris, and looks on Euphrates, two of the known Rivers of those four, which are by all men ascribed to Paradise.
