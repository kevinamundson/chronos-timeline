§. III. *THE TRIBE OF ASHER*.

## p02031 · sig. 219a

†. I. *The bounds of the Tribe of Asher.*

## p02032 · sig. 219a

The Asherites descended of Asher the Son of Jacob *Iacob* by Zelpha, the hand-maid of Lea, were increased while they abode in Egypt *Aegypt*, to the number of 41500. and odd persons, all men above twenty years of age, and able to bear arms at the time, when they were mustered by Moses at Mount Sinai: all which number perishing in the Deserts *Desarts*, there remained of their issues, besides women and children 53400. bodies fit for the wars: which past the River of Arnon, into the Plains *Plaines* of Moab, and after the Conquest of Canaan, had for their portion that part of Phoenicia, from Sidon *Zidon* and the fields of Lebanon *Libanus*, unto Ptolemais *Ptolomais* Acre *Acon* along the Sea-coast: containing thirty English miles or thereabout: and from the Mid-land sea to the East border some twelve miles: though Antoninus makes it somewhat larger. This part of Canaan was very fruitful, abounding in Wine, Oil, and Wheat, besides the Balsamum, with other pleasant and profitable commodities: according to that Prophecy, Asher *ASSER* pinguis panis: concerning Asher, his bread shall be fat: *And he shall give pleasures* *for a King*.

## p02033 · sig. 219a

†. II. Of Sidon.

## p02034 · sig. 219a

The first City seated on the North border of the Territory of Asher, was Sidon, which Joshua *Iosua* calls the great Sidon, both for strength and magnitude. The Greeks *Greekes* and Q. Curtius make Agenor the founder thereof: and Justin *Iustine* derives the name from the abundance of Fish found on those shores: whereof it has been called Sidon. But that it was far more ancient, *Moses, Joshua*, and Josephus *Iosephus* witness, the same being founded by Sidon the eldest of Canaans Sons: and So strong it was in Josuas *Iosuas* time, as neither did himself attempt it, neither could the Asserites, or any of their Successors master it: but it continued all the time of the Judges *Iudges* and Kings, even unto the coming of Christ, a City interchangeably governed, by their own Princes or other Magistrates: though according to the warnings, and threats of the *〈◊ 〉 Isaiah* *Esay**, Jeremiah* *Hieremie**, Ezekiel, *and 〈◊ 〉, it was often afflicted both by the enemies sword, and by the pestilence.

## p02035 · sig. 219a

Sidon is seated on the very wash of the Phoenician Sea, which is a part of the Mediterranean *Mediterran* or Mid-land Sea. It has to the North the City of Berythus, and the River Leontis: and to the South Sarepta, or Zarephath *Sarphat*: which stands between it and Tyre, the distance between which two great and famous Cities, to wit, Sidon and Tyre, is fourteen thousand paces, says Ziegler *Seiglerus*: but Vadianus makes it two hundred furlongs, and So does 〈◊ 〉 in his description of the holy Land, and both from Strabo: which two hundred furlongs make five and twenty miles. This difference of distance as well between these two known Cities, as all the rest, make it over difficult to devise any new scale to the Map, and description of the holy Land.

## p02036 · sig. 219b

What Kings it had till Agenor's *Agenors* time there is no memory: The Story which Zeno the Philosopher, who was a Zidonian, wrote thereof, being by time consumed and lost. It seems to be more ancient than Tyre: which was also built by the Sidonians *Zidonians*. For as Strabo notes, Homer speaking of Sidon, neglects the memory of Tyre: because it was but a member of Sidon, and a City subject to the Kings thereof: though it be true that in after-times it contended with Sidon for Primacy, and became far more renowned, opulent, and strong: From Sidon had Solomon *Salomon* and 〈◊ 〉, their principal workmen, both in Timber and Stone; for the building of the Temple. For as it flourished in all sorts of learning, So did it in all other Mechanical Arts and Trades: the Prophet Zechariah *Zacharie* calling them the wise Sidonians. The City was both by nature and art exceeding strong, having a Castle or 〈◊ 〉 on the North-side, standing upon an inaccessible Rock, and compassed by the Sea, which after the Citizens became Christians, was held and defended by the Knights of the Dutch Order: and another Castle it has on the South side by the Port of Egypt, which the Templars *Templers* guarded. It also sent many other Colonies besides that of Tyre, into places remote: as unto Thebes, and Sephyra, Cities of Boeotia in Greece.

## p02037 · sig. 219b

Strabo and Pliny *Plinie* give the Sidonians the invention of * Glass, which they used to make of those sands which are taken out of the River Belus, falling into the Mediterranean Sea, near Ptolemais or Acre. and from whence the Venetians fetch the matter of those clear Glasses which they make at Murana: of which S. Jerome *Hierome* and Pliny. Sidon *insignis artifex vitri*: Sidon *vitarijs 〈◊ 〉 Nobilis*; Sidon *a famous Glassmaker, or a skillful worker in Glass-houses* *Glasse-houses**.*

## p02038 · sig. 219b

They were in Religion Idolaters( as the rest of the Canaanites) worshippers of Baal and 〈◊ 〉: which Idols though common to the other of the issue of Canaan( as Pineda gathers out of 1. Sam.. 31. 10. and Judg *Iud*.. 10. 6.) yet especially and peculiarly were accounted the Gods of the Sidonians: as appears 1. Kings 11. 5. in the story of Salomons Idolatry: where Astaroth is called the God of the Sidonians: and 1. Reg. 16. 33 in the story of Ahab *Achab*, the chief worshipper of Baal, where it is said that he marrying Jezebel the Daughter of the King of the Sidonians, worshipped their Baal. Divers Baals and divers Ashtaroth *Astaroths* in their Idolatries they acknowledged: as it appears by the plural names of Baalim and Astaroth, 1. Sam.. 12. 10. and elsewhere: for even the name Astaroth, as I am informed by a skillful 〈◊ 〉, is plural: the singular being Astoreth: whence Judg.. 2. 13. the Septuagint read 〈◊ 〉: *They worshipped the Astartes* *Astarties*. The occasion of this their multiplying of their Baals, and Ashtoreths *Astoreths*, may be diversely understood: either in respect of the diversity of the forms of the Images, or of the worship in divers places, or of the stories depending upon them: which( as fables use to be) were doubtless in divers Cities divers. Augustine quaest. 16. in Judg. thinks Baal and Astarte to be Jupiter and Juno. For the Carthaginians( which were Tyrians) call Juno by some such name as *Astarte. Tully* *Tullie**, lib.*. 3. *de Nat. Deorum* ("There was an ancient city, which the Tyrians held"), making divers Goddesses of the name of Venus, expounds the fourth to be Astarte: whom he makes to be borne of Tyre *Tyrus* and Syria, and to have been the wife of Adonis: as also Macrobius *Macrob*. 2. *Saturn. cap*. 21. says that Adonis was with great veneration commonly worshipped of the Assyrians: and Jerome upon Ezek. 8. 44. notes that Thammuz( whom there the Idolatrous women are noted to bewail) is the name of Adonis among the Syrians. So that it may seem that in the worship of Astarte, or Venus, they did bewail her husband Adonis: as also the Grecians *Graecians* did in their songs of ADONIS. Mourn for ADONIS *the fair, dead is *ADONIS the fair, How be it others in that place of Ezekiel not without good probability, expound the mourning for Thammuz, to be the mourning for Osiris in the sacrifices of Isis: whose loss of her husband Osiris, was as famous in the Egyptian *Aegyptian* Idolatry, as with the *Grecians, Venus *loss of Adonis. And to this agrees that which Plutarch has, *de Iside & Osiride*; that Osiris with the Egyptians *Aegyptians* is called Tammuz *Ammuz*: which word may seem to be the same with Ezekiel's *Ezekiels* Thammuz. But how soever these Sidonians were thus anciently 〈◊ 〉 with the milk of Idolatry: yet they were more apt to receive the Doctrine and Gospel of Christ after his Ascension, than the Jews *Iewes*: who had been taught by Moses, and the Prophets So many years, whereof our Saviour in Matthew and *Luke. Woe be to thee Corazin, &c. for if the great works which were* *done in thee, had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they had repented long ago, &c. but I say unto you, it shall be easier for Tyre and Sidon, at the day of judgement than for you.*

## p02039 · sig. 220a

It received a Christian Bishop with the first: who was afterward of the Diocesse of Tyre. But in the year of our Redemption 636. it fell into the hands of the Saracens: and continued in their possession till Baldwin *Baldwinus* the first, then King of Jerusalem *Hierusalem*, in the year IIII. by the help of the Danes and Norwegians *Norwaies*, who came with a Fleet to visit the holy Land, and took Port at Joppa *Ioppa*, it was again recovered, the 〈◊ 〉 thereof being given to Eustace Grenier *Gremer*, a Noble man of that Country. And again in the year 1250. it was rebuilt and strengthened by Louis *Lodowicke* the French King: while he spent four year in the War of the holy Land. Lastly, in the year 1289. it was reconquered by the Saracens: and is now in possession of the Turk *Turke*, and has the name of Zai.

## p02040 · sig. 220a

†. III. *Of Sarepta, with a brief History of Tyre in the same coast.*

## p02041 · sig. 220a

Sarepta, or after the *Hebrew, Zarephath*, is the next City Southward from Sidon, between it and the River called Near, or *the Fountain of the Gardens of Lebanon* *Fons hortorum Libani*( of which more hereafter) standing in the way towards Tyre, a City very famous for the excellent Wine growing near it: of which Sidonius.

## p02042 · sig. 220a

This City had also a Bishop of the Diocesse of Tyre: after it came to the Saracens and Turks *Turkes*, as the rest: and is now called Safed *Saphet*, says Postellus.

## p02043 · sig. 220a

Not far from Sarepta was situate that sometime famous City of Tyre, whose fleets of ships commanded, and gave the law over all the Mediterranean Sea, and the borders thereof: during which time of greatness and power, the Tyrians erected *Utica* *Vtica**, Leptis*, and Carthage in Affrica, of which VIRGIL. *Urbs* *Vrbs** antiqua fuit Tyrij tenuere* ("The Latins, the city of the Sun") Coloni Carthago. And Carthage was therefore called *Punica quasi Phoenicum*, a Colony of the Phoenicians. In Spain *Spaine* they founded Cadiz *Gades*, now Caliz. In *Italy* *Italie**, Nola*: in Asia the less, Dromos Achillis, which City the Scholiast of Apollonius places near the River Phyllis, in Bithynia.

## p02044 · sig. 220a

It had anciently the name of Tyre, or Tyre; and So it is written in Joshua the 19. taking name from the situation; because built on a high Rock, sharp at one end. The Latins *Latines*, as it seems, knew it by the name of Sarra; for Virgil calls the purple of *Tyre, ostrum Sarranum* ("Tyre, Sarranian purple"), by which name Juuenal *Iuuenal* and Silius remember it. The Sidonians built it upon a high Hill, whereof many ruins remain to this day; the place being still known by the name of the ancient Tyre: and because it was a Colony of the Sidonians, the Prophet Isaiah calls it the Daughter of Sidon; which Trogus also confirms, though Berosus by affinity of name makes Thir as the Son of Japheth *Iaphet* to be the Parent thereof: and though no doubt it was very ancient( for So much the Prophet Isaiah also witnesses, *Is not this your glorious City whose antiquity is of ancient* days: ) yet, that Thir as the son of Japheth set himself in the bosom of the Canaanites who built Sidon, and peopled all that Region: I see nothing to persuade me.

## p02045 · sig. 220b

But that new Tyre in after-times So renowned, seems to be the work of Agenor: and of this opinion was Curtius: and Josephus, and 〈◊ 〉 make this City elder than Salomons Temple 240. years: Cedrenus 361. who also adds that Tyre the Wife of Agenor gave it her name: but of Agenor I will speak more at large in the story of their Kings.

## p02046 · sig. 220b

For strength and for the commodity of the harbour, and the better to receive Trade from all places, it was in this new erection founded in an 〈◊ 〉, 700. paces from the continent: and therefore Ezekiel places it in the midst of the Sea, as some read, or as others in the inner-most part of the Sea, whence he calls it *situate at the entry of the Sea, *as also the same Prophet calls 〈◊ 〉 *the 〈◊ 〉 of the people* *for many Isles* *Iles*: and Isaiah *a Mart of the Nations*: and So Proud *Proude*, Wealthy, and Magnificent was this City, as the Prophet Isaiah calls the Merchants thereof Princes, and their Chapmen the Nobles of the World.

## p02047 · sig. 220b

It excelled both in learning, and in manu-facture: especially in the making and dying of Purple, and Scarlet-cloth: which, says Julius *Iulius* Pollux, was first found out by Hercules *Hercutes* Dog, who passing along the Sea-coast, and eating of the Fish Conchilis or Purpura: the hair of his lips became of that colour. It worshipped the same Idolls that Sidon did: saving that Hercules became their Patron in after-times. For Alexander Macedon, when the Tyrians presented him with a crown of gold, and other gifts, desiring to remain his friends and allies, answered them, that he had vowed a sacrifice to Hercules, the Defender of their City: and the Ancestor of the Macedonians Kings: and must therefore enter it. Whereupon they sent him word, that Hercules his Temple was in the Mountain of old Tyre: where he might perform that ceremony: but this availed not. For Alexander was not So superstitious, as ambitious, he desired to enter the Town, which being denied, he as one whom no peril could fear, nor labour weary, gathered together as many ships as he could; and brought from Lebanon So great a number of Cedars, and So many weighty Stones, from the old City of Tyre adjoining, as not withstanding that his materials were often washed away with the strength of the Sea, and the Tides, yet he never rested, till he had made a foot passage from the Continent to the Island: and having once approached their Walls, he over. topped them with Turrets of wood, and other frames: from whence( having filled the body of force with the violent moving Spirit of resolution) he became Lord thereof, putting all to the sword that resisted, after which he caused 2000 more to be hung up in a rank all along the Sea-shore: which execution upon cold blood he performed( as some Authors affirm) upon the issues of those slaves which had formerly slain all their Masters, taking their Wives *Wiues*, Children, Riches, and power of Government to themselves. This victory of Alexander over the *Tyrians, Josephus *remembers: and how Sanaballat revolted from Darius, and came to Alexander with 8000. Soldiers *Souldiers*: who was the last Satrapa or Provincial 〈◊ 〉, which Darius seated in Samaria: the same who having married his Daughter to 〈◊ 〉, brother to Jaddus *Iaddus* the high Priest of Jerusalem, obtained of Alexander that a Temple might be built on the Mountains *Mountaines* Garizim over Samaria: that the forces of the Jews being divided, Alexander might the better hold them in obedience. The Honour of which Priesthood he bestowed on his son-in-law Manasseh *Manasse*, whom the Jews opposed, for that he had married out of their Tribes, and with a Gentile: but while Alexander besieged *Gaza, Sanaballat*, whom William *Guil*. Tyrius calls Sanballat *Sanabula*, died.

## p02048 · sig. 220b

Long before this desolation of Tyre, by the cruelty of Alexander, it was attempted by Salmanasser the Assyrian King: when the growing pride of the Assyrians, after that they had conquered the ten Tribes, with the rest of Syria, became 〈◊ 〉 of the beauty, riches, and power of that City. He besieged it both on the Landside, and with three-score ships of War held the Port: to the end that neither any victuals nor any supply of men might enter it: but the Tyrians with twelve sail scattered that fleet, and took 500. prisoners of the Assyrians: notwithstanding, the Assyrian continued his resolution and lay before it by his Lieutenants five years, but with ill success. And this siege Menander of Ephesus *Ephesius*, cited by Josephus, made report of in his Chronicles, as he found the Story among the Annalls of the Tyrians( which the said Menander 〈◊ 〉 into Greek *Greeke*) adding that Elulaios *Elulaeus*, whom Tyrius calls Helisaeus, was then King of Tyre, having governed the same six and twenty years. Soon after this repulse of Shalmaneser *Salmanassar*, and about 200. years before the victory of *Alexander, Nebuchadnezzar* *Nabuchodonosor** *at such time as he destroyed Jerusalem with the Temple, came before this City: who indeed gave to Alexander the example of that desperate work, of joining it to the Continent. For Nebuchadnezzar had formerly done it: though by the diligence of the Citizens, and the strength of the Sea, the same causeway and passage was again broken down, and demolished.

## p02049 · sig. 221a

〈◊ 〉 Nebuchadnezzar, for many years, the Tyrians defended themselves: for So long did these Babylonians continue before it, *As every head was made bald, and every* *shoulder made bare*, says Ezekiel, who with the Prophet Isaiah had manifestly foretold the destruction of this proud place. In the end and after thirteen years siege or more, the Tyrians despoiled of all their hopes, and remembering over-late the predictions and threatenings of Gods Prophets, having prepared a convenient number of ships, abandoned their City, transporting with themselves the ablest of all that remained: and with their wives, children, and portable riches sailed thence into *Cyprus, Carthage*, and other Maritime Cities of their Tributaries, or Confederates: So as the Babylonians finding nothing therein, either to satisfy So many labours and perils, or any person upon whom to avenge themselves for the loss of So many bodies in that War: It pleased God in recompense thereof( who strengthened this resolution, as in a work of his own) to make Nebuchadnezzar victorious over the Egyptians: and gave him that Kingdom and the spoil thereof, as it were in wages for his Army. Whereupon Saint Jerome notes, that God leaves not the good deeds of the Heathen unrewarded: who though they cannot hope by any laudable worldly action, to attain unto that eternal happiness reserved for his Servants *Seruants* and Saints: yet such is the boundless goodness of God, as he often repays them with many worldly gifts and temporal blessings.

## p02050 · sig. 221a

Now of this enterprise of Nebuchadnezzar's *Nabuchodonosors* against Tyre, profane Historians have not been silent. For both Diocles, and Philostratus( as Josephus cites them) the one in his second Book, the other in his Phoenician Histories remember it.

## p02051 · sig. 221a

After these two great Devastations *Vastations* by the Kings of Babylon and Macedon: this City of Tyre repaired and recovered it self again: and continued in great glory about 300. years, even to the coming of our Saviour Christ: and after him flourished in the Christian Faith near 600. years: the Archbishop whereof gave place to none but to the Patriarke of Jerusalem only: who within his own Diocesse had fourteen great Cities, with their Bishops and Suffragans: namely 〈◊ 〉, otherwise Porphiria, Acre or *Ptolemais, Sarepta, Sidon, Caesarea Philippi, Berytus, Byblus, Botrys, Tripolis, Orthosia, Archis, Aradus, Antaradus*( or Tortosa) and 〈◊ 〉. But in the year 636. it was with the rest of that beautiful Region of Phoenicia and Palestine *Palaestina*, subjected to the cruel and faithless Saracen. Under the burden and yoke of whose tyranny it suffered with the other Palestine Cities 488. years.

## p02052 · sig. 221a

In the year 1112. it was attempted by Baldwin King of Jerusalem; but in vain: yet in the year 1124. by *Warmund* *Guaremonde**, Patriarke *of *Jerusalem, Vicegerent *to Baldwin the second, with the assistance of the Venetians, and their 〈◊ 〉 of Galleys *Gallies*, it was again recovered, and subjected to the Kings of Jerusalem, and So it remained 165. years.

## p02053 · sig. 221a

Finally, in the year 1189. Saladine having first taken Jerusalem, removed his whole Army and sate down before Tyre: drawing his fleet of ships and Galleys from Alexandria into the port, this City as then only remaining in the Christian power.

## p02054 · sig. 221b

The citizens finding themselves reduced into great famine, and many other miseries, they at once with certain rafters of timber, fired, burnt, and brake the 〈◊ 〉 fleet, and sallying out resolutely upon his army, slew So great numbers of them, and followed their victory with such fury, as that the Saracens forsaking their Trenches and Tents, removed in great disorder and dishonour. Two years after which victory the body of that famous Frederick *Fredericke* Barbarossa( who by the lamentable accident of following the Christians enemies over a River unfordable, perished by the weight of his armor therein) 〈◊ 〉 brought and interred in the Cathedral church of Tyre, near unto that glorious Sepulchre of Origen, garnished and graven with guilt pillars of Marble, 940. years before therein buried: but in the year 1289. the Saracens again attempted it, and carried it, and it now remains subject to the Turks.

## p02055 · sig. 221b

†. IIII. *Of Ptolemais or Acre.*.

## p02056 · sig. 221b

The third City along the coast of the Sea, which the Asserites could not obtain, on the South bound of Asher was Acho, which was the ancient name thereof after Jerome, though other good Authors affirm that it took name from Acre the brother of *Ptolemy* *Ptolomie**. Pliny *calls it Ace: and otherwise the Colony of Claudius. It had also the name of Coth or Cod, and by Ziegler it is called Hactipos.

## p02057 · sig. 221b

But lastly, it was entitled Ptolemais after the name of one of the Egyptian Ptolemies *Ptolomies*: which City also as it is 1. Mac. 11. another of the Ptolemies, faithlessly wrested from his son in law Alexander, which called himself the son of Antiochus Epiphanes: the same Alexander having married Cleopatra daughter of the said Ptolemy not long before. Therein also was Jonathan *Ionathan* Maccabeus *Macchabaeus* treacherously surprised and slain, as it is 1. Mac. 12. 48. by the perfidiousness of Tryphon, whom soon after Antiochus pursued as it is in the Story ensuing: and by like reason about the same time was the aforesaid Alexander in the war against Demetrius one of the sons of Antiochus the great with whom Ptolemy joined, overthrown and treacherously murdered by Zabdiel the Arabian: to whom he fled for succour: and his head presented unto his father in law Ptolemy: who enjoyed not the glory of his victory and treason above three days, for God struck him by death.

## p02058 · sig. 221b

For the beauty and strength of this City, this Alexander made it his regal seat; two parts of the same being environed by the Sea, and the Port for safety and capacity not inferior to any other in all that Tract. This City is distant from Jerusalem some four and thirty miles: four miles to the North from the Mountain Carmel, and as much to the South from Castrum Lamberti: from *Tyre, Antoninus* makes it two and thirty Italian miles. In the midst of the City there was a Tower of great strength sometime the Temple of Bel-zebub: and therefore 〈◊ 〉 the Castle of Flies, on the top whereof there was maintained a perpetual light, like unto that called Pharus in Egypt: to give comfort in the night to those ships, which came near and sought that part. It had in it a Bishops seat, of the Diocesse of Tyre, after it became Christian: but in the year 636.( a fatal year to the Christians in those parts) it was forced and taken by Omar *Haomarus* the Saracen. In the year 1104. it was regained by Baldwin the first, by the help of the Galleys of 〈◊ 〉: to whom a third of the revenue was given in recompense. Again, in the year of our Lord God, one thousand one hundred four-score and seven Saladine King of Egypt, and Syria, became Lord thereof. In the year of Christ, one thousand one hundred ninety and one, by Richard King of England, and Philip King of France it was repossessed and redelivered to the Christians. Lastly, in the year 1291. it was by the fury of the Saracens besieged with an Army of 150000. entered, sacked, and 〈◊ 〉 demolished: though in some sort afterward rebuilt, and it is now Turkish.

†. VI. *Of Acziba, Sandalium, and others.*

Between Ptolemais and Tyre along the Sea coast, was the strong City of Acziba, or Achzib *Achazib*, which S. Belforest* *Belsorrest** *finds Acziba and Sandalium, or the Castle of Alexander to be one, but I know not whence he had it.

## p02063 · sig. 222a

The twelve searchers of the Land which Moses sent from Cadesbarne, travailed as far to the North as Roob, or Rechob, in the Tribe of Asher, which 〈◊ 〉, as also Berothah *Berothae* which by Ezekiel cap. 47. v. 16. is placed in these North borders, belonged in Davids *Dauids* time to the King Hadadezer *Hadarhezer*, as it may be gathered out of the second of Samuel the 8. cap. and 8. verse, and cap. 10. verse. 6. and it defended it self against the Asserites, as *Sidon, Tyre, 〈◊ 〉, Ptolemais, Alab, Helbah, *and Aphek did.

## p02064 · sig. 222a

This Aphek it was, whose Wall falling down, slew seven and twenty thousand of Benhadads Soldiers, after that a hundred thousand had been slaughtered by the Israelites, under the conduct of Ahab. Of which Josh.. 15. and 53. and in the second of Kings 13. 17. he reads, Fortiter, for, in Aphek. Where others convert it, *Percutiens Syrosin Aphek*.

## p02065 · sig. 222b

The next place along the coast is Sandalium, first called Scandalium *Schandalium* of Alexander *Schander*, which we call Alexander, for Alexander Macedon built it, when he besieged Tyre: and set it on a point of Land which extends it self into the Sea, between Acziba and Tyre: which Castle Baldwin the first rebuilt and fortified; in the year of Christ 1157. when he undertook the recovery of Tyre.

## p02066 · sig. 222b

Not much above a mile from this Castle, there arises that most plentiful spring of water, which Solomon remembers, called *the Well of living waters*: from whence not only all the fields and plains about Tyre are made fruitful by large pipes hence drawn: but the same Spring, which has not above a bow-shot of ground to travail till it recover the Sea, drives six great Mills in that short passage, says Brochard.

## p02067 · sig. 222b

Within the Land, and to the East of Acziba, and Sandalium, stands Hosa: and beyond it, under the Mountains of Tyre, the City of Achsaph, or Achshaph *Axab*, or after S. Jerome Achshaph, a City of great strength, whose King amongst the rest was slain by Joshua, at the waters of Merom.

## p02068 · sig. 222b

†. VII. *Of Thoron, Giscala, and some other places.*

## p02069 · sig. 222b

Farther into the Land towards Jordan, was seated the Castle of Thoron, which *Hugo de Sancto 〈◊ 〉 *built on the Easter-most Hills of Tyre, in the year 1107. thereby to restrain the excursions of the Saracens, while they held Tyre against the Christians: the place adjoining being very fruitful, and exceeding pleasant. From this Castle the Lords of Thoron, famous in the Story of the Wars *Warres* for the recovery of the Holy Land, derive their names, and take their Nobility. It had in it a curious Chappell, dedicated to the blessed Virgin, in which *Humphrey of Thoron*, Constable to Baldwin the third, King of Jerusalem, lies buried. There were five Castles besides this within the Territory of Asher: whereof four are seated almost of equal distance from each other: to wit, *Castrum Lamperti, Montfort, Indin* ( or Saron) Castrum Regium, and Belfort: The first near the Sea under the Hills of Saron: the next three, to wit, *Indin, Montfort*, and Regium, stand more within the Land, and belonged to the Brother-hood and Fellowship of the Teutonici, or Dutch Knights( by which they defended themselves, and gave succour to other Christians at such time as the Saracens possessed the best part of the upper Galilee) the chief of which Order was in Ptolemais Acre.. The first 〈◊ 〉 was for beauty and strength called Belfort, seated in the high ground upon the River Naar, near the City Rama: of which in this Tribe Josh.. 19. 29. for which the Vulgar reads Horma: making the article a part of the word, and mistaking the vowels: from the siege of this Castle of Belfort, the great Saladine King of Syria and Egypt, was by the Christians Army raised, and with great loss and dishonour repulsed.

## p02070 · sig. 222b

To the East of Belfort, is the strong City of Alab( or Ahlab *Achlab*) which S. Jerome 〈◊ 〉 Ahlab, one of those that defended themselves against Asher, as Roob( or Rechob) not far thence did.

## p02071 · sig. 222b

Towards the South from Roob they place Gabala( which Herod, surnamed the of Ascalon *Ascalonite* rebuilt) making it of the Territory of *Chabol, Quod 〈◊ 〉 lingua dispicere* ("Cabul, which in the [illegible] language [means] to despise.") significat( says Weissenburg *Weishenburg*) So called, because Hiram of Tyre was ill pleased with those twenty Cities, seated hereabout, which Solomon presented unto him in recompense of those provisions sent him for the building of the Temple. Others think this Chabol or Cabul, containing a circuit of those twenty Cities given to Hiram, to have been without the compass of the holy Land: though bordering Asher on the North side: as it is said, 1. Reg. 9. 11. that they were in Regione limitis: that is, in limit Regionis, in the border of the Country: for it was not lawful, say they, to give to strangers any part of the possessions allotted to the Israelites: howsoever, that after Hiram had refused them, they were peopled by the Israelites, it appears 2. 〈◊ 〉. 8. 14. And it seems they were conquered by David *Dauid* from the Syri Rechobaei, whose City Roob, or Rechob, was in these parts.

## p02072 · sig. 223a

Almost of equal distance from the Castle of Thoron, they place the Cities of Giscala, and Gadara: of which Gadara is rather to be placed over *Jordan: Giscala *was made famous by John *Iohn* the Son of Levi *Leui*, who from a mean estate gathering together four hundred Thieves *Theeues*, greatly troubled all the upper Galilee: at such time as the Romans attempted the conquest of Judea *Iudaea*: by whose practice Josephus, who then commanded in the upper Galilee, was greatly endangered: whereof himself has written at large, in his second Book of those Wars. This John betraying in all he could the City of Giscala( whereof he was native) to the Roman State: and finding a resistance in the City, gave opportunity, during the contention, to the Tyrians and Gadarenes *Gadarims*, to surprise it: who at the same time forced it, and burnt it to the ground: but being by Josephus authority rebuilt, it was afterward rendered to Titus by composition. They find also the Cities of Cana Major, and * Kadesh *Cades*: ( or Kedesh *Cedessa*) of the first was that Syro-Phoenician, whose Daughter Christ delivered of the evil Spirit. Near the other, they say, it was that Jonathas *Ionathas* Machabaeus over-threw the Army of Demetrius.

## p02073 · sig. 223a

There are besides these forenamed Cities within the Tribe of Asher, divers others: as on the South border, and near the Sea, Mishal *Messall* or Mishal: within the Land a Besara, b Bethdagon, and Beth-emek *Bethemec*, standing on the South border between Asher and Zebulun *Zabulon*: on the North side joining to Syro-Phoenicia, is the City of Hethlon *Hethalon*, or Hethlon, the utmost of the holy Land that way: under which towards the Sea is Chali, and then c Enoch supposed to be built by Cain, and named of his Son Enoch, but without probability, as I have formerly proved: there are others also besides these, as Ammon or Chammon, of which Josh.. 19. 28. where also we read of *Neiel* *Nehiel**, Rama, Allammelech* *Alamelec*, and 〈◊ 〉: the Cities of Helkath *Alcath*, or *Helkath, Habdon*, and Rechob, and Mishal, which we have already mentioned, were by the Asserites given to the Levites *Leuites*. Of others held by the 〈◊ 〉, mention is made, Judg. 1. 30. to which out of Joshua we may ad *Ebron, Amhad*, and others, on which no story depends; and therefore I will not pester the description with them.

## p02074 · sig. 223a

†. VIII. *Of the Rivers* *Riuers** and Mountains of Asher.*

## p02075 · sig. 223a

The rivers to the North of Asher, are Adonis, afterward Canis, to which Ziegler joins *Lycus, Ptolemy, Leontis*: both which fall into the Sea near Berytus: which River of *〈◊ 〉, Montanus *draws near unto Sidon: finding his head notwithstanding, where Ptolemy does, between Sidon and Tyre. It has also a River called *the Fountain of the Gardens of Lebanon*, which Adrichome out of Brochard 〈◊ 〉 Eleutherus: for which he also cites Pliny; and the 〈◊ 〉 of Machabees the 11. Chap. but neither of those authorities prove Eleutherus to be in Asher: for this River falls into the Sea at the Isle of Aradus: not far from Balanea *Balanaea*, witness Ptolemy: and therefore Pinetus calls it Balanea, and Postellus Balanea: which River bounds Phoenicia on the North side: to which 〈◊ 〉 also agrees: but this principal River of *Asher, Arias Montanus *calls *Gabatus. Christian* *Christianus** Schrot *out of the mouth and Papers of Peter Laicstan( which Laicstan in this our age both viewed and described the Holy Land) calls the main River *Fons hortorum Libani*: and one of the streams which runs into it from the North side, Naar, and another from the South-west, Chabul: of the City adjoining of the same name: for 〈◊ 〉 it cannot be. There is also another River described by Adrichome, named Jepthael *Iepthael*, which I find in no other Author, and for which he cites the nineteenth of Joshua, but the word d Ghe which is added there to Jepthael, is not taken for a River, but for a Valley: and for a Valley the Vulgar, the Geneva, and Arius Montanus turn it. There is also found in Asher, the River of Belus, remembered by Josephus and Tacitus, which is also called Pagidas, says * Pliny: out of the sands of this River are made the best Glass, which sometime the Sidonians practised: and now the Venetians at *Murana. Arias Montanus *makes Belus to be a branch of Chedumim, which it cannot be: for Belus is known to flow from out the Lake Cendevia *Cendeuia*, as all Cosmographers both Ancient and Modern, and the later Travellers *Trauailers* into those parts witness. It is true that the River of Kishon *Chison* takes water from Chedumim: but not in that fashion which Montanus has described it: neither does it find the Sea at Ptolemais Acre.; according to Montanus: but farther to the South between Caiphas and Sycaminum *Sicaminum*, witness *Ziegler, Adrichem*, and Schrot.

## p02076 · sig. 223b

Besides these Rivers there are divers famous Springs and Fountains *Fountaines*, as that of living waters adjoining to Tyre: and a Misrephoth *Maserephot*, or after S. *Jerome, Misrephoth-maim* *Maserephotmaim*, whose Well filled by the Flood *floud* of the Sea adjoining, ( they say) the Inhabitants by seething the water make salt thereof, as at Nantwich.

## p02077 · sig. 223b

The Mountains which bound Asher on the North, are those of Anti-Lebanon *Anti-libanus*, which with Lebanon bound Coelesyria: two great ledges of Hills, which from the Sea of Phoenicia and Syria, extend themselves far into the Land Eastward: 〈◊ 〉 hundred stadia or furlongs, according to Strabo: for that length he gives to the Valley of Coelesyria: which those Mountains enclose: but Pliny gives them 1500, furlongs in length from the West( where they begin at Theipsophon, or Deifacies, near Tripolis) to the Mountains of Arabia beyond Damascus: where Anti-Lebanon turns towards the South. These ledges where they begin to part Trachonitis *Traconitis* and Bashan *Basan*, from the Desert Arabia, are called Hermon: which Moses also names Sion, the Phoenicians Sirion *Syrion*, and the Amorites Sanir, neither is this any one Mountain apart; but a continuation of Hills: which running farther Southerly, is in the Scriptures called Gilead *Galaad* or Gilead: the same being still a part of Lebanon, as the Prophet Jeremiah proves: *Gilead tu mihi caput Libani*: noting that this Gilead is the highest of those Hills *Hils* of *Lebanon. Strabo *knows them by the name of Traconitae: and Ptolemy by *Hippus. Arias Montanus *calls these Mountains bordering *Asher, Lebanon*, for Antilibanus, contrary to all other Cosmographers, but he gives no reason of his opinion.

## p02078 · sig. 223b

They take the name of Lebanon from their white tops, because according to Tacitus, the highest of them are covered with Snow all the Summer, the Hebrew word Libanon( says Weissenburg) signifies whiteness. Others call them by that name of the Frankincense which those trees yield: because 〈◊ 〉 is also the Greek word for that Gum.

## p02079 · sig. 223b

Niger out of Aphrodiseus affirms, that on Lebanon, there falls a kind of honey dew, which is by the Sun congealed into hard sugar, which the Inhabitants call Sacchar, from whence came the Latine word Saccarum.

## p02080 · sig. 223b

The Rivers which Lebanon bestows on the neighbour Regions are, *Chrysorrhoas, Jordan, Elentherus, Leontes, Lycus, Adonis, the Fountain of the Gardens* *Fons** of Lebanon, *and others.

## p02081 · sig. 223b

The rest of the Mountains of Asher, are those Hills above Tyre, and the Hills of Saron, both exceeding fruitful: but those are but of a low stature, compared with Lebanon: for from Nebo, or the Mountain of Abarim, in *Reuben, Moses *beheld Lebanon three-score miles distant.
