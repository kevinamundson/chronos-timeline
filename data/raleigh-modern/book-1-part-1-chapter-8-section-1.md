§. I. Whether SHEM and HAM were elder then JAPHETH *IAPHET*.

## p01209 · sig. 111b

OF these Sons of Noah, which was the eldest, there is a question made. S. Augustine esteemed Shem for the eldest, Ham for the second, and Japheth for the youngest: and herein the opinions of Writers are divers. But this we find everywhere in the Scriptures, and especially in Moses, that there was never any respect given to the eldest in years, but in virtue, as by the examples of *Enoch* *Henoch**, Abraham, Jacob* *Iacob*, and David *Dauid*, is made manifest. In a few words, this is the ground of the controversy; The Latine translation, and So the Geneva, has converted this Scripture of Genesis the 10. v. 21. in these words: Unto SHEM *also the Father of all the Sons of *Eber *HEBER*, *and elder brother of *JAPHETH, *were children borne*. But Junius *Iunius* agreeing with the Septuagint, places the same words in this manner: To SHEM *also the Father of all the Sons of *Eber, *and brother of *JAPHETH, *the eldest son were children borne: *So the transposition of the word( elder) made this difference. For if the word( elder) had followed after Japheth, as it is in the vulgar translation placed before it, then had it been as plain for Japheth, as it is by these translations for Shem. Now( the matter being otherwise indifferent) seeing Gods blessings are not tied to first and last in 〈◊ 〉, but to the eldest in piety, yet the arguments are stronger for Japheth then for Shem. And where the Scriptures are plainly understood without any danger or inconvenience, it seems strange why any man of judgement should make valuation of conjectural arguments, or men's opinions. For it appears that Noah in the five hundredth year of his life, begat the first of his three Sons, *Shem, Ham*, and Japheth: and in the six hundredth year( to wit) the hundredth year following, came the general Flood *floud*; two years after which Shem begat Arphaxad, which was in the year 602. of Noahs life, and in the year of Shems life one hundred: So as Shem was but 100. years old, two years after the Flood: and Noah begat his first borne being 500. years old; and therefore, were Shem the elder, he had then been a hundred years old at the Flood, and in the six hundredth year of Noahs life, and not two years after. Which seeing the Scripture before remembered has denied him, and that it is also written: Then NOAH *awoke from his wine, and knew what his younger* *yonger** son had* *done unto him( to wit) *HAM; of necessity the first place does belong to Japheth. This younger son So converted by the vulgar and *Geneva, Junius *turns it *filius minimus; His youngest son*; but S. Chrysostom *Chrysostome* takes it otherwise, and finds Ham *Cham* to be the middle or second brother, and Japheth the youngest son of all: which Ham for his disobedience and the contempt of his Father( whose nakedness he derided) was disinherited, and lost the pre-eminence of his birth, as Esau and Reuben did. Pereira *Pererius* conceives that Ham was called the younger in respect of Shem the eldest, but avows withal, that the Hebrew has not that precise difference of younger and youngest, because it wants the comparative degree. It is true that Shem himself was always named in the first place yet whereas in the first Verse of the tenth Chapter of *Genesis, Shem *is accounted before Japheth: in the second Verse Moses leaves to begin with the issue by Shem, and recites the children of Japheth first. So the first place was given to Shem for his election and benediction, and for this weighty respect, that the Hebrew Nation, Abraham, the *Prophets, David*, and Christ our Saviour were descended of him. And therefore, whether we shall follow the Vulgar Pagninus, and the Geneva, who agree in this conversion, SHEM frater JAPHETH major; or with the *Septuagint, Junius*, and Tremellius *Tremelius*, SHEM fratri JAPHETH maioris; or with Pereira, SHEM frater JAPHETH ill magnus: inferring that Shem was the great and famous brother of Japheth, let the Reader judge. But for ought that I have seen to the contrary, it appears to me that Japheth was the eldest. For where Pereira qualifies the strength of the former Argument, That Shems age at the time of the Flood did not agree with his eldership( with a supposition that the Scriptures took no account of smaller numbers) I do not find in the Scriptures any such neglect at all: for it is written, that SHEM *was an hundred years old, and begat *ARPHAXAD *two years after the Flood*; and again in the 12. Verse: So SHELA *lived after he begat *Eber, *four hundred and three years, & c. *So as the number of two years, of three years, of five years, and afterward of two years were always precisely accounted.
