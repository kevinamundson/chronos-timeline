§. III. That Nimrod, not ASSHUR *ASSVR*, *built Nineveh* *Niniue**: and that it is probable out of *Isaiah *ESAY* 23. 13. that ASSHUR *built Ur of the Chaldees*.

## p01430 · sig. 143a

NOW as of Nimrod: So are the opinions of Writers different touching Asshur, and touching the beginning of that great state of Babylon and Assyria: a controversy wearisomely disputed without any direct proof, conclusion, or certainty. But to me( of whom, where the Scriptures are silent, the voice of Reason has the best hearing) the interpretation of Junius *Iunius* is most agreeable; who besides all necessary consequence does not disjoin the sense of the Scriptures therein, nor confuse the understanding thereof. For in this sort he converts the Hebrew Text: *Erat enim principium regni eius Babel, & Erech, & Akkad* *Accad**, & Chalneh, in terra Shinar* *Shinaar** is; e terra hac 〈◊ 〉 in Assyriam ubi aedificavit Niniven* *Niniuen* ("For the beginning of his kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Accad, and Calneh, in the land of Shinar; from this land [illegible] went out into Assyria, where he built Nineveh"); ( which is) *For the beginning of this Kingdom was Babel, and Erech, and Akkad, and Calneh* *Chalneb**, in the land of 〈◊ 〉: and he went forth of this land into Assyria, and built Nineveh. *So as Junius takes Asshur in this place, not for any person, but for the Region of Assyria: the land being So called in Moses time, and before it. For certainly, the other construction, ( where the word( Asshur) is taken for Asshur the son of Shem *Sem*) does not answer the order which Moses observes through all the Books *Bookes* of Genesis, but is quite contrary unto it. For in the beginning of the tenth Chapter he sets down the sons of Noah, in these words: *Now these are the Generations of the sons of *NOAH: Shem, HAM, and JAPHETH *IAPHETH*, *unto whom sons were borne after the Flood* *floud**: *then it follows immediately: *The sons of *JAPHETH were GOMER, & c. So as Japheth is last named among Noahs sons, be he eldest or youngest: because he was first to be spoken of: with whom( having last named him) he proceeds and sets down his issue, and then the issue of his sons: first, the issue of *Gomer, Japheths* *Iapheths** *eldest son; and then speaks of 〈◊ 〉 and his sons: for of the rest of that Family he is silent. Anon after he numbers the sons of Ham, of which Cush was the eldest: and then the sons of Cush and Mizraim; and afterward of Canaan; leaving Shem for the last, because he would not disjoin the Story of the Hebrews *Hebrewes*. But after he begins with Shem, he continues from thence by *Arphaxad, Shela*, and Eber *Heber*, unto Abraham, and So to Jacob *Iacob*, and the Fathers of that Nation. But to have brought in one of the sons of Shem in the middle of the generations of Ham, had been against order; neither would Moses have past over So slightly the erection of the Assyrian Empire, in one of the sons of Shem, if he had had any such meaning: it being the story of Shems sons which he most attended. For he names Nimrod apart, after the rest of the sons of Cush, because he founded the Babylonian and Assyrian Empire: and in the eleventh Chapter he returns to speak of the building of Babel in particular, having formerly named it in the tenth Chapter, with those other Cities which Nimrod founded in Shinar. And as he did in the tenth Chapter, So also in the eleventh he makes no report of Shem, till such time as he had finished So much of Nimrod as he meant to touch: and then he begins with the issue of Shem, which he continues to Abraham and Israel. And of Junius opinion touching Asshur, was Calvin *Caluin*: to which I conceive that *P. Comestor, in historia Scholastica, *gave an entrance, who after he had delivered this place in some other sense, he uses these words: *Vel intelligendum non est de* ("Nor is it to be understood concerning [Nimrod];")* *ASSHUR filio Shem, &c. sed ASSHUR *( id est) Regnum Assyriorum inde egressum est, quod tempore* ("(that is) The kingdom of the Assyrians went out from there, which at the time [of Nimrod began];")* *SERUG *SARVG* proavi ABRAHAMI factum est, ( which is) *Or else it is not to be understood of *ASSHUR *the son of *Shem, &c. but ASSHUR *( that is, the Kingdom of the Assyrians) came from thence( videlicet, from Babylon) or was made out of it: which happened in the* time of SERUG *the great grand-father of *ABRAHAM. After which he reconciles the differences in this sort: If you take the ancient Belus( meaning Nimrod) to be the first Erector of the Assyrian Empire, or the first Founder thereof, it is true, *Quantum ad initium; Respecting the beginning*; but others conceive that it had beginning from Ninus, which is also true, *Quantum ad regni ampliationem; Regarding the enlargement of the Empire. *To this I may ad the opinion of Epiphanius, confirmed by Cedrenus, who takes Asshur to be the son of Nimrod: and So does Methodius, and 〈◊ 〉, S. Jerome *Hierome*, and Cyril *Cyrillus*, and now lastly Torniellus: who says he took upon him that name of Asshur after he had beaten the Assyrians, as Scipio did of Africanus, after his conquest in Africa: and that Asshur was a common name to the Kings of Assyria, as it appears by many Scriptures, as Psalm 81. Isaiah 10. Ose 5. &c. but to help the matter, he makes Nimrod of the race of Shem, and the son of Irari. But Rabanus Maurus, who was Arch-Bishop of Mainz *Mentz* in the year of Christ 854. an ancient and learned Writer, understands this place with Commestor, or Commestor with him, agreeing in substance with that translation of Junius: to which words of Moses he gives this sense: *De hac terra Assyriorum 〈◊ 〉 imperium, qui ex nomine* ("Concerning this land of the Assyrians [illegible] the empire, which [took its name] from [Assur];")* *NINI, BELI *filij, Ninum condiderunt, urbem magnam, &c. Out of this land grew the Empire of the Assyrians, who built Ninus the great City, So named of *NINUS *NINVS* *the son of *BELUS *BELVS*. On the contrary Calvin objects this place of Isaiah: *Behold the land of* *the Chaldeans* *Chaldaeans**, this was no people, *ASSHUR *founded it by the inhabitants of the Wilderness*; then which there is no one place in the Scriptures, that has a greater diversity in the translation and understanding; insomuch as *Michael de Palatio *upon Isaiah( though in all else very diligent) passes it over. But Calvin seems hereby to infer, that because Asshur founded the state of the Chaldeans, therefore also Asshur rather than Nimrod established the Assyrian Empire, and built Nineveh: contrary to the former translation of Junius, and to his own opinion. Now out of the Vulgar( called Jerome's *Hieromes* translation) it may be gathered that Asshur both founded and ruined this Estate or City of the Chaldoeans, by Isaiah remembered: unto which City, People, or State, he plainly tells the Tyrians that they cannot trust, or hope for relief thence. Or rather it may be taken, that the Prophet makes this City of Chaldea *Chaldaea*, and that Estate, an example unto those Phoenicians, whom in this place he foretells of their ruin: which City of Chaldea being of strength, and carefully defended, was( notwithstanding) by the Assyrians utterly wasted and destroyed: whereby he gives them knowledge, and foretells them, that their own City of Tyre, ( invincible, as themselves thought) should also soon after be over-turned by the same Assyrians: as( indeed) it was by Nebuchadnezzar *Nabuchodonosor*. And these be the words after Jerome: *Ecce terra Chaldoeorum, talis populus non fuit* ("Behold the land of the Chaldeans, such a people was not [there],")*, *〈◊ 〉 *fundavit eam, in captivitatem traduxerunt robustos eius, suffoderunt domos eius, posuerunt eam in ruinam* ("he founded it, they carried its strong men into captivity, they undermined its houses, they put it in ruin,")*, *( which is) *Behold the Land of the Chaldoeans, such a people there were not*( or, *this was no people*, after the Geneva) ASSHUR( or the *Assyrians) founded it, they carried away their strong men captive, they undermined their houses, and ruined their City. *The Septuagint express it but in a part of another Verse, in these words: *Et in terra Chaldoeorum, & hoec desolata est ab Assyrijs, quoniam murus eius corruit* ("And in the land of the Chaldeans, and this was desolated by the Assyrians, because its wall fell,")*, *making the sense perfect by the preceding Verse, which all together may be thus understood: *If thou go over to Chittim* ( which is Macedon or *Greece) yet thou shalt have no rest*( speaking to the *Tyrians) neither in the Land of the Chaldoeans, for this is made desolate by the Assyrians, because their walls fell together to the ground. *PAGNINUS *PAGNINVS* and VATABLUS *VATABLVS* convert it thus: *Ecce terra Chasdijm, iste populus non erat illic olim; nam* ("Behold the land of the Chaldeans, this people was not there formerly; for [the Assyrian]")* *ASSHUR *sundavit eam navibus, erexerunt arcs illius; contriverunt oedes eius, posuit eam in ruinam* ("he founded it with ships, they raised its towers; they broke down its houses, he put it in ruin:")*: *which may be thus Englished: *Behold the Land of the Chaldoeans, this people was not once therein inhabiting: for *ASSHUR *built it a harbour for ships, they erected the Towers thereof, and again brake down the houses thereof, and ruined it. *IVNIVS in the place of ships sets the word *( pro Barbaris* ("(for barbarians")) that is, *for the Barbarians*: and the *Geneva, by the Barbarians*. But this is undoubted that the Prophet Isaiah( as may be gathered by all the sense of the Chapter) did therein assure the Tyrians of their future destruction, which( accordingly) fell on them: wherein( for the more terror) he makes choice to note the calamities of those Places, Cities, and Regions, by whose Trade the state and greatness of the Tyrians was maintained; as by the Cilicians from Tarshish *Tharsis*; from the 〈◊ 〉, and other Groecians under the name of Cittim; also by the Egyptians *Aegyptians*, the Chaldoeans, and the rest. For Tyre was then the Mart Town of the World most renowned. And( as it appears in our discourse of Paradise) not the least part of her chief merchandise came in by the City Ur or Urchoa *Vrchoa* in Chaldea, where the body or chief stream of Euphrates( even that stream which runs through Babylon and Otris, which now falls into Tigris) had his passage into the Persian 〈◊ 〉: though now it be stopped up. For( as we have heretofore noted) the Arabians( that descended from Sheba and Raamah) dwelling on the East banks of the Persian Gulf, trading with the Tyrians( as those of *Eden, Haran* *Charran*, and Calneh did) transported their merchandise by the mouth of Tigris, that is, from Teredon and of Euphrates, that is, from Ur or Urchoa: and then by Babylon, and thence by River and over Land they conveyed it into Syria, and So to Tyre: as they do this day to Aleppo. So then Ur of the Chaldees was a Port Town, and one of those Cities which had Intelligence, Trade, and Exchange with the Tyrians: for it stood by the great Lakes of Chaldea, through which that part of Euphrates ran, which passage is now stopped up. *〈◊ 〉 cursum vetustas abolevit* ("[illegible] antiquity has abolished the course [of the river];")( says Niger. ) And Pliny *PLINIE*, *Locus ubi Euphratis ostium fuit, flumen salsum* ("The place where the mouth of the Euphrates was, [is now] a salt river"); *Time has worn away the channel of Euphrates: and the place where the mouth there of was, is a Bay of salt water. *These things being thus, certainly( not without good probability) we may expound the City of the Chaldees, whose calamities Isaiah here notes for terror of the Tyrians, to be the City anciently called Ur; and( by *Hecataeus* *Hecatoeus**) Camerina*; by *Ptolemy* *Ptolemie**, Urchoa*: and by the *Greeks* *Greekes**, Chaldoeopolis, The City of Chaldea: *which the sons of Shem, until Abrahams time, inhabited. And whereas in all the Translations it is said, that Asshur both founded it and ruined it: it may be understood, that Asshur the Founder was the son of Shem; and Asshur the destroyers were the Assyrians, by whom those that inhabited Ur of Chaldea, were at length oppressed and brought to ruin: which thing God fore-seeing commanded Abraham thence to Haran, and So into Canaan. And if the Hebrew word by Vatablus and Pagninus converted( by ships) do bear that sense, the same may be the better approved; because it was a Port Town: and the River So far up as this City of Ur was in ancient time navigable, as both by Pliny and Niger appears. And if the word *( for the Barbarians*) or *( by the Barbarians*) be also in the Hebrew Text, it is no less manifest, that the most barbarous Arabians of the Desert were and are the confronting, and next people of all other unto it. For Chaldea is now called Arachaldar, which signifies desert Lands, because it joins to that part of Arabia So called: and Cicero( calling those Arabians by the name of Ituraeans) adds, that they are of all other people the most salvage; calling them, *Homines omnium maxime barbaros* ("Men, the most barbarous of all.").

So as this place of 〈◊ 〉, which breeds some doubt in Calvin, proves in nothing the contrary opinion, nor in any part weakens the former translation of 〈◊ 〉, nor the interpretation of Comestor and Rabanus. For though other men have not conceived( for any thing that I have read) that Asshur is in this place diversely taken( as for the son of Shem, when he is spoken of as a Builder of Ur; and when as a Destroyer thereof, then for the Assyrian Nation) yet certainly the evidence of the truth, and agreement of circumstances seem to enforce it. And So this Founding of the City of the Chaldees by Asshur( into which the most of the posterity of Shem that came into Shinar, and were separate for the Idolatry of the Cushites *Chusites* and Nimrodians, retired themselves) has nothing in it to prove that the same Asshur built Nineveh, or that the same Asshur was all one with Ninus; except we will make Asshur, who was the son of Shem, both an Idolater, and the son of Belus. For( out of doubt) Ninus was the first 〈◊ 〉 Sacrificer to Idols; and the first that set up a Statue to be honored as god. Now if Asshur must be of that Race, and not of the Family of Shem, as he must be if he founded Nineveh, then all those which seek to give him the honor thereof, do him by a thousand parts more injury, by taking from him his true Parent and Religion.

Besides, if this supposed Asshur whom they make the Founder of Nineveh( and So the son of Belus) were any other, and not the same with Ninus; then what became of him? Certainly he was very unworthy and obscure, and not like to be the Founder of such an Empire and such a City, if no man have vouchsafed to leave to posterity his expulsion thence, and how he lost that Empire again or quitted it to Ninus: whose acts and conquests are So largely written, and( according to my apprehension) far differing from truth. It will therefore be found best agreeing to Scripture and to Reason, and best agreeing with the story of that age written by profane Authors, that Nimrod founded *Babel, Erech*, and Akkad, and Calneh, the first works and beginnings of his Empire, according to Moses, and that these works being finished within the Valley of Shinar, he looked farther abroad, and set in hand the work of Ninus, lying near unto the same stream that Babel and Calneh did: which work his grand-child Ninus afterward amplified and finished, as Semiramis( this Ninus his wife) did Babylon. Hence it came to passe, that as Semiramis was counted the Foundress of the City which she only finished: So also Ninus of *Ninine: Quam quidem Babylonem potuit instaurare; She might repair or renew Babylon, *says S. Augustine. For So did Nebuchadnezzar vaunt himself to be the Founder of Babylon also, because he built up again some part of the wall, over-borne by the fury of the River: which work of his stood till Alexanders time, whereupon he vaunted thus: Is *not this great Babel which I have built?*
