§. XIIII. *Of the River Gihon* *Gehon** and the Land of Cush: and of the ill translating of the Ethiopia* *Aethiopia** for Cush, *2. CHRON. 21. 16.

Now, as Havilah *Hauilah* in the East India drew Pishon *Pison* So far out of his way thither, So I say did Cush( being by the Seventy translated Ethiopia) force Gihon into Africa. For Cush being taken for Ethiopia by the Greeks *Greekes*, whom the Latins *Latines* followed, Gihon consequently was esteemed for Nile *Nilus*. But Ethiopians *Aethiopians* are, as much, as *black or burnt faces*, whose proper Country is called Thebaids *Thebaides*, lying to the Southward of all Egypt *Aegypt*. And although there be many other Regions of Ethiopians, and far South in Africa, yet those of Thebaids are those So often remembered in the Egyptian *Aegyptian* stories, and out of which Nation they had many times their Kings of Egypt: all which 〈◊ 〉 are very near, or else directly under the Equinoctial line, which is very far from that land inhabited by the Cushites *Chusites*; who are neither black of colour, nor in any sort neighbouring Torrida Zona. But this Translation of the *〈◊ 〉, 〈◊ 〉 *does qualify in this manner: There are( says he) two 〈◊ 〉, the East, and the West: and this division he finds in Strabo, out of Homer. Now because there is no colour to make Cush *Chush* Ethiopia in *Africa, Pereira* *Pererius** *will make Cush and the Land of the Cushites ( which is Arabia Petraea, and a part of 〈◊ 〉 the Happy, with the Region of Midian *Madian*) to be the East Ethiopia.

Now if it be granted, that Cush and the Land of the Cushites, be that Tract from Sur to Havilah, according to the Scriptures: Habitavit *Habitauit* ISMAEL *ab Havilah unto Sur*, *quae respicit Aegyptum oeuntibus Assyrios* ("which faces Egypt for those going to the Assyrians"); ISMAEL *dwelt from Havilah unto Sur, that is towards Egypt, as thou go toward Assyria*; The same suffices to prove that Gihon cannot be Nile, but a River which waters Cush, and not Ethiopia. But this place of Scripture, Habitavit ISMAEL, & c. has this sense: Ismael dwelt from 〈◊ 〉, which is the way of Assyria, or the Country bordering Assyria; and Sur, which lies toward Egypt, which is as much to say, as, The issues of Ismael( whereof there were twelve Princes) whom God had promised to make a great people, inhabited all those Regions between the border of Egypt and Assyria. And that they were( according to the word of God) So increased and multiplied, it well appeared, when Zearah the Chusite, which others call Tharantha, brought an Army of ten hundred thousand against Asa King of Judah *Iuda*. Which Army came not out of Ethiopia beyond Egypt; for that had been a strange progress for such a multitude, as ten hundred thousand, having So mighty a King as the King of Egypt, between 〈◊ 〉 and Ethiopia. But these were the *Cushites, Amalechites, Midianites* *Madianites**, 〈◊ 〉, *and Arabians. For it is written, that after Asa( strengthened by God) had defeated this World of an Army, he in following his victory took some of the Cities of King Zearah round about, as Gerar. Now that Gerar is a City of the Ethiopians, it cannot be suspected: for these be the words of the Scripture disproving it: And ABRAHAM *departed thence toward the South Country, and dwelt between Kadesh* *Cadesh** and Sur, and sojourned in Gerar: *Now Sur is that part, upon which Moses and the Israelites first set their feet after they passed the Red Sea, where the Amalechites in Rephidim set on them, supposing that they had been weary, and unable to resist. Again, in the story of Isaac it is written: Wherefore ISAAC went to ABIMELECH, *and the 〈◊ 〉 unto* Gerar: and I am sure Abimelech and the Philistines *Philistims* were no Ethiopians. And lastly, Moses himself, where he describes the bounds of Canaan, has these words: Then *the border of the Canaanites was from Sydon, as thou come to Gerar: *for Sydon was the Frontier of Canaan towards the North, and Gerar by Gaza *Gazah* towards the South. But indeed, howsoever Pereira does with an honest excuse salve his Translation of Cush for Ethiopia, yet it appears plainly, that the Septuagint and Josephus *Iosephus* did altogether misunderstand this place. And first, for Homers East and West Ethiopia, they are both found elsewhere. For Pliny *Plinie* in his fifth *fift* Book and eighth Chapter, cites Homer for an Author, of these two Ethiopia's *Aethiopia's*. But the East Ethiopia is that which compasses Nile to the South of Egypt, and is the South border thereof; now a part of the Empire of the 〈◊ 〉, under Prester John *Iohn*; And the West Ethiopia is that, which joins it self with the River Niger, which we call Senegal *Senega* and Gambia *Gambra*: for thereabouts are these Ethiopians called *Perorsi, Daratites*, with divers other names, which Pliny numbers. But all these are in Africa, and beyond the Deserts *Desarts* thereof, says Pliny out of *Homer, Agrippa*, and Juba; which Regions indeed( I mean that of Niger, and that of Prester John, and the Troglodytes) lye due East and West. But as for Cush and the Region of the *Ishmaelites* *Ismaelites**, & c*. they are extended directly North from that Ethiopia, which is beyond Egypt. Now, that Josephus was exceeding gross herein, it appears by that fiction, which he has of Moses when he served Pharaoh *Pharao*, in the wars against the Ethiopians: for in that( to make *Cush, Ethiopia*) he transports Midian by miracle over the Red Sea, and beyond all Egypt, and sets it in Ethiopia, as shall be showed more at large in the Chapter of the Worlds Plantation. Again, that Gihon was improperly translated *〈◊ 〉, Pereira *confesses, and lays it rather to the corruption of the Greek *Greeke* Copy, than otherwise. And whereas the Septuagint have converted this place of the Prophet Jeremiah *HIEREMIE*; *And what hast thou now to do in the way of Egypt, to drink the water of Nile?* *Quidtibi vis in via Aegypti, ut bibas aquam Gihon? *to this says PERERIVS, profecto *Hebraic ibi non est* ("is not")* vox Gihon, sed Sichor, quae significat nigrum & turbidum; Truly*( says PERERIVS) *the word Gihon in this place, is not found in the Hebrew, but Sichor, which signifies black and troubled water.*

Furthermore, this is a manifest and unanswerable argument, that Cush was ill taken for *Ethiopia. Moses *married the Daughter of Jethro, Prince and Priest of Midian, whom both the Greek and Latine call a Madianite, and not Aethiopissam, as( with 〈◊ 〉. ) The Geneva converts it, though it help it a little with a marginal note. Now it is without dispute, that Zipporah was of the Country of Midian, which is that part of Arabia Petraea, bordering the Red Sea; for it is written in the second of Exodus, that MOSES fled from Pharaoh *into the Land of Midian, and sate down by a* *Well, & c*. and again, in the third of *Exodus; when *MOSES *kept the sheep of *JETHRO *his father in law, Priest of Midian, &c. *Indeed, these four nations are 〈◊ 〉 where mixed in the Scriptures, because they dwell confusedly together( to wit) the Midianites, the Ishmaelites, the Amalekites, and the Cushites, which were all in one general word, Arabians, and in the Scriptures sometimes called by one of those names, and sometimes by another, as in Gen. 37. v. 25. 27. & 28. that Joseph *Ioseph* was sold to the Ishmaelites; and in the same Chapter, v. 36. it is written, that the Midianites sold Joseph to *〈◊ 〉, Pharaoh's* *Pharao's** *Steward. The Genevans *Geneuians*, in a marginal note( to avoid this confounding of the Nations) say, that Moses wrote according to their opinion, who took the Midianites and Ishmaelites to be all one. But Moses wrote not after any mans opinion, he wrote the truth, and these were all Arabians, and So in this very place it appears by their merchandise, which they brought with them, when they bought Joseph: for their Camels were laden with Spicery and Balm, and Myrrh, which are the Trades of Arabia Felix *foelix*: from whence chiefly, and from the East India, all the World is served with Myrrh and Frankincense; and their Spices they received from the East side of the Arabian Gulf, as aforesaid. And in the 39. Chapter it is said: That Putiphar bought Joseph of the Ishmaelites, which the Chaldean *Chaldaean* Paraphrast in the same place calls Arabians. Now, to make this the more manifest, it is written in the sixth *sixt* of 〈◊ 〉, That when Israel had sown, then came up the Midianites, and the Amalekites, and they of the East, and came upon them: they of the East, were Arabians of the Desert; So as where before in the buying of Joseph, the Midianites and the Ishmaelites were confused, here the Midianites and Amalekites are made one Nation. For in the prosecution of the Story of Gideon *Gedeon*, the Midianites only are named, as comprehending both Nations; and in the eight Chapter, v. 24. these Nations are all called Ishmaelites, and neither Midianites nor Amalekites. As when Gideon desired, that every man would give him the golden ear-rings, which they had taken after the victory against Zebah and Zalmunna, Kings of Arabia, amounting to 1700. shekels of gold, it is written: *For they had golden ear-rings, because they were Ishmaelites. *And these Ishmaelites were a great and valiant Nation, and ever in action of war. *Manus eius contr 〈◊ 〉,* *& manus omnium contra eum; His hand*( says God of ISMAEL) *shall be against all men, and every mans hand against him. *Of these Ishmaelites came the Muslim *Mahometan* Arabians, though some Writers think Mahomet to be of the Schenitae. And these Ishmaelites, which inhabit chiefly in Cedar, and the Deserts of Sur and Paran *Pharan*( says Josephus) use poison upon their Arrows *Arrowes*, as the Indians do. Towards the South-east are the Midianites, and Cushites: and beyond them, towards the Deserts of Arabia, the Amalekites; and all are one Nation, and all Arabians.

Lastly, the ill translation of Ethiopia for Cush, is among other places, made most apparent, in the second of Chronicles, in these words: *So the Lord stirred up against* Jehoram *IEHORAM*, *the spirit of the Philistines, and the Arabians, which confine the Ethiopians*; So Jerome *Hierome* reads it: the Geneva translation has it, *which were besides the 〈◊ 〉 *Now, how far it is off between the Philistines, and the Negro's, or the 〈◊ 〉 every man that looks in a Map may judge. For the Philistines and Arabians do mix and join with the Land of the Cushites, and are distant from Ethiopia about two and thirty, or three and thirty degrees, and therefore not their next Neighbours; but all Egypt, and the Deserts of Sur and Paran, are between them. So as this place of the second of Chronicles, should have been translated in these words: *So the Lord stirred up against *Jehoram, *the spirits of the Philistines, and the Arabians, which confine and border upon the Cushites, *who indeed are their next Neighbours. * *Nulla superest dubitatio quin Ethiopia in Sacris literis sit Arabiae propinqua; There remains no doubt*( says Steuchius *STEVCHIVS*) *but Ethiopia in the Scriptures, is taken for that Country, which joins to Arabia.*

Now may we think is it probable, or possible, that Moses could be ignorant of Nile? No, he knew it, no living man So well, and therefore would never have named Gihon, for Nile, or Nile for Gihon. Surely, if Moses had meant Nile, when he named Gihon, he would have called the River( into which he was cast upon Reeds, and preserved by God, working compassion in the Daughter of Pharaoh) a River of Egypt, wherein he was borne and bred, and wrought So many Miracles. Besides, the River of Nile is often named in the Scriptures, but never by the name of Gihon. And if Moses had told the Israelites, that Nile had been a River of Paradise, they might justly have thought, that he had derided them: for they had lived there all days of their lives, and found no such Paradise at all, nor any memory, or speech thereof; except we shall believe the Paradise of Hesperides, where( says Pliny) there was nothing found in his time, but wild Olives *Oliues* in stead of golden Apples. But Nile is twice called Sichor, once in Isaiah *Esay*, and once in the Prophet Jeremiah; and yet in those places it is not said to be a River of Ethiopia, but of Egypt. For in a word, the Israelites had never any communion or affairs with the Ethiopians, nor any intelligence, or trade, beyond Egypt, to the South; but the Enemies which they had on the South, and East-parts, were these Nations of the *Cushites, Philistines, Ishmaelites, Amalekites, *and Midianites: who being often governed by many little Kings, or Reguli, were distinguished in names, according to the Fathers and Heads of those Nations; but in one general name were all Arabians. On the North-side of Canaan, they were afflicted with the Coelesyrians, with the *Magogians, Tubalines*, and others their adherents; and thirdly within themselves, the Nations, which remained of the ancient 〈◊ 〉, held the strongest Cities upon the Sea-coast( as) *Tyre, Sidon, Acre* *Acon**. Gaza, *and many others: yea, Jerusalem *Hierusalem* itself was with-held from Israel( from the days of Moses, even unto the time of David *Dauid*) by the Jebusites *Iebusites*.

That which now remains of most difficulty is, that it does not appear, that any part of Gihon waters that part of Arabia the stony, which the Cushites inhabited in the times of the Kings of Israel: and in this Desert it was that Matt. Beroaldo *Beroaldus* lost himself in seeking out Paradise: for he was driven( to my understanding) to create two Rivers *Riuers*, and call them Gihon, and Pishon; to the end that the one might water Cush, and the other Havilah, for I find none such in rerum natura ("in the nature of things"), as he has described: by which Rivers he also includes within Paradise, even Arabia the *Arabiathe* Desert.

And as he well proved that Pishon was not Ganges, nor *Gihon, Nile*: So where to find them else-where it seems he knew not. Certainly this River of Gihon, which he makes to fall into the Mediterranean *Mediterran* at Gaza, and whose Springs he finds far East in Arabia, is but imaginary: for the Current by Gaza is but a small stream, rising between it and the Red Sea, whose head from Gaza itself is little more than twenty English miles, as shall appear hereafter. But undoubtedly, hence it comes that many were mistaken. They all considered of the habitations of the Cushites, as they were planted when the state of Israel stood, and when it flourished, being then their near Neighbours, and never looked back to the first seats and plantation of CUSH. For after the Flood *Floud*, CUSH and his children never rested, till they found the Valley of Shinar, in which, and near which himself with his sons first inhabited. HAVILAH took the River-side *Riuer-side* of Tigris chiefly on the East, which after his own name he called HAVILAH, ( now *Susiana: ) Raamah*, and Sheba farther down the River, in the entrance of Arabia Felix. Nimrod seated himself in the best of the Valley, where he built Babel, whereof that Region had afterwards the name of Babylonia. CUSH himself and his brother MIZRAIM first kept upon Gihon, which falls into the Lakes of Chaldea *Chaldaea*, and in process of time, and as their people increased, they drew themselves more Westerly towards the Red or Arabian Sea: from whence MIZRAIM past over into Egypt, in which Tract the Cushites remained for many years after. Now because there could be no such River found in Arabia the stony, which they might entitle Gihon, they translated Cush Ethiopia, and *〈◊ 〉, Nile*. And if we do examine this mistaking by example, we shall the better perceive it as it was. For let us suppose, that BRUTE *BRVTE*, or whosoever else that first peopled this Island, had arrived upon the River of Thames, and calling the Island after his name BRITANNIA, it might be said that Thames or Tems was a river, that watered Britannia: and when afterwards in process of time, the same BRUTE had also discovered and conquered Scotland, which he also entitled by the same name of Britannia, later ages might conclude that Scotland was no part thereof, because the river of Tems is not found therein. Or let us suppose that Europa *EVROPA*, the daughter of the King of Tyre in Phoenicia, gave the name to Europe, according to Herodotus, and that the first discoverers thereof arrived in the mouth of some river in Thrace, which then watered as much of Europe, as he first discovered, shall we in like sort resolve, that *France, Spain* *Spaine*, and *Italy* *Italie**, & c*. are no parts of Europe, because that River is not found in them, or any of them? In like manner was it said by Moses in his description of Gihon, that it watered the whole land of Cush; but not the whole land which the Cushites should or might in future time conquer, people, and inhabit, seeing in after ages they became Lords of many Nations, and they might( perchance) have been Masters in time, ( as the Saracens which came of them were) of a great part of the World. For( though the Babylonian Empire, which took beginning in Nimrod the son of CUSH, consisted at the first but of four Cities, ( to wit) *Babel, Erech, Acad*, and Calneh *Chalne*, yet we find, that his Successors *Successours* within a few years after commanded all the whole World in effect: and the fame of Babel consumed the memory of Chusea. For of this *Tower of Confusion *did all that land take the name of Babylonia: and the greatness of that Empire founded by Nimrod a younger *yonger* son, obscured the name and nation of his father CUSH in those parts, until they crept farther off, and in places not yet entitled, and farther from the Babylonian Empire, where the Cushites retained their names, which also they fastened to the Soil and Territory by themselves afterwards inhabited and held. And we may not think, that CUSH or any of his could in haste creep through those desert Regions, which the length of 130. years after the Flood had( as it were) fortified with thickets, and permitted every bush and briar, reed and tree to join themselves( as it were) into one main body and Forrest. For if we look with judgement and reason into the worlds plantation, we shall find, that every family seated themselves as near together as possible they could; and though necessity enforced them, after they grew full of people, to spread themselves, and creep out of Shinar or Babylonia, yet did they it with this advice, as that they might at all times resort, and succour one another by river, the fields being then( without all doubt) impassable. So Nimrod, who out of wit and strength usurped dominion over the rest, sate down in the very confluence of all those rivers, which watered Paradise: for thither it was to which the greatest troupes of NOAHS children repaired; and from the same place whence Mankind had his beginning, from thence had they again their increase. The first Father of men Adam, had therein his former habitation. The second Father of Mankind NOAH, began from thence his dispersion.

Now as Nimrod the youngest, yet strongest, made his choice of Babel( as aforesaid) which both Tigris and Euphrates cleansed and enriched; So did HAVILAH place himself upon Piso-Tigris: RAAMAH and his son SHEBA farther down upon the same River, on the Sea-coast of Arabia: CUSH himself upon Gihon, the fairest branch of Euphrates. And when they began to spread themselves farther off, yet they always fastened themselves to the Rivers sides: for *Nineveh* *Niniue**, Haran* *Charran**, Reseph, Calneh, Ur *in Chaldea, and the other first-peopled Cities were all founded upon these Navigable Rivers, or their branches, by which the one might give succour and assistance to the other, as is already often remembered.
