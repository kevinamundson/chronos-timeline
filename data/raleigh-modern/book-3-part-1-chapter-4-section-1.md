# Book III, Part I, Chapter IV, §. 1

## p03523 · sig. 402a

§. I. *Of the number and names of the Persian Kings.*

## p03524 · sig. 402a

OF the successors of Cyrus, and the continuance of the Persian Empire, there are many opinions; As that of Metasthenes, who has numbered the Persian Kings and their times, as follows.

## p03525 · sig. 402a

Darius Medus, and Cyrus jointly 2 years. Cyrus alone. 22 years. Priscus Artaxerxes. 20 years. Darius Longimanus. 37 years. Darius Nothus. 19 years. Artaxerxes Mnemon. 55 years. Artaxerxes *Ataxerxes* Ochus. 26 years. Arses, or Arsames. 4 years. Darius the last, conquered by Alexander. 6 years.

## p03526 · sig. 402a

To which Philo agrees; which number of years added, make in all an hundred ninety and one. But in this Catalogue Metasthenes has left out Cambyses and Xerxes, and names Artaxerxes Ahasuerus *Assuerus* for the immediate successor of Cyrus; in place( says Melancthon) of Darius the son of Hystaspes: for Metasthenes, as Melancthon conjectures, does not account Cambyses in the Catalogue, because his reign was confounded with that of Cyrus.

## p03527 · sig. 402a

There is a second opinion, though ridiculous, of Seder Olam, who finds but four Persian Kings from the beginning to the end of that Empire.

## p03528 · sig. 402a

*Genebrard, Schubert*, and Beroaldo *Beroaldus* have also a differing account from the Greeks *Greekes*; whom nevertheless Eusebius and most of the Latins *Latines* follow, and So does Krentzheim, who has fully answered, and as I take it, refuted all the former Authors varying from that account: For in this sort do the Greeks marshal the Persian Kings with the times of their reigns.

## p03529 · sig. 402a

Cyrus in all. 30 years. Cambyses, with the Magi. 8 years. Darius Hystaspes. 36 years. Xerxes 21 years. Artaxerxes Longimanus. 40 years. Darius Nothus. 19 years. Artaxerxes Mnemon. 43 years. Artaxerxes Ochus. 23 years. Arsames. 3 years. Darius the last. 6 years.

## p03530 · sig. 402a

Which numbers, put together, make in all two hundred and thirty.

## p03531 · sig. 402a

This account( as I have said) the most Chronologers and the best learned approve. These Persian Princes being all warranted by the authority of the Scriptures, as Peucer in his historical Animadversions *Animaduersions* has gathered the places; finding first Cyrus in the second of *Chronicles, chap. *36. verse. 22, 23. Ezra 1. chap. 1. verse. 1. and often elsewhere.

## p03532 · sig. 402b

Secondly Cambyses in the 11th of Daniel, who may indeed be well esteemed for one of those three Kings in the second verse named, and So the marginal Commentor upon the Geneva understands that place; but, under correction, mistakes the matter greatly, when he says in the same note, that Darius 〈◊ 〉 was an enemy to the people of God, and stood against them: his great favour and liberality to the Jews *Iewes* being elsewhere proved.

## p03533 · sig. 402b

Thirdly, is Darius Hystaspes found in Ezra the first, c. 4. v. 5 who in the sixth *sixt* verse is also named Ahassuerus.

## p03534 · sig. 402b

Fourthly, in the eleventh of Daniel verse the second, Xerxes is plainly foretold and described, and the great war which he should make against the Greeks by Daniel remembered.

## p03535 · sig. 402b

〈◊ 〉, Artaxerxes Longimanus in Ezra the fourth, verse seven, who is also called *Artaxerxes, c*. 4. 1. *lib. Ezra v. *7. and cap. 7. v. 7.

## p03536 · sig. 402b

Sixthly, *Darius Nothus, Ezra cap*. 4. verse. 24. and cap. 5. verse. 6. *〈◊ 〉. cap*. 12. verse. 22.

## p03537 · sig. 402b

Seventhly, Artaxerxes Mnemon in *Nehe. c*. 2. v. 1. who was father to Artaxerxes Ochus, and Arsames: for Darius the last he was of another Family, the Line of Cyrus the Great ending in Ochus, who descended from Xerxes the son of *Atossa, Cyrus *his Daughter; and the issue male of Cyrus failing with his own Sons.

## p03538 · sig. 402b

But to proceed. Eusebius with the Latins, following the Greeks, apply the beginnings and ends of every Persian King with their Acts, to some certain Olympiad; As the war of Astyages( Cyrus his maternal Grand-father) and Alyattes( Croesus his father) to the nine and fortieth Olympiad; The beginning of Cyrus reign to the beginning of the five and fiftieth Olympiad; The taking of Sardis by Cyrus to the eight and fiftieth Olympiad; The invasion of Egypt *Aegypt* by Cambyses to the third year of the threescore and third Olympiad, and So of the rest. Which reference with good agreement between several forms of computation ad the more credit unto both.

## p03539 · sig. 402b

Again, this historical demonstration is confirmed by the Astronomical computation of Ptolemy *Ptolomie*, who refers the death of Alexander the Great, who died the 12. of November, in the beginning of the hundred and fortieth Olympiad, to the four hundred and four & twentieth year after Nabonassar. And the Aera of Nabonassar began on the six and twentieth of February: which conferred with the Olympiad, was in the ninth Month of the first year of the eighth Olympiad; So that whether we follow the account of the Olympiads, as do the Greek *Greeke* Historians, or that of Nabonassar with Ptolemy, we shall find every memorable accident to fall out right with each computation.

## p03540 · sig. 402b

〈◊ 〉 Ptolemy reckons the time answerable to two hundred and four and 〈◊ 〉 Julian *Iulian* years, and an hundred and forty days from Nabonassar, to the sixteenth of July in the seventh *seuenth* year of Cambyses.

## p03541 · sig. 402b

The Greeks, and namely Diodorus Siculus, place the taking of Egypt by Cambyses in the second or third year of the threescore and third Olympiad, and the beginning of Cambyses seventh year in the first of the threescore and fourth Olympiad: which first of the threescore and fourth Olympiad runs along with part of the two and twentieth of Nabonassar. The like agreement is consequently found about the beginning and end of Cyrus.

## p03542 · sig. 402b

Likewise the twentieth of Darius, who succeeded Cambyses, is according to Ptolemy the two hundred and six and fortieth of Nabonassar, which( observing the 〈◊ 〉 of Nabonassars Aera and the Olympiad, viz. eight and twenty years) it agrees with the third of the threescore and ninth Olympiad, where in it is placed by the Greeks. In this Josephus *Iosephus* agrees with the Greeks throughout, saving that he joins Darius Medus, whom Xenophon calls Cyaxares, with Cyrus, in the destruction of Babylon, which is true, and not contrary to the Greek computation, but may very well stand with it.

## p03543 · sig. 403a

Lastly, the disagreements and confused accounts of those that follow the other Catalogue of the Persian Kings formerly rehearsed, does give the greater credit to this of the Greeks, which being constant in it self, accords also with the computation of other Historians, and Astronomers, and likewise with the holy Scriptures.
