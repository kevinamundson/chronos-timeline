# Book I, Part I, Chapter I, §. 2

> Sir Walter Raleigh, *The History of the World* (1617). Modern edition. Original spelling: EarlyPrint A10357 (EEBO-TCP), London 1617, Stansby for Walter Burre. The original text is untouched; this file is the stored modern copy.

## p00804 · sig. 44a

§. II. *That the wisest of the Heathen, whose authority is not to be despised, have acknowledged the World to have been created by GOD.*

## p00805 · sig. 44a

This work and creation of the World, did most of the ancient & learned Philosophers acknowledge, though by divers terms, and in a different manner expressed, I mean all those who are entitled by S. *Augustine, Summi* Philosophi, Philosophers of highest judgement and understanding. Mercury *Mercurius* Trismegistus calls God, Principium universorum, The original of the universal: to whom he gives also the attributes of *Mens, natura, actus, necessitas, finis, & renovatio* ("Mind, nature, action, necessity, end, and renewal.")*. *And wherein he truly, with S. Paul, casts upon God all power; confessing also, that the World was made by Gods almighty word, and not by hands: *verbo, non manibus fabricatus est mundus* ("the world was made by a word, not by hands.")*. *ZOROASTER( whom Heraclitus followed in opinion) took the word Fire, to express God by( as in Deuteronomy, and in S. Paul it is used) *Omnia ex uno igne genita sunt; All things*( says be) are *caused, or produced out of one fire.*

## p00806 · sig. 44b

So did Orpheus plainly teach, that the World had beginning in time, from the will of the most High God; whose remarkable words are thus converted: Cum abscondisset omnia JUPITER *IVPITER* *summus, 〈◊ 〉 in lumen gratum emisit, ex sacro cord operans cogitata & mirabilia* ("the highest [illegible] sent [it] forth into the welcome light, working thoughts and marvels from the sacred heart")*: *Of which I conceive this sense; When great JUPITER *had hidden all things in himself; working out of the love of his sacred heart, he sent thence, *or brought forth *into grateful light, the admirable works which he had fore-thought.*

## p00807 · sig. 44b

Pindarus the Poet, and one of the wisest, acknowledged also one God, the most High, to be the Father and Creator of all things; *Unus* *Vnus** Deus Pater Creator summus. Plato* ("One God, the Father, the supreme Creator. — Plato")* *calls God the cause and original, the nature and reason of the 〈◊ 〉; *totius rerum natura, causa, & origo Deus* ("God [is] the cause and origin of the whole nature of things.")*. *But hereof more at large hereafter.

## p00808 · sig. 44b

Now, although the curiosity of some men have found it superfluous, to remember the opinions of Philosophers, in matters of Divinity: ( it being true, that the Scripture has not want of any foreign testimony) yet as the Fathers, with others excellently learned, are my examples herein; So S. Paul himself did not despise, but thought it lawful, & profitable, to remember whatsoever he found agreeable to the word of God, among the Heathen, that he might thereby take from them all escape, by way of ignorance, God rendering vengeance to them that know him not: as in his Epistle to Titus, he cites Epimenides against the Cretians, and to the *Corinthians, Menander*, and in the seventeenth of the *Acts, Aratus *&amp;c. for Truth( says S. Ambrose) by whomsoever uttered, is of the holy Ghost; *Veritas a quocunque dicatur, a Spirita sancto est* ("Truth, by whomsoever it is spoken, is from the Holy Spirit.")*: *and lastly, let those kind of men learn this rule; *Quae sacris 〈◊ 〉, prophana non sunt* ("they are not")*; Nothing is profane that serves to the use of holy things.*
